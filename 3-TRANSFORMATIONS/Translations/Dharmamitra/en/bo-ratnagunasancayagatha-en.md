# Verses on the Collection of the Precious Qualities of the Perfection of Wisdom — DharmaMitra zero-shot (english) ^0

The Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom. ^0-1

## Introductory meaning ^0-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^0-2]]

In the language of India: Ārya-prajñāpāramitā-sañcayagāthā. In the language of Tibet: 'Phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa sdud pa tshigs su bcad pa. Homage to the Noble Mañjuśrī! ^0-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^0-3]]

Then, in order to cause those four assemblies to be perfectly and thoroughly delighted, the Blessed One taught this perfection of wisdom once more, and at that time spoke these verses: ^0-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^0-4]]

Establishing the highest joy, respect, and faith,
Having cleared away the obscurations of the afflictions and transcended all stains,
Listen to this perfection of wisdom of the noble ones, who are dedicated to the welfare of beings,
And to that which is the conduct of the heroic ones. ^0-4

## Teaching of omniscience ^1-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-1]]

The first chapter teaches omniscience of all aspects.
Whatever rivers flow here in Jambudvīpa,
Producing flowers, fruits, medicinal plants, and forests,
All depend upon the Naga King, the Lord of Nagas, who dwells in Anavatapta;
They are the power and glory of that Lord of Nagas. ^1-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-2]]

Whatever Dharma the Victor’s Śrāvakas may teach,
Explain, or express through logical reasoning,
And whatever attainment of supreme, noble bliss and its fruits they achieve,
All of these are the power of the Tathāgata, the Great Being. ^1-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-3]]

If you ask why, it is because whatever system of Dharma the Victor has taught,
Those supreme beings, having become his disciples, have thoroughly trained in it,
And having directly realized it, they teach the training exactly as it is;
This is accomplished through the power of the Buddha, and not through their own individual strength. ^1-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-4]]

One who does not apprehend the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Does not apprehend the bodhisattva, and does not apprehend the mind of awakening;
Upon hearing this, one who is not bewildered and has no fear,
That bodhisattva practices the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^1-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-5]]

There is no form, no feeling, no perception, and no intention;
Within those, there is not even an atom's worth of a station for consciousness.
They do not dwell on any phenomena, but practice without a dwelling place;
Without grasping, they attain the awakening of the Sugatas. ^1-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-6]]

Just as a wandering mendicant, through his knowledge of craftsmanship,
Brings about the disintegration of the aggregates without any objective apprehension,
Any bodhisattva who understands phenomena in that way,
Does not touch nirvana, and is established in wisdom. ^1-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-7]]

Furthermore, thinking, "What is this wisdom? Whose is it?
From where does it come?" they realize all these phenomena are empty;
Having closely examined them, they are without discouragement or fear;
Such a bodhisattva is close to awakening. ^1-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-8]]

If, while lacking such knowledge, one perceives form,
And engages with the aggregates of feeling, volition, and consciousness,
Even if one conceptualizes that "these aggregates are empty," that bodhisattva
Is still practicing with signs and lacks faith in the state of non-arising. ^1-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-9]]

One who does not practice in form, feeling, perception, or intention,
And does not practice in consciousness, practices without a fixed abode.
Not apprehending the thought "I am practicing," such a one relies on wisdom;
The unborn and intelligent touch the supreme peace of meditative stabilization. ^1-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-10]]

Any bodhisattva who abides here in peace in this way,
Has been prophesied by the Tathāgatas of the past.
They have no conceit of thinking, "I am in meditative equipoise," or "I have arisen,"
Why is that? Because the nature of phenomena is fully understood. ^1-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-11]]

Practicing in such a way, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas,
Because they thoroughly understand that non-practice is the practice.
They do not apprehend even the phenomena that are practiced;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-12]]

That which does not exist is called "nonexistent."
Foolish beings, having conceptualized it, treat it as either existing or non-existing.
Existence and non-existence—these two are phenomena that do not exist.
Any bodhisattva who understands this will definitely emerge. ^1-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-13]]

One who knows the five aggregates to be like an illusion,
And does not treat the illusion as one thing and the aggregates as another,
Free from diverse perceptions and practicing in total peace—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-14]]

Those who possess spiritual friends and possess special insight,
Will never become afraid upon hearing the Mother of the Victors.
Those who associate with negative friends and rely on others,
Are like an unfired clay vessel that disintegrates when touched by water. ^1-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-15]]

If one asks, "Why is this person called a 'bodhisattva'?"
They seek the total exhaustion of all attachment, cutting through attachment.
They touch the unattached awakening of the Victors;
Therefore, they receive the name "bodhisattva." ^1-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-16]]

If one asks, "Why is this one called a 'great being' (mahāsattva)?"
Because they have become the foremost among the vast assembly of sentient beings,
And because they sever the great thicket of views within the realm of sentient beings;
For that reason, they are truly called a "great being." ^1-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-17]]

Possessing great generosity, great intelligence, and great power,
They have entered the supreme Great Vehicle of the Victors;
Clad in great armor, they subdue the illusions of Māra;
For that reason, they are truly called "great beings." ^1-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-18]]

For example, just as a magician might create an illusion at a crossroads,
And then decapitate tens of millions of people in that great crowd—
Just as those who are being killed are, so too is the bodhisattva;
One who fully understands that all wanderers are like emanations has no fear. ^1-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-19]]

Form, perception, feeling, intention,
And consciousness are neither bound nor freed, and they do not exist.
Thus, entering into awakening without a discouraged mind,
This is the supreme armor of holy beings. ^1-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-20]]

If one asks, "Why is this called the Great Vehicle of Awakening?"
It is because by mounting it, one delivers all sentient beings from suffering.
This vehicle is a vast celestial palace, as immense as space;
It is the supreme vehicle, the means for fully attaining joy, delight, and bliss. ^1-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-21]]

One who rides cannot perceive any direction toward which they go;
Though it is said they go to nirvāṇa, no going is perceived.
For example, just as when a fire is extinguished there is no going anywhere,
For that reason, it is perfectly described as "nirvāṇa." ^1-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-22]]

The bodhisattva does not apprehend a prior limit, a later limit,
Or a present; they are the purity of the three times.
That which is such is unconditioned and free from conceptual elaboration;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-23]]

When wise and learned bodhisattvas, contemplating non-arising,
Practice in this way,
Though they generate great compassion, they have no perception of sentient beings;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-24]]

If one gives rise to the perception of sentient beings or the perception of suffering,
Thinking, "I shall act for the benefit of beings and eliminate their suffering,"
One is a bodhisattva who conceptualizes a self and sentient beings;
This is not the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-25]]

Know that all sentient beings are exactly as oneself is;
Know that all phenomena are exactly as all sentient beings are;
Not conceptualizing either non-arising or arising—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-26]]

Having abandoned both the arising and the complete passing away of all things,
As many as are expressed by names throughout the worlds,
One attains that supreme, deathless primordial wisdom, beyond which there is no other;
For that reason, this is called the perfection of wisdom. ^1-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-27]]

Any bodhisattva who practices in that way without doubt,
Should be known as one endowed with wisdom, abiding in equanimity;
Having fully understood that all phenomena are devoid of intrinsic nature,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-28]]

The second chapter, teaching the knowledge of all paths, is concluded. ^1-28

## Teaching of the knowledge of all paths ^2-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-1]]

One who does not dwell in form, does not dwell in feeling,
One who does not dwell in perception, does not dwell in volition,
And one who does not dwell in consciousness, dwells in the nature of reality;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-2]]

Permanent and impermanent, happiness and suffering, pleasant and unpleasant,
Self and selflessness, suchness and likewise emptiness;
One does not dwell in the attainment of the fruit or the stage of an arhat,
Nor does one dwell in the stage of a pratyekabuddha or likewise the stage of a buddha. ^2-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-3]]

Just as the Guide does not dwell in the unconditioned realm,
Likewise, one does not dwell in the conditioned, but practices without a fixed location;
In that way, the bodhisattva dwells by not dwelling in any basis;
To dwell without a location is what the Victor has taught as "dwelling." ^2-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-4]]

Those who think, "May I become a disciple of the Sugata,"
And those who desire to become Pratyekabuddhas or likewise Kings of Dharma,
Cannot achieve these without relying on this patience,
Just as one cannot cross from this shore to the other without seeing the ferry. ^2-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-5]]

The teaching of the Dharma, the listening, and whatever is taught,
The attainment of the result, the Pratyekabuddhas, and likewise the Protectors of the World,
And whatever nirvāṇa is attained by the clarity of the wise—
The Tathāgata has taught that all these are like an illusion. ^2-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-6]]

These four types of persons are not afraid of that:
The Child of the Victors skilled in the truths, the irreversible one,
The Arhat who has cleared away stains and abandoned doubt,
And fourth, the one who is fully embraced by a spiritual friend. ^2-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-7]]

The wise and learned Bodhisattva who practices in this way,
Does not train in the stage of an Arhat, nor in the stage of a Pratyekabuddha;
For the sake of omniscience, they train following the Buddha-dharma.
One who does not train in the distinction between training and non-training is one who trains. ^2-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-8]]

It is not for the sake of the increase, decrease, or grasping of form;
Nor is it for the sake of grasping the various phenomena that one trains.
While training, one also grasps omniscience itself;
This very transcendence is the training of those who delight in excellence. ^2-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-9]]

Form is not wisdom, and wisdom is not found within form.
Consciousness, perception, feeling, and intention—
These are not wisdom, nor is wisdom present within them.
This is identical to the element of space, without any distinction. ^2-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-10]]

The nature of all objective referents is boundless;
Whatever the nature of sentient beings is, that too is boundless.
The nature of the expanse of space is also boundless;
The wisdom of the Knower of the World is also boundless. ^2-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-11]]

The Guide has proclaimed that perception is the near shore;
By dismantling and abandoning perception, one goes to the far shore.
Those who are free from perception have attained this subsequent state;
Having crossed over, they abide by the Teacher’s command. ^2-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-12]]

Even if the Teacher were to remain for as many eons
As there are grains of sand in the Ganges and proclaim the word "sentient being,"
Since they are pure from the beginning, how could a sentient being ever be born?
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-13]]

When I spoke in accordance with this supreme perfection,
At that time, the Supreme Being of the past prophesied,
Saying, "In a future time, you will become a Buddha,"
Thus the Conqueror spoke, saying that I had been prophesied. ^2-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-14]]

Whoever, as a protector, practices this perfection of wisdom,
And whoever holds and masters it with devotion,
Will not be harmed by poison, weapons, fire, or water;
Mara and the factions of Mara will find no opportunity to harm them. ^2-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-15]]

Suppose someone were to make offerings to the stūpas
Of the Sugatas who have passed into parinirvāṇa, constructing them of the seven precious gems,
And were to fill a thousand million buddha-fields
With as many stūpas of the Sugatas as there are grains of sand in the Ganges; ^2-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-16]]

If in infinite tens of millions of realms,
All the sentient beings who dwell therein,
Were to do nothing else but offer divine flowers, the finest incense,
And ointments for three eons or even longer, ^2-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-17]]

Yet someone were to write down in a book this Mother of the Sugatas,
From whom the Ten Powers of the Guides arise,
And, bearing it, were to honor it with flowers and ointments,
The merit of having worshiped stūpas would not approach even a fraction of that. ^2-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-18]]

This Perfection of Wisdom of the Victors is a great mantra of knowledge;
It pacifies the misery and suffering of the many realms of sentient beings.
Those protectors of the worlds in the ten directions, past and present,
Who train in this mantra of knowledge will become unsurpassed kings of physicians. ^2-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-19]]

Those who engage in the practice of benefit and compassion,
Having trained in this vidyā-mantra, the wise shall touch enlightenment;
One should know that all forms of happiness,
Whether conditioned or unconditioned, arise from this. ^2-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-20]]

Just as seeds that are sown and remain in the earth will eventually grow,
Once the assembly of conditions is found, diverse forms are fully produced;
The five perfections and however many qualities of awakening there are,
All of them are born from the perfection of wisdom. ^2-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-21]]

On whichever path a universal monarch constantly travels,
On that very path, the seven precious jewels and all the military hosts follow;
Wherever this Perfection of Wisdom of the Victors goes,
From that very place, all the qualities and dharmas will follow. ^2-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-22]]

The Victor, having been asked a question by Śakra, replied:
If there were Buddha-fields as numerous as the sands of the Ganges,
And all of them were filled to the brim with the relics of the Victors,
I would still take this very Perfection of Wisdom. ^2-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-23]]

Why is that? It is not that I lack respect for the bodily relics;
However, because they are completely suffused with wisdom, they are worthy of worship.
For example, just as a person who relies on a king receives honor,
So too the relics of the Buddha rely upon the Perfection of Wisdom. ^2-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-24]]

A precious jewel, endowed with all qualities and of inestimable value,
Is worthy of veneration even when placed within a casket.
Even after it has been removed, one still treats the casket with joy;
Yet those qualities belong to the precious jewel itself. ^2-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-25]]

Likewise, through the qualities of the supreme perfection of wisdom,
The Victors receive worship of their relics even after they have passed into nirvana.
Therefore, those who wish to grasp the qualities of the Victors
Should take up this perfection of wisdom; it is liberation. ^2-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-26]]

Wisdom is the precursor to the practice of generosity,
And likewise for ethical discipline, patience, diligence, and meditative concentration.
It is the total sustainer that prevents virtuous qualities from being lost;
This demonstrates the single nature of all phenomena. ^2-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-27]]

For example, although there are billions of trees in the world of Jambudvīpa,
In many various forms, with diverse colors and diverse shapes,
Aside from speaking of them in terms of the number of things that cast a shadow,
The shadows themselves are not diverse, nor is there any distinction between them. ^2-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-28]]

In the same way, these five perfections of the Victors
Also acquire the very name of the perfection of wisdom.
When they are fully dedicated for the sake of omniscience,
All these six become of one taste, known as "awakening." ^2-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-29]]

If a bodhisattva, without deep understanding, regards
Form, perception, feeling, intention, and consciousness
As impermanent, they are practicing a counterfeit;
The wise never cause the destruction of phenomena. ^2-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-30]]

In which form is not apprehended, nor feeling, nor perception,
Nor consciousness, nor intention is apprehended,
And all phenomena are thoroughly known to be unborn and empty—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-31]]

If someone were to take as many sentient beings as there are grains of sand in the Ganges,
And, having disciplined them all, establish them in the state of an arhat,
Compared to one who writes down this perfection of wisdom,
And gives the text to a supreme being, the merit of the latter is far more sublime. ^2-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-32]]

Why is that? Because those supreme speakers, having trained in this,
Here demonstrate that all phenomena are emptiness.
Having heard that, the Hearers quickly reach liberation,
And they reach the awakening of Pratyekabuddhas and the awakening of Buddhas. ^2-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-33]]

If no sprout exists, a tree could never grow in the world;
In that case, how could branches, flowers, and fruit ever appear?
If the mind of enlightenment did not exist, no Victors would ever arise in the world;
And if so, how could the fruits—Indra, Brahmā, and the Hearers—ever appear? ^2-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-34]]

Just as when the solar orb radiates its web of light,
Sentient beings then apply themselves diligently to their tasks,
So too, when the mind of enlightenment arises in the world for the sake of the wisdom of the sages,
Through primordial wisdom, sentient beings come to possess the qualities of the Dharma. ^2-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-35]]

For example, if the Lord of Nagas were not present in Lake Anavatapta,
How could the rivers ever flow here in Jambudvīpa?
If there were no rivers, flowers and fruits would not grow,
And the great ocean, too, would lack its many precious forms. ^2-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-36]]

Likewise, in this case as well, without the mind of enlightenment,
How could the primordial wisdom of the Sugatas ever arise throughout all these worlds?
Without primordial wisdom, there is no increase in qualities and no enlightenment;
Even the ocean-like qualities of a Buddha would also not exist. ^2-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-37]]

Whatever sentient beings there are in this world that provide light,
Compared to the light emitted by all of them to illuminate the world,
The emergence of a single ray from the sun’s orb is far superior;
The light from the host of luminaries cannot match even a fraction of it. ^2-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-38]]

However much the hosts of śrāvakas generate a mass of merit
Through being endowed with generosity, ethical discipline, and meditation,
It cannot match even a fraction of a bodhisattva’s single thought of rejoicing;
The mass of merit of the śrāvaka hosts cannot match even a fraction of that. ^2-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-39]]

Whatever Buddhas of the past, numbering tens of millions of billions,
Who reside in infinitely many billions of fields, and those who have passed into parinirvāṇa,
Those protectors of the world,
Teach the precious Dharma for the sake of completely ending suffering. ^2-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-40]]

From the time they first generate the aspiration for supreme enlightenment,
Until the time the true Dharma of those guides is exhausted,
Whatever merit those Victors possess during that interval,
Including the perfections and all the Buddha-qualities, ^2-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-41]]

And whatever virtues—both contaminated and uncontaminated—
Of the Buddha’s sons, the śrāvakas, those in training, and those beyond training,
The bodhisattva gathers together and rejoices in,
And dedicates it all toward enlightenment for the sake of the welfare of beings. ^2-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-42]]

If, while dedicating, the notion of a mind arises,
Or the notion of enlightenment, or the notion of sentient beings,
Then because of such notions, one dwells in views and the mind is attached to the three;
Because there is an object of observation, it is not a perfect dedication. ^2-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-43]]

If one understands that these phenomena have ceased and are exhausted,
And that which is dedicated is also exhausted,
And that a phenomenon never dedicates to a phenomenon,
Then by knowing in such a way, it is a perfect dedication. ^2-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-44]]

If one conceives of it in terms of signs, that is not a dedication.
If there are no signs, that is the dedication to enlightenment.
Just as eating fine food that has been mixed with poison,
The Victor has taught that focusing on virtuous qualities is the same. ^2-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-45]]

Therefore, one should train in dedication in this way:
Whatever the nature, the source, and the characteristics of that virtue
Are as known by those Victors,
One should rejoice in it and dedicate it in exactly that same way. ^2-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-46]]

If one dedicates merit to enlightenment in this way,
One is "poison-free," does not forsake the Buddha, and speaks what the Victors have taught.
The hero who dedicates in this way outshines
All those bodhisattvas in the world who engage in conceptual apprehension. ^2-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-47]]

How could billions of blind people, lacking a guide,
Ever know the path or enter the city?
Without wisdom, these five perfections are sightless;
Lacking a guide, they are unable to reach enlightenment. ^2-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-48]]

When they are fully embraced by wisdom,
They then find their sight and receive this name.
For example, just as a painting that is complete except for the eyes,
As long as the eyes are not drawn, the payment is not received. ^2-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-49]]

When conditioned and unconditioned phenomena, as well as white and black dharmas,
Have been thoroughly deconstructed by wisdom so that not even an atom is observed,
That is accounted as the perfection of wisdom in the world;
It is just like space, which does not abide in anything at all. ^2-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-50]]

If one thinks, "I shall practice the wisdom of the Victors,
And I shall liberate many billions of beings afflicted by suffering,"
A bodhisattva who conceptualizes the notion of sentient beings,
This is not the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-51]]

Any bodhisattva who, while practicing in the past,
Practiced this perfection skillfully and without doubt,
Will, upon hearing this, immediately perceive it as the Teacher,
And will quickly realize the peace of awakening. ^2-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-52]]

Even if one has honored billions of Buddhas in former lives,
If one lacks faith in the Victors' perfection of wisdom,
That person of small intelligence, upon hearing it, will reject it;
Having rejected it, they become defenseless and go to the Unrelenting Hell. ^2-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-53]]

Therefore, if you wish to attain the supreme primordial wisdom of a Buddha,
Have faith in this Mother of the Victors.
For example, it would be unfitting to be like a merchant who,
Having traveled to an island of jewels, returns home with his goods exhausted. ^2-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-54]]

One should understand that the purity of form is the purity of the fruition;
The purity of the fruition and the purity of form become the purity of omniscience.
The purity of omniscience, the purity of the fruition, and the purity of form
Are like the element of space—indivisible and inseparable. ^2-54

## Teaching of the knowledge of all bases ^3-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-1]]

The third chapter teaches the knowledge of all bases.
Through the perfection of wisdom, the heroic practitioners
Truly transcend the three realms, yet they do not enter a narrow liberation.
Although they have eliminated the afflictions, they manifest birth;
Although they are free from aging, sickness, and death, they manifest passing away. ^3-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-2]]

These beings are stuck in the mire of name and form;
They wander in the wheel of cyclic existence, which is like a wheel driven by wind.
Knowing that deluded beings are like deer caught in a snare,
Those endowed with wisdom move through the sky like birds. ^3-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-3]]

When one whose conduct is completely pure does not engage with form,
And does not engage with consciousness, perception, feeling, or intention,
Practicing in that way, they completely abandon all attachments,
And, liberated from attachment, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^3-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-4]]

The wise and luminous bodhisattva who practices in this way,
Having severed attachment, moves through the world without clinging,
Like the sun shining brightly when freed from the eclipse of Rāhu,
Or like a fire that blazes, consuming grass, wood, and forests. ^3-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-5]]

When a bodhisattva, through the perfection of wisdom, sees
That all phenomena are naturally pure and utterly pristine,
They do not perceive an agent, nor do they perceive any phenomena;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^3-5

## Teaching of the complete realization of all aspects ^4-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-1]]

The fourth chapter explains the complete realization of all aspects.
Śakra, the king of the gods, asked the Victor:
"How do Bodhisattvas practicing wisdom apply diligence?"
"They do not apply even a particle of diligence toward the aggregates or the elements;
Not applying diligence toward the aggregates is the Bodhisattva's diligence." ^4-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-2]]

Anyone who, having heard that these phenomena are like emanations and illusions,
Is without doubt and repeatedly practices the training,
Know that such a person has long since entered the Mahayana,
And has performed service to many tens of billions of Buddhas. ^4-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-3]]

When a person traveling many yojanas on a wilderness path
Sees cowherds and lush forests at the boundary,
They think, "These are signs that a village or city is near,"
They will find relief and be free from the fear of thieves and robbers. ^4-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-4]]

Likewise, when seeking complete awakening, those who get to hear
This Perfection of Wisdom of the Victors,
Will find relief and become fearless;
This is not the level of an Arhat, nor the level of a Pratyekabuddha. ^4-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-5]]

Just as when a person travels to see the waters of the great ocean,
If one sees trees, forests, and mountains, the ocean is still far away;
If such signs are not seen, one can be certain that the great ocean is near,
And have no doubt about it. ^4-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-6]]

Likewise, those who have truly entered into supreme awakening should understand that by hearing
This Perfection of Wisdom of the Victors,
Even if they have not received a direct prophecy from the Guide,
"Before long, I shall touch the awakening of a Buddha." ^4-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-7]]

When the leaves have emerged on the trees during the fine springtime,
Before long, the branches will produce foliage, fruit, and flowers.
Whoever receives this Perfection of Wisdom into their hands,
Before long, will attain the awakening of the Guides. ^4-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-8]]

For example, when a pregnant woman is afflicted by the pains of labor,
It is said of her, "The time for giving birth has arrived."
Likewise, when a Bodhisattva hears the Victor's wisdom,
And generates joy and aspiration, they will swiftly touch awakening. ^4-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-9]]

Yogis who practice the supreme Perfection of Wisdom,
Do not see any increase or decrease in form.
One who does not see phenomena, non-phenomena, or the sphere of phenomena,
And does not touch nirvana, is established in wisdom. ^4-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-10]]

Whoever practices here does not conceptualize the Buddha-qualities,
Nor do they conceptualize the powers, the bases of miraculous power, awakening, or peace.
They practice through the blessing of non-conceptuality, free from mental construction;
This is the practice of the supreme Perfection of Wisdom. ^4-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-11]]

Subhūti asked the Buddha, the Moon of Speakers:
"What are the obstacles for those who delight in virtuous qualities?"
The Teacher replied: "The obstacles that will arise are many;
Of those, I shall briefly describe just a few." ^4-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-12]]

When writing down this Perfection of Wisdom of the Victors,
Various and diverse kinds of flashes of inspiration will arise;
Yet if, without having benefited sentient beings, they vanish as swiftly as lightning,
This complete deterioration is the work of Māra. ^4-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-13]]

When this is explained, some will harbor doubts, thinking:
"The Leader has not even mentioned my name here;
He has not mentioned my family, my level, or my lineage."
This is the work of Māra, causing them to reject it rather than listen. ^4-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-14]]

Not knowing this, they abandon the root,
And in their delusion, they search for the branches and leaves.
It is just like finding an elephant and then searching for its tracks;
Such is the case for those who have heard the Perfection of Wisdom but still search for other sūtras. ^4-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-15]]

For example, it is like someone who has found food possessing a hundred flavors,
Yet, having found the finest food, they search for poor food.
Just so, having found this Perfection of the Bodhisattvas,
They seek the enlightenment of the Arhat stage. ^4-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-16]]

They will desire honor and desire gain,
And with a mind full of views, they will become intimate with householders;
Abandoning the Dharma, they will engage in non-Dharmic activities—
Leaving the path and wandering onto a wrong path, this is the work of Māra. ^4-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-17]]

Those who, at that time, have become longing and faithful,
Having gone to listen to this sublime Dharma,
Once they perceive the Dharma preacher to be preoccupied,
They will depart without joy and with a troubled mind. ^4-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-18]]

At that time, these activities of Māra will occur.
At that time, after many monks have been stirred to agitation,
Many other various obstacles will also arise,
Which prevent them from upholding this Perfection of Wisdom. ^4-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-19]]

Whatever priceless jewels may be found,
They are rare and always subject to many harms.
Likewise, the Victorious Ones' supreme Perfection of Wisdom,
This precious Dharma is also rare and always subject to many harms. ^4-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-20]]

For those small-minded beings who have newly entered the vehicle,
If they have not attained this rare and precious jewel,
Mara will strive to create obstacles for them;
The Buddhas of the ten directions apply themselves to protecting them. ^4-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-21]]

When a mother falls ill, though she has many children,
They all become distressed and perform healing rites for her.
In the same way, the Buddhas of the world systems in the ten directions
Direct their intention toward this supreme Perfection of Wisdom, who is their mother. ^4-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-22]]

All those who have passed, the current Protectors of the worlds of the ten directions,
And those who will arise in the future, all emerge from her.
She is the Mother who gives birth to the Victors, the teachers of the world,
And she reveals the mental activities of other sentient beings. ^4-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-23]]

The suchness of the world and the suchness of the Arhats,
The suchness of the Pratyekabuddhas and the suchness of the Bodhisattvas—
These are a single suchness, devoid of entities, a non-dual suchness;
The Tathāgata knows this Perfection of Wisdom. ^4-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-24]]

Whether the wise remain in the world or pass into nirvāṇa,
This flawless true nature, the emptiness of all phenomena, remains.
Bodhisattvas realize this suchness;
And therefore, the Buddha is given the name "Tathāgata." ^4-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-25]]

The Perfection of Wisdom is the pleasure grove where they abide;
It is the sphere of activity for the Guides who possess the ten powers.
Although they completely deliver sentient beings from the sufferings of the three lower realms,
They never entertain the notion of a "sentient being." ^4-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-26]]

For example, just as a lion dwelling in a mountain glen is fearless,
And its roar strikes terror into many small wild animals,
So too the Lion among men, by relying on the Perfection of Wisdom,
Terrifies the many non-Buddhists and lets out his roar throughout the world. ^4-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-27]]

For example, just as the rays of the sun abiding in the sky
Dry up the earth and make forms clearly visible,
Likewise, the Dharma King, relying on the Perfection of Wisdom,
Dries up the river of craving and perfectly teaches the Dharma. ^4-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-28]]

One does not see forms, nor does one see feelings,
One does not see perceptions, nor does one see intentions,
That in which consciousness, mind, and intellect are not seen—
The Tathāgata taught that this is "seeing the Dharma." ^4-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-29]]

Sentient beings speak the words, "I see space,"
But how can space be seen? Examine the meaning of this.
The Tathāgata taught that the seeing of phenomena is also like that;
Such seeing cannot be described by any other analogy. ^4-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-30]]

Whoever sees in that way sees all phenomena.
Just as a king remains impartial while his ministers perform all the tasks,
Whatever activities of the Buddhas and teachings of the Śrāvakas there may be,
The Perfection of Wisdom performs them all. ^4-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-31]]

Just as a king does not travel to towns or the countryside,
Yet from within his own domain he gathers all that is collected,
A bodhisattva does not go anywhere away from the nature of phenomena,
Yet they gather all the qualities of the state of buddhahood. ^4-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-32]]

Those who have firm faith in the Sugatas and bodhisattvas,
And whose intentions are focused on the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Having completely transcended the two levels of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
May they swiftly attain the unassailable awakening of the Victors. ^4-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-33]]

For example, if a ship sailing upon the ocean should break apart,
Any person who fails to grasp onto a corpse, some grass, or a piece of wood,
Will perish in the midst of the waters and never reach the shore;
However, those who do grasp hold will reach the far side of the ocean. ^4-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-34]]

In this way, those endowed with faith who have attained confidence,
Do not fail to follow the Mother, the perfection of wisdom. ^4-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-35]]

If their qualities of skillful means are fully embraced by wisdom,
They will swiftly reach the most wonderful enlightenment of the Sugatas. ^4-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-36]]

For example, if someone carries water in an unfired clay pot,
One should know that because it is fragile, it will quickly fall apart.
When water is carried in a fired pot, it does not break on the way;
Without any danger, one arrives home safely. ^4-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-37]]

Likewise, although a bodhisattva may possess great faith,
If they lack wisdom, they will quickly deteriorate.
When faith is fully embraced by wisdom,
They will transcend the two levels and attain supreme awakening. ^4-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-38]]

For example, if a ship has not been well constructed, then in the midst of the ocean,
It will be destroyed along with its cargo and the merchants.
However, if that ship has been well and properly constructed,
It will not be destroyed and will reach the shore together with the cargo. ^4-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-39]]

In this way, even if a bodhisattva is thoroughly imbued with faith,
If they lack wisdom, they will quickly fall away from enlightenment.
However, if they possess the supreme perfection of wisdom,
They will reach the enlightenment of the Victors, unimpaired and undiminished. ^4-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-40]]

Just as a person who is a hundred and twenty years old, aged and suffering,
Is unable to walk on their own even if they stand up,
If they are supported by people on both the left and the right,
They will have no fear of falling and will proceed with ease. ^4-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-41]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva whose power of wisdom is weak,
Even if they have set out, will decline along the way.
If they are fully embraced by skillful means and supreme wisdom,
They will not decline and will reach the awakening of a Tathāgata. ^4-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-42]]

For those bodhisattvas who abide on the level of a beginner,
And have entered into the supreme awakening of a Buddha with a resolute intention,
Those excellent students who possess devotion for their gurus
Should always rely upon wise masters. ^4-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-43]]

Why is that? Because the qualities of the wise arise from them;
They provide instruction in the perfection of wisdom.
"The Buddha-qualities depend upon the spiritual friend,"
So spoke the Victor, who possesses the excellence of all virtues. ^4-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-44]]

Dedicate generosity, ethical discipline, patience, as well as diligence,
Meditative concentration, and wisdom toward awakening.
Do not harbor a superior conceit by clinging to the aggregates of awakening;
Thus should it be taught to those who are beginners. ^4-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-45]]

Those who practice in that way are the moons of speakers who live an excellent life;
They are the refuge, the protector, and the dwelling place of beings.
They are the support, the intellect, the island, and the guide for those seeking benefit,
The illuminator, the lamp, the unshakeable speaker of the supreme Dharma. ^4-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-46]]

Those of great renown don the armor of difficult deeds,
Which is not the armor of the aggregates, elements, and sense sources.
They are free from the concept of the three vehicles and have no grasping;
They are irreversible, unwavering, and possess the quality of being imperturbable. ^4-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-47]]

Those who possess such qualities are free from conceptual elaboration;
Free from doubt, hesitation, and uncertainty, they are endowed with the true meaning.
Having heard the perfection of wisdom, they do not shrink back;
They do not rely on others and should be known as irreversible. ^4-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-48]]

This Dharma of the Leaders is profound and difficult to see;
It is not something realized by anyone, nor is it something to be attained.
Therefore, the Compassionate Benefactor, having attained awakening,
Felt little concern, thinking, "Who among the masses of beings will understand?" ^4-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-49]]

Sentient beings delight in foundations and desire sense objects;
Abiding in grasping, they are unskilled, foolish, and deluded like darkness.
The phenomena to be attained are without foundation and without grasping;
Therefore, conflict with the world arises. ^4-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-50]]

The element of space in the eastern direction and the southern direction,
And likewise in the western direction and the northern direction, is infinite;
It exists above, below, and in all the ten directions as far as they extend.
It is without differentiation and without any distinctions. ^4-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-51]]

The suchness of the past, the suchness of the future,
The suchness of the present, and the suchness of the Arhats,
The suchness of all phenomena, and the suchness of the Victors—
This suchness of phenomena is entirely without distinction. ^4-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-52]]

The awakening of the Sugatas is free from any differentiation of phenomena;
Any bodhisattva who wishes to attain this,
If they lack the wisdom of the Guides endowed with skillful means, will not attain it. ^4-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-53]]

Just as a bird with a massive body of one hundred and fifty yojanas,
If its wings are exhausted and it lacks strength and skill,
Even if it leaps from the Abode of the Thirty-Three to this world of Jambudvīpa,
It will surely meet with disaster and ruin. ^4-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-54]]

Even if these five perfections of the Victors were practiced for many hundreds of billions of eons,
And one constantly relied upon infinite and vast aspirations within the world,
Without skillful means and separated from wisdom, one falls into the level of a śrāvaka. ^4-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-55]]

Whoever wishes to set forth in this Buddha vehicle
Should train with an even mind toward all beings, the recognition of them as parents,
A helpful attitude, and a loving heart,
Being without harshness, remaining straightforward, and speaking gentle words. ^4-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-56]]

Thus did the elder Subhūti request of the Protector of the World:
"Please explain the signs of the ocean of qualities free from afflictions,
And how those of great power do not turn back;
O Victor, please grant a prophecy regarding even a mere portion of such qualities." ^4-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-57]]

They are free from the perception of diversity and speak words consistent with reason;
They do not rely on other śramaṇas or brahmins.
These wise ones have forever abandoned the three lower realms;
They are intensely devoted to the paths of the ten virtuous actions. ^4-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-58]]

They teach the Dharma to beings without any regard for material gain.
They take singular delight in the Dharma and always speak with gentle words.
They possess constant alertness while moving, walking, sleeping, and sitting.
They walk while looking only a yoke-length ahead, keeping the mind free from delusion. ^4-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-59]]

Pure and wearing clean robes, they are pure in the three isolations;
They do not desire gain, and like great bulls, they always long for the Dharma.
They have transcended the realm of Māra and are not dependent on others;
They meditate on the four concentrations, yet do not dwell within those concentrations. ^4-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-60]]

They do not desire fame, and their minds are not entangled by anger.
Even if they live as householders, they are always without attachment to any material things.
For the sake of their livelihood, they do not seek out vast possessions.
They do not practice wrathful spells of violence, nor do they practice spells for union with women. ^4-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-61]]

They do not act as diviners, predicting whether those engaged in desire will have a boy or a girl.
They strive in the supreme perfection of wisdom, which is utterly secluded, free from strife and contention, and their minds of loving-kindness are firm.
Desiring omniscience, they are steadfast and possess a mind constantly dedicated to it. ^4-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-62]]

They have completely avoided the borderlands and the regions of barbarian peoples.
They are never uncertain about their own stage, remaining as steadfast as Mount Meru.
They would even sacrifice their lives for the sake of the Dharma and are devoted to the diligence of yoga.
One should know that these are the signs of the state of non-retrogression. ^4-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-63]]

Form, feeling, perception, volition, and consciousness are profound; by nature, they are signless and utterly peaceful.
For example, just as one might seek to reach the bottom of the ocean with an arrow,
When the aggregates are analyzed with wisdom, their bottom cannot be found. ^4-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-64]]

Any bodhisattva who has become profound in that way,
And who realizes this Dharma—the ultimate, unattached vehicle in which aggregates,
Elements, and sense sources do not exist—
What could there be that is a more perfect accomplishment of merit than that? ^4-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-65]]

For example, consider a man who indulges in desire and,
Having made an appointment with a woman, fails to meet her;
However many conceptual thoughts he experiences in his mind throughout a single day,
For that many eons, the bodhisattva will achieve attainment. ^4-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-66]]

Any bodhisattva who, for many billions of eons,
Practices generosity toward Arhats and Pratyekabuddhas and maintains ethical discipline,
Cannot match the merit of one who speaks of the Dharma that possesses the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Even if that merit from giving and morality were spoken of well. ^4-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-67]]

Any bodhisattva who meditates on the supreme perfection of wisdom,
And having arisen from that, teaches the stainless Dharma,
And dedicates that for the sake of beings as the cause of enlightenment—
There is no virtue in the three worlds equal to that. ^4-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-68]]

Know that such merit is also accumulated, and likewise empty,
Hollow, insubstantial, and without essence.
When one practices the wisdom of the Sugatas in that manner,
At the time of practicing, one fully grasps immeasurable merit. ^4-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-69]]

Knowing that all these dharmas, which the Buddha has thoroughly practiced and clearly taught,
Are exhausted as mere expressions,
Even if one were to speak of them for many tens of billions of eons,
The expanse of phenomena is without exhaustion and without increase. ^4-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-70]]

Whatever is called the perfection of the Victors,
Those phenomena are also proclaimed to be merely names.
A bodhisattva who dedicates without conceptual arrogance
Will not decline and will touch the supreme awakening of a Buddha. ^4-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-71]]

Just as when a lamp is lit, the wick is not consumed by the first encounter with the flame,
The wick is not the flame, yet without it, the flame does not burn;
The wick is not burned by its final contact with the flame,
Just as without that final flame, the wick is not burned. ^4-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-72]]

Supreme enlightenment is not reached by the initial mind,
Yet without that initial mind, it cannot be reached.
Peaceful enlightenment is not attained by the final mind,
Yet without that, it is also impossible to attain it. ^4-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-73]]

From a seed, the sprout, flower, and fruit arise;
Just as it is not the case that the tree does not exist because the seed has ceased,
So too, the initial mind is the cause of awakening;
It is not the case that awakening does not exist because that mind has ceased. ^4-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-74]]

Just as a water jar is gradually filled, little by little, from the first drop to the last,
So too, the initial mind is the cause of supreme awakening;
By gradually perfecting virtuous qualities, one becomes a Buddha. ^4-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-75]]

While practicing the dharmas of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness,
They do not touch upon nirvāṇa, yet do not engage in signs;
For example, just as a skilled boatman goes back and forth,
Without staying on either shore and without remaining in the ocean. ^4-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-76]]

A bodhisattva practicing in this way has no conceit, thinking,
"I will be prophesied by the Ten-Powered Ones and shall reach enlightenment."
Thinking, "There is nothing at all here," they have no fear of enlightenment.
To practice in such a manner is to practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^4-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-77]]

Seeing the world as a desolate wilderness of paths, famine, and disease,
They are without fear and don their armor;
Striving constantly until the furthest limits of the future, with supreme understanding,
They do not give rise to even a speck of weariness in their minds. ^4-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-78]]

Thus the bodhisattvas practice the wisdom of the Victors;
They know that these aggregates are empty and unborn from the very beginning.
While not in meditative equipoise, they engage in compassion for the realm of sentient beings,
And even during that interval, they never decline from the Buddha-dharma. ^4-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-79]]

For example, a skillful person endowed with all excellent qualities,
Who is powerful, invincible, diligent, and knowledgeable in the techniques of the arts,
Who has mastered archery and many other fields of craftsmanship,
And who, knowing how to perform illusions, greatly desires the welfare of beings— ^4-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-80]]

Having gathered together his father, mother, and wife,
Travels along a wilderness path where many enemies dwell,
And there conjures many heroic and courageous men;
Just as they then travel in safety and return home once again, ^4-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-81]]

Likewise, the skillful bodhisattva also,
Generates great compassion for all realms of sentient beings,
And having completely transcended the four Māras and even the two levels,
Abides in supreme meditative absorption, yet does not touch upon awakening. ^4-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-82]]

Wind relies upon space, and the mass of water relies upon that.
This great earth relies upon that, and living beings rely upon it.
Such is the causal basis for sentient beings' engagement in karma;
Reflect on the meaning of this: upon what does space abide? ^4-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-83]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva who abides in emptiness,
Through aspirations based on the knowledge of sentient beings in the world,
Manifests a great variety of diverse activities,
Yet does not touch upon nirvana, nor do they remain in emptiness. ^4-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-84]]

When a bodhisattva who is wise and clear-minded
Practices this supreme samadhi of empty peace,
During that time, one does not cultivate any signs;
Abiding in the signless, one practices peace and utter tranquility. ^4-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-85]]

A bird flying in the sky has no fixed station;
It does not remain there, yet it does not fall to the ground.
In this way, a bodhisattva who practices the gateways to liberation
Does not touch upon nirvāṇa, yet does not engage with signs. ^4-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-86]]

For example, a man trained in archery shoots an arrow into the sky,
And by successively following it with other arrows,
He allows no opportunity for that first arrow to fall to the ground;
If that person so desires, the arrow will fall to the ground. ^4-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-87]]

Likewise, those who practice the supreme perfection of wisdom,
And who act through the power of wisdom, skillful means, and miraculous abilities,
Will not attain that sublime emptiness as long as their roots of virtue are not yet complete. ^4-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-88]]

For example, a monk endowed with the supreme power of miracles
Might remain in the sky and simultaneously display various transformations;
He demonstrates walking, moving, lying down, and sitting,
And he does not grow weary, nor does he become exhausted. ^4-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-89]]

Likewise, the wise bodhisattva, while dwelling in emptiness,
Abides without a fixed place, having perfected gnosis and miraculous powers;
They display an infinite variety of activities for the sake of sentient beings,
And for ten million eons, they feel no weariness or exhaustion. ^4-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-90]]

For example, just as someone standing at a great precipice,
Holding two parasols in their two hands, leaps into the sky,
And having cast their body into the great abyss,
They do not fall, but continue to travel for as long as they are there. ^4-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-91]]

Likewise, the wise bodhisattva abides in compassion,
Having fully taken up the two umbrellas of skillful means and wisdom;
They contemplate all phenomena as empty, signless, and wishless,
And without touching nirvana, they also perceive the true nature of phenomena. ^4-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-92]]

For example, just as those who desire jewels go to a jewel island,
And having found jewels, return home again,
They do not live in happiness there as solitary captains,
While leaving their circle of kinsmen and friends in misery. ^4-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-93]]

So too, the bodhisattva who has gone to the jewel island of emptiness,
Having attained the meditative concentrations, faculties, and powers,
Does not take delight in merely touching nirvana alone,
And does not leave the suffering of sentient beings behind. ^4-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-94]]

For example, in order to gain knowledge of the route, a merchant seeking profit
Travels to whatever cities, towns, or villages lie along the way;
He does not remain there, nor does he remain even on the Island of Jewels,
And without staying at home, he becomes an expert on the path. ^4-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-95]]

Likewise, the brilliant bodhisattva is skilled in all the knowledge and liberations
Of the Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas;
He does not abide in those, nor does he abide in the primordial wisdom of a Buddha;
He does not abide in the unconditioned; he is one who knows the nature of the path. ^4-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-96]]

When, having connected with beings through loving-kindness,
He practices the meditative stabilizations of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness,
It is impossible to designate that he has attained nirvana,
Or to designate him as something conditioned; he does not abide anywhere. ^4-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-97]]

For example, just as the body of a magically conjured person is not invisible,
And can be designated even by a name,
Likewise, a bodhisattva who practices the doors of liberation
Can be designated even by a name. ^4-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-98]]

If, having thoroughly inquired about their conduct and faculties,
The bodhisattva does not fully demonstrate the dharmas of emptiness and signlessness,
Nor explain the dharmas of the irreversible stage,
One should understand that such a person has not been prophesied. ^4-98

## Teaching of the peak of complete realization ^5-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-1]]

The fifth chapter explains the clear realization of the pinnacle.
One who does not desire the level of an Arhat, the wisdom of a Pratyekabuddha,
Or the three realms, even in a dream,
But sees the Buddhas and teaches the Dharma to beings—
One should understand that this is the prophecy of non-retrogression. ^5-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-2]]

If, in a dream, one sees sentient beings dwelling in the three lower realms,
And in that very moment makes a prayer to cut the continuum of the lower realms,
And pacifies a mass of fire through the power of the blessing of truth—
One should understand that this is the prophecy of non-retrogression. ^5-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-3]]

The many spirits and diseases in the human world
Are pacified through the blessing of truth by those endowed with benefit and compassion;
Yet they remain without conceit and do not give rise to pride—
One should understand that this is the prophecy of non-retrogression. ^5-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-4]]

Or if, because they have acquired various blessings of truth,
They develop conceit, thinking, "I have been prophesied,"
Or if they become conceited because other bodhisattvas have received a prophecy,
Know that those who abide in such conceit are of small intelligence. ^5-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-5]]

For when Māra approaches by means of a name,
He speaks thus: "This is the name of you, your parents,
And your lineage back through seven generations of ancestors;
And when you become a Buddha, this will be your name." ^5-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-6]]

"And such will be the nature of your future ascetic discipline and yoga;
Your former qualities were also like this," he declares.
Any bodhisattva who, upon hearing such words, becomes conceited,
Should be known as one of small intelligence who has been instigated by Māra. ^5-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-7]]

A bodhisattva who frequents secluded villages, cities, mountain clefts,
And remote forest hermitages, yet praises themselves and disparages others,
Should be known as one of small intelligence who has been instigated by Māra. ^5-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-8]]

Except for those who constantly dwell in villages, regions, and towns,
Striving for awakening and the ripening of sentient beings,
And who do not generate the desire for the state of an Arhat or Pratyekabuddha—
This is what the Sugata calls the solitude of the [Buddha's] sons. ^5-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-9]]

Even if one dwells for many millions of years in a mountain cleft
Five hundred yojanas wide and filled with snakes,
A bodhisattva who does not understand this solitude
Will fall into arrogance and remain in a state of distraction. ^5-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-10]]

A bodhisattva who strives for the benefit of beings and has attained meditative concentration,
Powers, faculties, liberations, and absorptions—if one treats them with contempt,
Thinking, "This person does not practice in forest isolation,"
The Victor has declared that such a person dwells within the domain of Māra. ^5-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-11]]

Whether one dwells in a town or in a forest,
If one is free from the mind of the two vehicles and is certain of supreme enlightenment,
This is the solitude of those who have entered into the service of beings;
Any bodhisattva who feels superior to them has degraded themselves. ^5-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-12]]

Therefore, those who seek supreme enlightenment,
Being wise and possessing intense resolve, should utterly conquer pride;
Just as a multitude of the sick rely on a physician for healing,
One should rely on a spiritual friend without any distraction. ^5-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-13]]

Bodhisattvas who have entered the supreme awakening of a Buddha
Should rely on the perfections as their spiritual friends.
Those who provide such instruction are the ground of accomplishment;
Through these two causes, the awakening of a Buddha is swiftly realized. ^5-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-14]]

For all the Victors of the past, the future, and those residing in the ten directions,
This perfection is the path; there is no other.
This perfection is said to be the clarity, the lamp, the light,
And the supreme guide for those who have entered upon supreme enlightenment. ^5-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-15]]

Just as the perfection of wisdom is empty of intrinsic characteristics,
One should understand that all these phenomena are likewise empty of such characteristics.
When one thoroughly understands that all phenomena are empty and signless,
To practice in such a manner is to practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^5-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-16]]

Sentient beings, completely famished, desire food,
And those with minds attached to cyclic existence wander perpetually.
The two phenomena of "self" and "mine" are unreal and empty;
Yet the foolish, by their own doing, tie knots in the sky. ^5-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-17]]

For example, it is like being stricken by poison through the mere perception of suspicion;
Even if that poison has not entered the body, one will fall unconscious.
Likewise, the childish who claim "I" and "mine,"
Have the notion of a self, and through unreal conceptualization, are born and die in impermanence. ^5-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-18]]

It is taught that the way one grasps is the way one is completely afflicted;
It is said that when "I" and "mine" are not observed, there is total purification.
In this, without anything being defiled or purified,
The Bodhisattva realizes the perfection of wisdom. ^5-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-19]]

However many sentient beings there are throughout Jambudvīpa without exception,
If all of them were to generate the mind of supreme enlightenment,
And were to practice generosity for many tens of millions of millennia,
And were to dedicate it all as a cause for awakening for the sake of all beings, ^5-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-20]]

If someone were to diligently apply themselves to the supreme perfection of wisdom,
And practice in accordance with it for even a single day,
The mass of merit from that giving would not equal even a fraction of this.
Therefore, one should always engage in wisdom without distraction. ^5-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-21]]

Yogis who practice the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Though they generate great compassion, have no perception of sentient beings.
At that time, those wise ones become the objects of offering for all the world;
They always make meaningful use of the alms of the country. ^5-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-22]]

Any bodhisattva who has long been connected to this,
In order to liberate beings of the heavens, the human realm, and the three lower realms,
And who desires to show the vast path to the realm of beings on the other side,
Should day and night apply themselves to the perfection of wisdom. ^5-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-23]]

Just as a person who finds a supreme jewel they had never found before is filled with joy,
But if, immediately after finding it, they become careless and lose it,
They will suffer constant longing for the jewel they have lost. ^5-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-24]]

Likewise, those who have entered supreme enlightenment should never abandon their yoga of the perfection of wisdom, which is like a precious jewel.
Just as one who finds a jewel, takes it up, wraps it carefully, and hurrying along, reaches a state of peace, ^5-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-25]]

Like the sun, free from clouds and arrayed in a lattice of light rays,
Arising to conquer all the dense, pitch-black darkness,
It eclipses all fireflies, living beings, and spirits,
As well as the hosts of stars and all the light of the moon. ^5-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-26]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva who practices the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Dwelling in emptiness and the signless,
Having destroyed the dense darkness of views, outshines all worldly beings,
Arhats, Pratyekabuddhas, and the many bodhisattvas. ^5-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-27]]

For example, a prince who desires to give away wealth
Becomes the foremost of all and is worthy of being highly honored;
Even now, he brings joy to many sentient beings;
What need is there to mention when he has acquired the means and is established in the kingdom? ^5-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-28]]

In this way, the bodhisattva who is skilled in the practice of wisdom
Bestows the nectar of immortality and brings joy to gods and humans.
If even now they strive for the benefit of many sentient beings,
What need is there to mention when they are established as Kings of Dharma? ^5-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-29]]

At that time, the Māras will be pierced with the thorns of anguish,
Distressed, sorrowful, dejected, and weak.
Thinking, "How can I make this bodhisattva's mind falter?"
They set the directions ablaze and hurl meteors to display terrifying sights. ^5-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-30]]

However, when those wise ones possess intense resolve,
And day and night look toward the supreme meaning of the perfection of wisdom,
At that time, their body, mind, and speech move like a bird in the sky;
How could the factions of darkness ever find an opportunity against them? ^5-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-31]]

When bodhisattvas fight and quarrel,
And harbor mutual discord and angry thoughts,
At that time, Māra rejoices and becomes extremely comfortable,
Thinking that both are far removed from the primordial wisdom of the Victors. ^5-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-32]]

Both are distant and will become like flesh-eating demons;
Both will cause their own commitments to degenerate.
How can there be awakening for those who are hostile and devoid of patience?
At that time, the Māras and their factions will be delighted. ^5-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-33]]

If a bodhisattva who has not received a prophecy
Becomes angry and initiates a dispute with one who has received a prophecy,
For as many moments as there were of that harsh and flawed state of mind,
For that many eons they must once again don their armor from the very beginning. ^5-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-34]]

The Buddhas reached awakening through the perfection of patience;
Generating mindfulness, thinking, "This mind of mine is not good,"
One should individually confess and restrain it for the future,
And not take delight in it; that is how one trains in this Dharma of the Buddha. ^5-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-35]]

When training, one does not make any claims about the training;
One does not perceive a trainee or the qualities to be trained in,
And does not conceptualize between training and non-training—
Whoever trains in this way trains here in these Buddha-qualities. ^5-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-36]]

Any bodhisattva who understands this kind of training
Will never again suffer a decline in training or become immoral;
In order to please the Buddha-dharmas, they train in this,
Skilled in the higher training, they train without apprehension. ^5-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-37]]

In this way, the wise light-makers train in wisdom;
They do not give rise to even a single unvirtuous thought.
Just as darkness cannot remain in the space before it
When it is overwhelmed by the rays of the sun moving through the sky, ^5-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-38]]

For those who have trained in the perfection of wisdom,
All the perfections are gathered within this.
Just as the sixty-two views are contained within the view of the transitory collection,
So too, it is taught that all these perfections are contained within this one. ^5-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-39]]

For example, when the life faculty ceases,
All other faculties, however many there may be, likewise cease.
In the same way, for the supreme sages who practice the perfection of wisdom,
It is taught that all the perfections are gathered within this. ^5-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-40]]

Bodhisattvas, being skillful, train in whatever qualities the śrāvakas possess,
And likewise, they train in all the qualities of the pratyekabuddhas;
Yet they do not abide in them, nor do they develop a longing for them,
Thinking, "This is what I must learn," and for that reason they train. ^5-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-41]]

Those who have entered into the supreme, irreversible awakening,
Rejoice from the depths of their hearts in the generation of the mind of enlightenment;
Though one might find the measure of the three thousandfold world system and Mount Meru by weighing them on a scale,
The virtue of such rejoicing is not like that. ^5-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-42]]

I also rejoice in the mass of merit of all sentient beings, as many as there are,
Who strive for virtue and desire to be of benefit;
Therefore, once they have attained the qualities of the Victors,
They bestow the gift of Dharma upon the world in order to bring suffering to an end. ^5-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-43]]

Any bodhisattva who fully understands that all phenomena are non-conceptual,
Empty, signless, and free from conceptual proliferation,
Does not seek awakening through a dualistic wisdom;
That yogi strives in the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^5-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-44]]

Just as the element of space and that space itself do not conflict,
And it does not exist as something to be attained by anyone at all,
Likewise, the bodhisattva who is skilled in the practice of wisdom
Is like space, and practices in a state of total peace. ^5-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-45]]

In the midst of a crowd, a conjured person does not think,
"I must please these people," and yet they perform that very action;
Although they are seen displaying various miraculous feats,
They possess no body, no mind, and not even a name. ^5-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-46]]

In the same way, while practicing wisdom, it never occurs to one to think,
"Having realized awakening, I shall liberate all beings."
Although they display various births and many activities,
They practice without conceptualization, just like an illusion. ^5-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-47]]

For example, a phantom buddha performs the deeds of a buddha,
Yet not even a trace of pride arises in that performance.
Likewise, the bodhisattva who is skilled in the practice of wisdom
Displays all activities just like a magical emanation. ^5-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-48]]

Just as a skilled carpenter creates mechanical figures of men and women
That perform all kinds of tasks,
Likewise, the bodhisattva who is skilled in the practice of wisdom
Performs all activities through non-conceptual primordial awareness. ^5-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-49]]

To those who practice skillfully in this way, the many hosts of gods
Join their palms in reverence and prostrate.
All the Buddhas throughout the world systems of the ten directions
Likewise utter garlands of praise for their qualities. ^5-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-50]]

Even if all the sentient beings in realms as numerous as the sands of the Ganges
Were to be considered as having become Māras,
And even if each of them emanated that many more from every single hair,
All of them together would be unable to create obstacles for the wise. ^5-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-51]]

Through four causes, the wise and powerful bodhisattva
Is difficult for the four Māras to overcome and cannot be shaken:
Abiding in emptiness, never forsaking sentient beings,
Acting exactly as one speaks, and possessing the blessings of the Sugatas. ^5-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-52]]

When this Perfection of Wisdom, the Mother of the Tathāgatas, is being taught,
Any bodhisattva who feels devotion and applies themselves with sincere resolve to its realization,
Know that such a one has entered into omniscience itself. ^5-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-53]]

It does not abide within the dharmadhātu or suchness;
For example, like a cloud in the sky, it abides without a place to abide.
A mantra-holder, a sky-traveler without a fixed abode, desiring a place to stay,
Through the power of mantra, blesses trees to be endowed with out-of-season flowers. ^5-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-54]]

In that way, the skillful and brilliant bodhisattvas practice;
They do not perceive a realizer, nor are the Buddha-dharmas observed.
One does not perceive an expounder, nor is one who desires or views the Dharma observed.
This is the dwelling of those who seek peace and delight in virtuous qualities. ^5-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-55]]

Apart from the liberation of the Tathāgatas and the Arhats,
This state is the dwelling of the Hearers and the Pratyekabuddhas;
It is a dwelling in the meditative concentration of peace, endowed with sublime tranquility and bliss,
The most supreme and unsurpassed of all such states. ^5-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-56]]

Though birds dwell in the sky, they do not fall;
Though fish dwell in the water, they do not suffocate or die.
Likewise, through the power of meditative concentration, Bodhisattvas
Who have reached the far shore dwell in emptiness but do not pass into nirvāṇa. ^5-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-57]]

Those who wish to attain the supreme qualities of all beings,
To touch the most marvelous and supreme primordial wisdom of the Buddha,
And to bestow the most excellent and holy gift of Dharma,
Should rely upon this supreme state of the benefactors. ^5-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-58]]

When the Guides fully describe the trainings,
This training is the supreme and unsurpassed among all trainings.
Any wise person who wishes to reach the far shore of all trainings
Should train in this Buddha-training, the Perfection of Wisdom. ^5-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-59]]

This is the supreme treasure of Dharma and the most excellent Dharma treasury;
It is the Buddha lineage, the treasury of happiness and joy for all beings.
The protectors of the world in the ten directions—those of the past and those yet to come—
All arise from this, yet the expanse of reality is never exhausted. ^5-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-60]]

Just as all the various trees, fruits, flowers, and forests
All emerge and grow perfectly from the earth,
Yet the earth is neither exhausted nor increased;
It does not decline, it is non-conceptual, and it never tires. ^5-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-61]]

The Buddha’s heirs, the listeners, the solitary realizers, the gods,
And all the various phenomena of happiness and well-being for all beings,
All these arise from the supreme Perfection of Wisdom;
Yet these wisdoms are also neither exhausted nor increased. ^5-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-62]]

However many sentient beings there are—the lesser, the middling, and the superior—
The Sugata taught that they all arise from ignorance.
When conditions assemble, the machine of suffering fully emerges;
That machine of ignorance neither exhausts nor increases. ^5-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-63]]

However many aspects of primordial wisdom, gateways, methods, and roots there may be,
All these arise from the supreme Perfection of Wisdom.
When conditions assemble, the machine of karma fully emerges;
In the Perfection of Wisdom, there is no exhaustion and no increase. ^5-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-64]]

Any bodhisattva who, regarding dependent origination,
Understands this wisdom to be without birth and without exhaustion,
Just as the cloudless sun dispels darkness by radiating its beams of light,
Will, by destroying the dense gloom of ignorance, attain the self-arisen state. ^5-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-65]]

Possessing great power, they also dwell in the four concentrations,
Yet they do not rely upon them, nor do they make them a fixed abode.
Nevertheless, these four concentrations, along with their respective branches,
Serve as the basis for attaining the supreme and most excellent awakening. ^5-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-66]]

Those who have attained supreme wisdom and abide in the meditative concentrations
Also experience the four sublime formless meditative stabilizations.
These concentrations are beneficial for supreme and sublime awakening;
Yet, a bodhisattva does not train in them for the sake of exhausting the outflows. ^5-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-67]]

This is a marvelous and wondrous accumulation of qualities:
Abiding in meditative concentration and stabilization, they are without signs.
If those who abide therein should have their bodies perish,
They will be reborn in the desire realm exactly according to their intentions. ^5-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-68]]

For example, it is like a person of Jambudvīpa who had never before
Reached the supreme city of the gods, but later goes there;
Having seen those lands that are fully possessed there,
Just as one who returns here does not become attached, ^5-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-69]]

Likewise, the bodhisattvas who possess supreme qualities,
Diligent in yoga and abiding in meditative concentration and absorption,
Dwell once again in the desire realm without attachment;
Like a lotus in the water, they are not stained by the qualities of the foolish. ^5-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-70]]

The great beings exist solely to bring sentient beings to maturity,
To purify the fields, and to complete the perfections;
They do not seek rebirth in the formless realm, thinking
That the qualities of enlightenment and the perfections would thereby decline. ^5-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-71]]

For example, if a person were to find a treasure of precious gems,
They would not give rise to a mind of desire for them;
Yet, at another time, they would take them all,
And having taken them and gone home, they would remain without attachment, just so. ^5-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-72]]

Likewise, the wise bodhisattva, having attained the fourfold tranquility
Of concentration and meditative absorption that bestows joy and bliss,
Relinquishes that blissful concentration and the meditative absorptions they have attained,
And out of compassion for beings, enters again into the realm of desire. ^5-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-73]]

If a bodhisattva, while dwelling in meditative absorption and concentration,
Should generate a mind that desires the vehicle of the Arhats or Pratyekabuddhas,
They have become uncomposed, agitated, and mentally distracted;
The Buddha's qualities are completely lost, like a ferryman whose boat has been wrecked. ^5-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-74]]

Furthermore, even though they are intensely engaged with the five sensory qualities—
Forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and textures—
If they take joy in the Bodhicitta that is free from the vehicles of the Arhats and Pratyekabuddhas,
Know that these heroes are always in meditative equipoise. ^5-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-75]]

For the sake of other beings and persons, the bodhisattva of pure intention
Applies themselves with diligent exertion to the perfection of perseverance.
For example, just as a female slave carrying water is powerless and under the control of her master,
So too do the heroic beings proceed under the control of all sentient beings. ^5-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-76]]

Whether they are reviled or even constantly beaten,
Just as a female slave does not speak back to her master,
Thinking, "This person might even kill me,"
They are overcome by a mind of extreme terror and fear. ^5-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-77]]

Likewise, those who have entered the supreme awakening for the sake of enlightenment
Should remain as if they were servants to all living beings.
From this, they will attain awakening and perfect all virtuous qualities;
Just as fire arising from grass and wood consumes that very fuel. ^5-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-78]]

Having abandoned one's own happiness with a mind free of expectations,
One should strive diligently day and night for the needs of other sentient beings.
Just as a mother would attend to an only child,
Remain with a sincere heart, free from any sense of weariness. ^5-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-79]]

Any bodhisattva who wishes to remain for a long time in cyclic existence,
A yogi who perseveres in the welfare of sentient beings and the purification of the realm,
If they do not give rise to even a speck of weariness,
They possess the perfection of diligence and are never negligent. ^5-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-80]]

If unskilled bodhisattvas count out tens of millions of eons,
And, perceiving it as a long time, strive for enlightenment with a sense of hardship,
They will suffer for a long duration while practicing the authentic Dharma.
Such individuals are lazy, and their perfection of diligence declines. ^5-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-81]]

From the first generation of the mind set upon supreme enlightenment,
Until the attainment of unsurpassed awakening,
If one maintains this mental focus for even a single day and night,
The wise and clear-minded should be known as having initiated diligence. ^5-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-82]]

If someone were to say, "After you have demolished Mount Meru,
You will attain supreme enlightenment," and if one then considers the extent of the weariness that would arise,
At that moment, the bodhisattva has become lazy. ^5-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-83]]

If, however, one thinks, "What is difficult about such a task?"
And develops the thought to reduce Mount Meru to dust in a mere instant,
That wise bodhisattva has initiated diligent effort,
And will, before long, attain the supreme enlightenment of a Leader. ^5-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-84]]

If, with the thought, "I shall bring all beings to maturity and act for their benefit,"
One applies oneself with body, speech, and mind,
Yet remains dwelling in the perception of a self, one has become lazy;
They are as far from omniscience as the earth is from the sky. ^5-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-85]]

When they have no perception of a body, a mind, or a sentient being,
And having turned back from perceptions, they practice the non-dual Dharma—
This is what the Benefactor called the perfection of perseverance
For those who desire the supreme, undeterred, and peaceful awakening. ^5-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-86]]

If they should hear others speaking ill or using harsh words,
The wise bodhisattva rejoices, thinking, "This is my happiness."
Who speaks, who hears, who [speaks] to whom, and for what reason?
One who possesses this supreme perfection of patience is wise. ^5-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-87]]

Any bodhisattva who possesses the excellent quality of patience—
Suppose someone were to fill the three thousandfold world system with jewels and offer them to the Knower of the World,
To the Buddhas, Arhats, and Pratyekabuddhas;
The mass of merit from that giving would not equal even a fraction of this. ^5-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-88]]

The body of one who abides in patience becomes completely pure,
Possessing the thirty-two major marks and infinite power;
They proclaim the supreme Dharma of emptiness to sentient beings.
Wise and patient, they bring joy to the world. ^5-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-89]]

If some being were to take a sachet of sandalwood powder
And, with great devotion, apply it to a bodhisattva,
While another person sprinkles glowing embers over his head,
He should generate a mind that is equal and impartial toward both. ^5-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-90]]

When the wise bodhisattva has practiced such patience,
And dedicates that generation of the mind toward supreme enlightenment,
That hero, through patience, surpasses all the world’s Arhats,
Pratyekabuddhas, and however many diverse realms of sentient beings there are. ^5-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-91]]

One who practices patience should also cultivate this thought:
"In the hells, the animal realm, and the world of Yama, there is much suffering.
Since I have helplessly experienced harm due to the causes of sensual desire,
Why should I not now endure hardship for the sake of enlightenment?" ^5-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-92]]

Whips, sticks, weapons, being killed, bound, or beaten,
Decapitation, and the cutting off of ears, feet, hands, and nose—
Thinking, "I shall endure whatever worldly sufferings there may be,"
The bodhisattva abides in the perfection of patience. ^5-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-93]]

Through ethical discipline, those who desire peace become noble.
Abiding in the domain of the ten powers, their ethical discipline never declines.
They follow all the various activities of the vows;
For the benefit of all beings, they also dedicate them to enlightenment. ^5-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-94]]

If one wishes to attain the awakening of an Arhat or a Pratyekabuddha,
One is immoral, unskilled, and one’s conduct has degenerated.
But if one fully dedicates toward supreme, peaceful awakening,
Even while engaged in sensory pleasures, one abides in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-95]]

Those qualities from which the virtues of certain awakening arise—
This is the meaning of ethical discipline for those endowed with virtuous qualities.
The complete degeneration of the awakening of those who perform beneficial dharmas—
This is what the Guide has called "immoral conduct." ^5-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-96]]

Even if a bodhisattva indulges in the five objects of sensory desire,
If they have gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the noble Sangha,
And maintain the mental orientation toward omniscience, thinking, "I must achieve buddhahood,"
The wise should understand that they abide in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-97]]

If, for ten million eons, one practices the ten paths of virtuous action,
Yet generates a desire for the state of an Arhat or a Pratyekabuddha,
At that moment, one’s ethical discipline is flawed and has degenerated;
That generation of such a thought is far weightier than even a formal defeat. ^5-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-98]]

While guarding ethical discipline and fully dedicating it to supreme awakening,
One is without conceit and does not praise oneself;
One has completely abandoned the perception of a self and the perception of sentient beings;
Such a bodhisattva is said to abide in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-98

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-99]]

If a bodhisattva practicing the path of the Victors thinks,
"These sentient beings are ethical, but those are unethical,"
Engaging in such diverse perceptions, they are utterly lacking in ethical discipline;
Their morality has degenerated, and they lack completely purified morality. ^5-99

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-100]]

For one who has no perception of a self or perception of a sentient being,
And whose perceptions are free from attachment, how could there be a lack of restraint?
One who has no conceit regarding restraint or lack of restraint—
This is what the Guide has called the restraint of morality. ^5-100

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-101]]

Any pure being who possesses such morality as this,
Is without bias toward anything pleasant or unpleasant.
They give away their heads, hands, and feet without a trace of discouragement;
They give away all their possessions, remaining forever free from attachment. ^5-101

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-102]]

Having realized that all phenomena are by nature selfless and insubstantial,
If they can give away even their own flesh without a discouraged mind,
Then at that time, what need is there to mention the giving of external objects?
If they were to be stingy, it would be impossible. ^5-102

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-103]]

Those who grasp at the actual perception of a self and hold things as "mine" are attached;
How could fools like these ever have the intention to give?
Those who are stingy will be born in the realm of hungry ghosts;
Even if they are born as humans, they will be destitute at that time. ^5-103

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-104]]

At that time, the bodhisattva, knowing that sentient beings are impoverished,
Will take delight in generosity and always give with great excellence.
They feel more joy from giving away the four continents, beautifully adorned, as if they were a glob of spittle,
Than they would from acquiring those continents. ^5-104

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-105]]

When wise and brilliant bodhisattvas give a gift, they think,
"May this gift be given to every single sentient being throughout the three realms of existence,"
And having given it, they dedicate it toward supreme enlightenment for the benefit of all beings. ^5-105

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-106]]

Having given a gift, one does not dwell upon it as a substantial thing;
One never has any expectation for its karmic maturation.
Those who are skilled in giving in such a way give everything;
By giving even a small amount, it becomes vast and immeasurable. ^5-106

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-107]]

However many sentient beings there are without exception throughout the three realms of existence,
If one were to take all of them as a basis for consideration, and if all of them,
To the Knowers of the World—the Buddhas, Arhats, and Pratyekabuddhas—
Were to give gifts for infinite eons while seeking the excellence of a Śrāvaka, ^5-107

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-108]]

Yet a wise bodhisattva with skillful means
Were to rejoice in the substance of those meritorious deeds,
And fully dedicate it toward supreme, sublime enlightenment for the sake of sentient beings,
By that complete dedication, one will outshine all living beings. ^5-108

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-109]]

Just as a single precious lapis lazuli gem outshines a massive heap of ordinary glass trinkets,
So too does the rejoicing of a bodhisattva outshine all the vast heaps of generosity of all beings. ^5-109

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-110]]

If a bodhisattva, having given a gift to living beings,
Does not regard it as their own and has no attachment to the object,
From that, the roots of virtue will grow to have great power,
Like the bright orb of the waxing moon in a cloudless sky. ^5-110

## Teaching of the gradual path ^6-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-1]]

The sixth chapter explains the gradual path.
Through the generosity of a bodhisattva, the path to the realm of hungry spirits is cut off;
It likewise eliminates poverty and all afflictions.
When practiced, one will obtain an infinite expansion of resources;
Through generosity, one brings sentient beings who are suffering to full maturity. ^6-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-2]]

Through ethical discipline, one abandons the various states of animal existence,
And by abandoning the eight unfree states, one always finds leisure.
Through patience, one will attain a sublime, vast, and beautiful form;
With a lovely golden complexion, one becomes worthy of being beheld by all beings. ^6-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-3]]

Through diligence, virtuous qualities will not decline;
One will find the treasury of the Victors, which is infinite primordial wisdom.
Through meditative concentration, one abandons the base qualities of sensual desire;
One fully accomplishes knowledge, supernormal powers, and meditative absorption. ^6-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-4]]

Through wisdom, one thoroughly understands the nature of phenomena,
And will perfectly transcend the three realms without exception.
Having turned the precious wheel, that preeminent leader among men
Teaches the Dharma to beings in order to bring suffering to an end. ^6-4

## Teaching of the Dharmakāya ^8-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-1]]

The eighth chapter: teaching the Dharmakāya.
One fully embraces the pure realm and the purity of sentient beings;
One fully embraces the lineage of the Buddha and the lineage of the Dharma;
Likewise, one fully embraces the lineage of the Saṅgha and all phenomena. ^8-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-2]]

As the supreme physician who heals the ailments of wandering beings,
He has spoken this teaching on wisdom, which possesses the path to enlightenment;
This path to awakening is titled "The Collection of Precious Qualities,"
And is taught so that all sentient beings may attain it. ^8-2
