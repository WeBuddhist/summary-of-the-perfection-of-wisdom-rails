Then, in order to perfectly delight those four assemblies, the Blessed One once again taught this perfection of wisdom and at that time spoke these verses: ^0-3

## Teaching the omniscience of all aspects. ^1-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-1]]

Chapter one: Teaching the omniscience of all aspects.
However many rivers flow here in Jambudvīpa,
Producing flowers, fruits, medicinal herbs, and forests,
They all depend on the nāga king, the lord of nāgas who dwells in Anavatapta;
That is the majestic power of that lord of nāgas. ^1-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-2]]

Whatever Dharma the śrāvakas of the Victor teach,
Explain, and express endowed with reasoning,
As well as producing supreme noble bliss and attaining its fruit—
All of that is the power of the person of the Tathāgata. ^1-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-3]]

Why is this? Because whatever ways of Dharma the Victor has taught,
Those who have become disciples of the supreme human fully train in them,
And having actualized them, they teach exactly as they have trained;
They do so by the power of the Buddha, not by the power of their own strength. ^1-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-4]]

In whom the supreme perfection of wisdom is not apprehended,
And the bodhisattva is not apprehended, nor the mind of awakening—
Having heard this, without bewilderment and without fear,
That bodhisattva practices the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^1-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-5]]

There is no form, no feeling, no perception, and no volition;
For that one, there is not even an atom of a dwelling place for consciousness.
That one does not dwell on any phenomena, but practices without a dwelling place;
Without grasping, that one attains the awakening of the Sugatas. ^1-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-6]]

Just as the wandering mendicant Śreṇika, through his knowledge,
Brought about the destruction of the unapprehendable aggregates,
Any bodhisattva who understands phenomena in that way
Does not touch nirvāṇa, and abides in wisdom. ^1-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-7]]

Furthermore, examining, "What is this wisdom, and whose is it?
From where does it come?" they consider all these phenomena to be empty;
Having closely examined them, without discouragement or fear—
That bodhisattva is close to awakening. ^1-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-8]]

If, lacking understanding, one perceives form,
And engages with the aggregates of feeling, volition, and consciousness,
Even if they conceptualize, "These aggregates are empty," that bodhisattva
Practices with signs and lacks faith in the unborn state. ^1-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-9]]

Whoever does not practice in form, feeling, perception, or volition,
And does not practice in consciousness, practices without dwelling;
Not apprehending what is called "practicing," they rely on wisdom;
Endowed with the intelligence of the unborn, they touch the supreme, peaceful samādhi. ^1-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-10]]

Whatever bodhisattva abides here thus, at peace within themselves,
Has received the prophecy from the Tathāgatas of the past;
They have no conceit, thinking, "I am in meditative equipoise," or "I have arisen."
Why is this? Because they completely understand the nature of phenomena. ^1-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-11]]

Practicing in this way, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas,
Because they thoroughly understand that non-practice is the practice;
Whatever phenomena they practice are also not apprehended:
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-12]]

That which does not exist is called "non-existent";
Foolish beings conceptualize this, conceiving of existence and non-existence.
Existence and non-existence, these two are non-existent phenomena;
Any bodhisattva who knows this will definitely emerge. ^1-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-13]]

Whoever here knows the five aggregates to be like an illusion,
And does not treat the illusion as one thing and the aggregates as another,
Free from diverse perceptions and practicing in perfect peace—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-14]]

One endowed with spiritual friends and endowed with special insight
Will never become afraid upon hearing the Mother of the Victors.
Whoever is endowed with bad friends and relies on others
Is like an unfired vessel that disintegrates when touched by water. ^1-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-15]]

If one asks, "Why is this one called a bodhisattva?"
Severing attachment, they desire the exhaustion of attachment to everything.
They touch the unattached awakening of the Victors;
Therefore, they obtain the name "bodhisattva." ^1-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-16]]

If one asks, "Why is this one called a great being?"
Because they become the supreme among the many multitudes of sentient beings,
And sever the great views of the realm of sentient beings;
Therefore, they are proclaimed to be a great being. ^1-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-17]]

With great giving, great intelligence, and great power,
They have entered the supreme Great Vehicle of the Victors;
Donning the great armor, they subdue the wiles of Māra;
For that reason, they are proclaimed to be great beings. ^1-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-18]]

For example, just as a magician conjures an emanation at a crossroads,
And cuts off the tens of millions of heads of a large crowd of people—
Whatever those to be killed are like, just so the bodhisattva
Fully knows all beings to be like emanations, and has no fear. ^1-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-19]]

Form and perception, feeling and volition,
And consciousness are not bound, not liberated, and do not exist.
Entering into awakening in this way without a discouraged mind,
This is the supreme armor of holy beings. ^1-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-20]]

Why is this called the Great Vehicle of Awakening?
It is that which, once mounted, brings all sentient beings beyond sorrow.
This vehicle is like space, a great immeasurable palace,
The supreme vehicle that brings about the manifest attainment of joy, happiness, and bliss. ^1-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-21]]

Whoever mounts it, going in any direction is not apprehended;
Though taught as going to nirvāṇa, no going is apprehended.
For example, just as a dead person [Mipham, Khenrab and the Sanskrit read: a fire that has gone out] has nowhere to go,
For this reason, that person is fully proclaimed as "nirvāṇa." ^1-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-22]]

The bodhisattva is not apprehended in the prior limit, the later limit,
Or the present; the three times are pure.
That bodhisattva is unconditioned and free from elaboration;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-23]]

Whenever a wise and knowing bodhisattva
Contemplates the unborn and practices in this way,
Generating great compassion without the perception of sentient beings,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-24]]

If one generates the perception of sentient beings and the perception of suffering,
Thinking, "I shall act for the benefit of beings and eliminate their suffering,"
One is a bodhisattva who conceptualizes self and sentient beings;
This is not the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-25]]

Knowing all sentient beings to be just as the self is,
Knowing all phenomena to be just as all sentient beings are,
Not conceptualizing either non-arising or arising—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-26]]

However many names of phenomena are proclaimed in the world,
Having abandoned arising and passing away regarding them all,
One attains the supreme, deathless primordial wisdom, beyond which there is no other;
Therefore, this is called the perfection of wisdom. ^1-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-27]]

Any bodhisattva who practices in this way without doubt
Should be known as endowed with wisdom and abiding in equality;
Fully understanding that phenomena are devoid of intrinsic nature,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-28]]

The second chapter, teaching the knowledge of all paths. ^1-28

## Teaching the knowledge of all paths. ^2-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-1]]

Whoever does not abide in form, does not abide in feeling,
Whoever does not abide in perception, does not abide in volition,
And whoever does not abide in consciousness, abides in the true nature of phenomena;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-2]]

One does not abide in the permanent and impermanent, happiness and suffering, pleasant and unpleasant,
Self and selflessness, suchness and likewise emptiness;
One does not abide in attaining the fruit, nor in the stage of an arhat,
And one does not abide in the stage of a pratyekabuddha, nor likewise the stage of a Buddha. ^2-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-3]]

Just as the Guide does not abide in the unconditioned realm,
Likewise, he does not abide in the conditioned either, but practices without abiding;
Thus the bodhisattva abides in a basis by abiding without abiding;
This abiding without abiding is what the Victor has taught as abiding. ^2-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-4]]

Those who think, "May I become a śrāvaka of the Sugata,"
Or wish to become a pratyekabuddha, and likewise a Dharma King, a Buddha,
Cannot attain them without relying on this patience,
Just as one sees no crossing to go from this shore to the other. ^2-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-5]]

The teaching of the Dharma, the listening, and whatever is taught,
The attainment of the fruit, the pratyekabuddha, and likewise the Protector of the World,
And whatever nirvāṇa is attained by the wise and lucid—
The Tathāgata has taught that all these are like an illusion. ^2-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-6]]

These four persons are not afraid of that:
The child of the Victors skilled in the truth, and the irreversible one,
The arhat who has cleared away stains and abandoned doubt,
And fourth, the one fully embraced by a spiritual friend. ^2-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-7]]

The wise and learned bodhisattva who practises in this way
Does not train in the stage of an arhat, nor in the stage of a pratyekabuddha;
For the sake of that omniscience, they train following the qualities of a Buddha;
Whoever does not train in training and non-training, that is training. ^2-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-8]]

It is not for the sake of the increase, complete decrease, or complete apprehension of form;
Not training for the sake of completely apprehending various phenomena,
Yet training and completely apprehending omniscience itself—
Whatever definite emergence this is, it is the training of those who delight in qualities. ^2-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-9]]

Form is not wisdom, there is no wisdom in form;
Consciousness, perception, feeling, and volition—
These are not wisdom, nor is there wisdom in them;
This is equal to the element of space, without difference. ^2-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-10]]

The nature of apprehensions is boundless;
Whatever the nature of sentient beings is, that too is boundless;
The nature of the element of space is also boundless;
The wisdom of the Knower of the World is also boundless. ^2-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-11]]

The Guide has fully proclaimed that perception is the near shore;
Dismantling and abandoning perception, one goes to the far shore;
Those who attain this freedom from perception
Have reached the far shore and abide in the word of the Teacher. ^2-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-12]]

Even if the Teacher were to remain for as many eons
As there are grains of sand, and fully proclaim the word "sentient beings,"
Since they are pure from the beginning, how could sentient beings arise?
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-13]]

When I spoke in conformity with this supreme perfection,
At that time the supreme being of the past
Said, "In a future time you will become a Buddha."
"I received the prophecy"—thus the Victor spoke. ^2-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-14]]

This perfection of wisdom in which the Protectors practice—
Whoever pays respect, retains, and fully understands its meaning,
Will not be harmed by poison, weapons, fire, or water,
And Māra and the factions of Māra will find no opportunity against them. ^2-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-15]]

If someone were to build stūpas of the seven precious things
For a Sugata who has passed into parinirvāṇa and make offerings,
And with those stūpas of the Sugata, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges,
Were to fill thousands of millions of fields, ^2-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-16]]

By way of supposition, if in infinite millions of fields
All the sentient beings, however many reside there,
Doing no other work, with divine flowers, supreme incense, and
Ointments, were to make offerings at the three times of each day, for an eon or more— ^2-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-17]]

Whoever, since from it the ten powers of the Guides will arise,
Writes this Mother of the Sugatas in a book,
Retains it, and pays reverence with flowers and ointments,
The merit of having built and made offerings to those stūpas does not match a fraction of it. ^2-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-18]]

This perfection of wisdom of the Victors is a great mantra of knowledge;
It pacifies the dharmas of sorrow and suffering of the many realms of sentient beings.
Whichever Protectors of the World are in the past, and whichever are in the ten directions,
Having trained in this mantra of knowledge, they became unsurpassed kings of physicians. ^2-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-19]]

Those who practice the conduct endowed with benefit and compassion,
The wise, having trained in this mantra of knowledge, touch awakening;
Whatever is conditioned happiness and unconditioned happiness,
One should know that all that happiness arises from this. ^2-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-20]]

Seeds, having been sown and resting in the earth, will emerge;
Having found the assembly of conditions, many diverse forms are fully produced;
The five perfections and however many qualities of awakening there are,
All of those too are born from the perfection of wisdom. ^2-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-21]]

Whichever path a universal monarch always travels,
On that very path, the seven precious things and all the military forces go;
Wherever this perfection of wisdom of the Victors goes,
Along that very path, all the qualities will go. ^2-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-22]]

Śakra, having been fully asked a question by the Victor, replied:
Even if there were Buddha-fields as numerous as the sands of the Ganges,
And all of those were filled to the brim with the relics of the Victors,
I would take this very perfection of wisdom. ^2-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-23]]

If asked why, it is not that I lack respect for the bodily relics;
However, because they are fully permeated by wisdom, they are worshipped.
For example, just as a person relying on a king obtains reverence,
The relics of a Buddha rely on the perfection of wisdom. ^2-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-24]]

A precious jewel endowed with all qualities is priceless;
Whatever casket it is placed in is worthy of homage;
Even if it is taken out, one takes joy in the casket,
For those qualities belong to that precious jewel. ^2-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-25]]

Likewise, through the qualities of the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Even when the Victors have passed into nirvāṇa, their relics receive worship.
Therefore, whoever wishes to hold the qualities of the Victors
Should take up this perfection of wisdom; this is liberation. ^2-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-26]]

Wisdom is what precedes the giving of generosity;
Ethical discipline, patience, diligence, and meditative concentration are likewise.
It fully upholds them so that virtuous qualities are not wasted;
This demonstrates all phenomena to have a single way. ^2-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-27]]

For example, in Jambudvīpa there are billions of trees,
Of various kinds, numerous types, and many forms;
Except for being spoken of as a number of shadows,
There are no diverse shadows, nor are there differences. ^2-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-28]]

Likewise, these five perfections of the Victors also
Attain the very name of the perfection of wisdom.
When dedicated for the sake of omniscience,
All these six become of one taste, called awakening. ^2-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-29]]

If a bodhisattva, while lacking full understanding,
Fully demonstrates form, perception, feeling, volition, and consciousness
As impermanent, they act upon a fabrication;
The wise never destroy phenomena. ^2-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-30]]

Where one does not take anything as form or feeling, and perception is not apprehended,
Nor is consciousness taken as such, and volition is not apprehended,
And all phenomena are fully known as unborn and empty—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-31]]

Compared to someone who, in fields as numerous as the sands of the Ganges,
Tames all the sentient beings residing there to the state of an arhat,
Whoever writes down this perfection of wisdom
And gives the text to a supreme sentient being has far more exalted merit. ^2-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-32]]

Why is that? Those supreme speakers, having trained in this,
Demonstrate here that all phenomena are emptiness.
Hearing this, śrāvakas swiftly reach liberation,
And one reaches the awakening of a pratyekabuddha and the awakening of a Buddha. ^2-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-33]]

If, without a sprout, a tree never emerges in the world,
How could branches, leaves, flowers, and fruits emerge on it?
If, without the mind of awakening, a Victor does not emerge in the world,
How could the fruits—Śakra, Brahmā, and śrāvakas—emerge? ^2-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-34]]

When the sun's disk radiates its web of light,
At that time sentient beings exert themselves in their work; likewise,
When the wise give rise in the world to the mind of awakening in order to know,
Through primordial wisdom, sentient beings become endowed with qualities. ^2-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-35]]

For example, if the lord of nāgas were not at Lake Anavatapta,
How could rivers flow here in Jambudvīpa?
If there were no rivers, flowers and fruits would not emerge,
And in the ocean, too, there would not be many forms of jewels. ^2-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-36]]

Likewise, here too, without the mind of awakening, how could
The primordial wisdom of the Sugata emerge in all these worlds?
Without primordial wisdom, there is no increase of qualities and no awakening,
And the ocean-like qualities of a Buddha would also not exist. ^2-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-37]]

However many illuminating creatures there are in this world,
Compared to the light emitted by all of them in order to illuminate,
A single ray of light emitted by the sun's disk is far superior;
All the lights of the host of illuminating ones cannot match a fraction of it. ^2-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-38]]

However much the hosts of śrāvakas, through generosity, ethical discipline,
And meditation, generate a fully endowed mass of merit,
Compared to a bodhisattva's single mind of rejoicing,
The heap of merit of the śrāvaka hosts cannot match a fraction. ^2-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-39]]

Whatever tens of millions of hundreds of billions of Buddhas of the past,
And those who abide in infinitely many thousands of tens of millions of fields,
And whatever Protectors of the World have passed into nirvāṇa,
Who teach the jewel Dharma in order to exhaust suffering— ^2-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-40]]

From when they first generated the mind for supreme awakening,
Until the time the true Dharma of those Guides is exhausted,
Whatever merit those Victors possess during that interval,
And whatever is endowed with the perfections, and whatever qualities of a Buddha— ^2-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-41]]

And whatever is of the sons of the Buddhas and the śrāvakas,
The contaminated and uncontaminated virtues of ones in training and ones beyond training,
Having gathered them, the bodhisattva rejoices,
And dedicates them all for awakening as a cause for the benefit of beings. ^2-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-42]]

If, when dedicating, the perception of a mind arises,
And if there is the perception of awakening and the perception of sentient beings,
Because of these perceptions, dwelling in views, the mind is attached to the three;
Since there is apprehension, it does not amount to being dedicated. ^2-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-43]]

If one knows in this way that these phenomena cease and are exhausted,
And that to which one dedicates is also exhausted,
And that a phenomenon never dedicates to a phenomenon,
By knowing perfectly in that way, it is dedicated. ^2-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-44]]

If one makes a sign of it, that is not dedicating;
But if there are no signs, it is the dedication to awakening.
Just like eating excellent food that is mixed with poison,
The Victor has taught that the apprehension of virtuous qualities is also like that. ^2-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-45]]

Therefore, one should train in dedicating in this way:
Just as those Victors know that virtue—
Whatever its aspect, whatever its origin, whatever its characteristic—
Rejoicing in it, one dedicates it in exactly that way. ^2-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-46]]

If one dedicates merit to awakening in that way,
It is without poison, one does not abandon the Buddha, and one speaks what the Victor has spoken.
The hero dedicating in that way outshines
All the however many bodhisattvas in the world who are ones with apprehension. ^2-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-47]]

Tens of millions of hundreds of billions of blind people without a guide,
Not even knowing the path, how could they enter the city?
Without wisdom, these five perfections are eyeless;
Being without a guide, they are unable to reach awakening. ^2-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-48]]

When they are perfectly embraced by wisdom,
They then gain eyes and obtain this name.
For example, just as a completed painting that lacks eyes
Earns no payment as long as the eyes are not painted. ^2-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-49]]

When conditioned, unconditioned, and white and black phenomena
Are broken down by wisdom and not even a particle is apprehended,
In the world it is counted as the perfection of wisdom;
It is like space, which does not abide in anything at all. ^2-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-50]]

If one thinks, "I shall practice the wisdom of the Victors,
And liberate many billions of sentient beings touched by suffering,"
Such a bodhisattva seeking awakening conceptually constructs the perception of sentient beings;
This is not practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-51]]

Any bodhisattva who, when practicing in the past,
Skillfully practiced this perfection and was without doubt,
Will, upon hearing it, immediately give rise to the perception of the Teacher;
They will swiftly realize peaceful awakening. ^2-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-52]]

Even if one honored many billions of Buddhas when practicing in the past,
If one does not have faith in the Victors' perfection of wisdom,
Upon hearing it, that person of small intelligence will abandon it;
Having abandoned it, they will become without refuge and go to the Avīci hell. ^2-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-53]]

Therefore, if you wish to attain the supreme primordial wisdom of a Buddha,
Generate faith in this Mother of the Victors!
For example, it is unfitting to become like a merchant
Who, having gone to an island of jewels, returns with exhausted goods. ^2-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-54]]

The purity of form should be known as the purity of the fruit;
The purity of the fruit and form becomes the purity of omniscience.
The purity of omniscience, the purity of the fruit, and the purity of form
Are equivalent to the element of space, indivisible and not to be cut apart. ^2-54

## Showing the knowledge of all bases. ^3-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-1]]

Chapter three: showing the knowledge of all bases.
Through the perfection of wisdom, wherever the hero acts,
They completely transcend the three realms, yet do not enter liberation;
Although afflictions are cleared away, they display birth;
Although without aging, sickness, and death, they display passing away. ^3-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-2]]

These beings are attached to the mud of name and form;
They wander in the wheel of saṃsāra, like a wheel of wind.
Knowing that deluded beings are like deer caught in a snare,
Those endowed with wisdom roam like birds in the sky. ^3-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-3]]

Whoever with completely pure conduct does not engage in form,
And does not engage in consciousness, perception, feeling, and volition,
Practising in that way, they completely abandon all attachment,
And, finding liberation from attachment, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^3-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-4]]

The wise and luminous bodhisattva practising in that way,
Having severed attachment, moves among beings without attachment,
Like the sun, freed from the eclipse of Rāhu, shining brightly,
And like a blazing fire that burns grass, wood, and forests. ^3-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-5]]

All phenomena being naturally pure and completely pure,
When the bodhisattva looks through the perfection of wisdom,
They do not apprehend an agent, nor do they apprehend all phenomena;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^3-5

## Teaching the complete realization of all aspects. ^4-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-1]]

Chapter Four: Teaching the complete realization of all aspects.
Śakra, king of the gods, asked the Victor:
"How does a bodhisattva strive in the practice of wisdom?"
"They do not strive even a particle in the aggregates and elements;
Not striving in the aggregates—that is the striving of a bodhisattva." ^4-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-2]]

Whoever, having heard that these phenomena are like emanations and illusions,
Has no doubt and continually applies themself to the training,
That sentient being should be known to have entered the Great Vehicle long ago,
And to have served many tens of billions of Buddhas. ^4-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-3]]

When a person who has set out on a wilderness path of many yojanas
Sees the excellent forests at the cowherds' boundary,
They think, "These are signs that a village or city is near,"
And they find relief, with no fear of robbers. ^4-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-4]]

Likewise, when thoroughly seeking awakening, whoever comes to hear
This perfection of wisdom of the Victors
Attains relief and becomes fearless;
They are not on the stage of an arhat, nor the stage of a pratyekabuddha. ^4-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-5]]

When a person traveling to see the water of the ocean
Sees trees, forests, and mountains, it is still far away;
But when they do not see these signs, they think, "The great ocean
Is near," and they have no doubt about it. ^4-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-6]]

Likewise, those fully entered into supreme awakening
Should be known as hearing this perfection of wisdom of the Victors;
Even if they have not received a direct prophecy from the Guide,
They know: "Before long, I will touch the awakening of a Buddha." ^4-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-7]]

In the fine springtime, when leaves have fallen from the trees,
Before long, the branches produce leaves, fruits, and flowers;
Whoever attains this perfection of wisdom in their hands
Will attain the awakening of the Guides before long. ^4-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-8]]

For example, when a pregnant woman is afflicted with pain,
It is said that the time for her to give birth has arrived;
Likewise, when a bodhisattva hears the wisdom of the Victors
And generates joy and aspiration, they will swiftly touch awakening. ^4-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-9]]

When practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom, a yogin
Does not see the increase or the complete decrease of form;
Whoever does not see phenomena, non-phenomena, or the dharmadhātu,
And does not touch nirvāṇa, that one abides in wisdom. ^4-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-10]]

Whoever practices here does not conceptualize the qualities of a Buddha;
They do not conceptualize the powers, bases of miraculous power, or peaceful awakening;
Non-conceptual and free from concepts, they practice through blessing;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^4-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-11]]

Subhūti asked the Buddha, the Moon of Speakers:
"What are the obstacles for those who delight in qualities?"
The Teacher replied, "There will be many obstacles;
Of those, I will fully explain just a few for now." ^4-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-12]]

When writing down this perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
Various different modes of eloquence will arise;
And without having accomplished the benefit of beings, as swiftly as a flash of lightning,
They fall away completely; this is the work of Māra. ^4-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-13]]

When this is explained, some will harbor doubts, thinking:
"Here the Guide has not even fully pronounced my name,
Nor fully pronounced my family, birthplace, or clan."
They will not listen, but abandon it; this is the work of Māra. ^4-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-14]]

Not knowing this, having discarded the root,
In their delusion, they will search for branches and leaves.
It is like finding an elephant and then searching for its footprints;
Having heard the perfection of wisdom, searching for sūtras is exactly like that. ^4-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-15]]

For example, just as someone who has found food with a hundred flavors,
Having found supreme food, searches for inferior food;
A bodhisattva who, having found this perfection,
Seeks awakening through the stage of an arhat, is exactly like that. ^4-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-16]]

They will desire honor and desire gain,
And with a mind of longing to look, they will become intimate with householders;
Having abandoned the Dharma, they will engage in non-Dharma activities;
Discarding the path and going on a wrong path, this is the work of Māra. ^4-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-17]]

Those who at that time, having become eager and faithful,
Go to listen to this sublime Dharma,
Will, upon knowing that the Dharma preacher is preoccupied with activities,
Depart, devoid of joy and unhappy. ^4-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-18]]

At that time, this work of Māra will arise:
At that time, those by whom many monks are agitated,
So that they do not uphold this perfection of wisdom—
Many other various obstacles will also arise. ^4-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-19]]

Whatever priceless jewels may be found,
They are rare and always subject to much harm.
Likewise, the supreme perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
This precious Dharma is also rare and always subject to much harm. ^4-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-20]]

Sentient beings of small intellect who have newly entered the vehicle,
Whoever has not found this rare jewel—
In order to create obstacles for them, Māra will become energetic;
But the Buddhas of the ten directions apply themselves to fully upholding them. ^4-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-21]]

When a mother who has many children falls ill,
They all become distressed and attend upon her.
Likewise, the Buddhas of the world systems in the ten directions also
Keep in mind this holy wisdom, who has become their Mother. ^4-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-22]]

Whichever Protectors of the World have passed and are in the ten directions,
And those who will appear in future times—they arise from this.
She is the Mother who gives birth to the Victors, the Teachers of the World,
And she reveals the mental conduct of other sentient beings. ^4-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-23]]

The suchness of the world and the suchness of an arhat,
The suchness of a pratyekabuddha and the suchness of the children of the Victors,
Are one itself, devoid of entities, not other—they are suchness;
The Tathāgata knows the perfection of wisdom. ^4-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-24]]

Whether the wise remain in the world or pass into nirvāṇa,
This flawless true nature of phenomena, the emptiness of phenomena, remains;
Bodhisattvas realize this suchness,
And therefore Buddhas are given the name Tathāgata. ^4-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-25]]

Relying on the perfection of wisdom as a pleasure grove,
The Guides with the ten powers have this as their domain;
Though they completely deliver sentient beings from suffering and the three lower realms,
They never have the perception of sentient beings. ^4-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-26]]

For example, just as a lion relying fearlessly on a mountain glen
Terrifies many small wild animals and lets out a roar,
The Lion among Men, relying on the perfection of wisdom,
Terrifies many non-Buddhists and also lets out a roar in the world. ^4-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-27]]

For example, just as the rays of the sun abiding in the sky
Dry up this earth and perfectly reveal forms,
Likewise, the Dharma King, relying on the perfection of wisdom,
Dries up the river of craving and perfectly teaches the Dharma. ^4-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-28]]

One does not see forms, and one also does not see feelings,
One does not see perceptions, and one does not see volitions,
That in which consciousness, mind, and intellect are not seen—
The Tathāgata taught that this is seeing the Dharma. ^4-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-29]]

Sentient beings express in words, "I see space,"
But how is space seen? Examine the meaning of this.
The Tathāgata taught that the seeing of phenomena is also like that;
This seeing cannot be described by any other example. ^4-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-30]]

Whoever sees in that way sees all phenomena.
Just as a king sits in equanimity while his ministers do everything,
Whatever deeds of the Buddhas and phenomena of the śrāvakas there are,
The perfection of wisdom does them all. ^4-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-31]]

Just as a king does not go to villages or provinces,
Yet gathers all revenue from his own realm,
The true nature of phenomena of a bodhisattva does not go anywhere,
Yet gathers all the qualities of the stage of a Buddha. ^4-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-32]]

Whoever has firm faith in the Sugatas and bodhisattvas,
And has intention and application toward the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Having completely transcended the two stages of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
Will swiftly attain the unassailable awakening of the Victors. ^4-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-33]]

For example, when a boat entering the ocean breaks apart,
Whoever does not hold onto a corpse, grass, or wood,
Will perish in the middle of the water and will not reach the shore;
But whoever holds onto them will reach the far side of the ocean. ^4-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-34]]

Likewise, whoever is endowed with faith and has obtained confidence,
But does not follow the Mother, the perfection of wisdom, ^4-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-35]]

If their qualities of skillful means are fully embraced by wisdom,
They will swiftly reach the supremely wondrous awakening of the Sugatas. ^4-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-36]]

For example, if someone carries water in an unfired clay pot,
One should know that, being fragile, it will quickly break.
When carrying water in a fired pot, there is no fear of it breaking on the way,
And, without breaking, it will safely arrive home. ^4-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-37]]

Likewise, even if a bodhisattva has much faith,
If they are bereft of wisdom, they will quickly fall to ruin.
If their faith is fully embraced by wisdom,
Having entirely transcended the two stages, they will attain supreme awakening. ^4-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-38]]

For example, a boat that is not well constructed in the ocean
Will be destroyed along with its wealth and its merchants.
If that boat has been well constructed,
It will not be destroyed and will reach the shore along with its wealth. ^4-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-39]]

Likewise, even if a bodhisattva is fully imbued with faith,
If they lack wisdom, they will quickly fall away from awakening.
If they possess the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Unharmed and undiminished, they will touch the awakening of the Victors. ^4-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-40]]

A person who is a hundred and twenty years old, aged and suffering,
Even if they stand up, is unable to walk on their own.
If they are supported by people on both the left and the right,
They will proceed with ease, without the fear of falling. ^4-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-41]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva whose power of wisdom is weak,
Even if they set out, will completely fall away.
If they are fully embraced by skillful means and supreme wisdom,
They will not fall away, and will touch the awakening of a Tathāgata. ^4-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-42]]

Those bodhisattvas who abide on the stage of a beginner,
And have set out for the supreme awakening of a Buddha with a superior intention,
Those excellent students possessing devotion to their master
Should always rely upon wise masters. ^4-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-43]]

Why is that? The qualities of the wise arise from them;
They teach the perfection of wisdom.
"The qualities of a Buddha depend upon the spiritual friend"—
So spoke the Victor, who possesses the supreme of all qualities. ^4-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-44]]

"Dedicate generosity, ethical discipline, patience, and likewise diligence,
Meditative concentration, and wisdom to awakening!
Do not harbor clinging to the supreme by being attached to the aggregates of awakening!"—
Thus should it be taught to the beginner. ^4-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-45]]

Those who practice thus, who are oceans of goodness and moons among speakers,
Are the refuge, the defender, and the dwelling place of beings;
They are the support, the intellect, the island, and Guides seeking their welfare,
Illuminators, lamps, and imperturbable speakers of the supreme Dharma. ^4-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-46]]

Those of great renown don the armor of difficult deeds;
It is not the armor of the aggregates, elements, and sense bases;
They are free from the perception of the three vehicles and without grasping;
They are irreversible, unwavering, and of an imperturbable nature. ^4-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-47]]

They are endowed with such qualities and free from conceptual elaboration;
Free from doubt, hesitation, and uncertainty, they are endowed with the true meaning;
Having heard the perfection of wisdom, they do not shrink back;
They do not rely on others and should be known as irreversible. ^4-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-48]]

This Dharma of the Guides is profound and difficult to see;
It is not realized by anyone, nor is there any attaining of it.
Therefore, the Compassionate Benefactor, having attained awakening,
Felt little inclination, thinking, "Who among the masses of sentient beings will understand?" ^4-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-49]]

Sentient beings delight in dwelling places and desire sense objects;
Abiding in grasping, the unwise are foolish and deluded like darkness.
The Dharma to be attained is without dwelling place and without grasping;
Therefore, conflict with the world arises. ^4-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-50]]

The element of space in the eastern direction and the southern direction,
And likewise in the western direction and the northern direction, is boundless;
It exists above, below, and in as many of the ten directions as there are;
It is without differentiation and without distinction. ^4-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-51]]

Whatever is the suchness of the past, the suchness of the future,
Whatever is the suchness of the present, the suchness of the arhats,
Whatever is the suchness of all phenomena, the suchness of the Victors—
All this suchness of phenomena is without distinction. ^4-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-52]]

The awakening of the Sugatas is free from differentiated phenomena;
Any bodhisattva who desires to attain this,
Endowed with skillful means, must apply themselves to the perfection of wisdom;
Without the wisdom of the Guides, they will not attain it. ^4-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-53]]

If a bird with a massive body of one hundred and fifty yojanas,
Whose wings are exhausted, lacks strength and skill,
And leaps from the abode of the Thirty-Three to this Jambudvīpa,
It will surely meet with ruin there. ^4-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-54]]

Even if these five perfections of the Victors
Are accomplished for many tens of millions and hundreds of billions of eons,
And infinite, vast aspirations are constantly relied upon in the world,
Without skillful means and devoid of wisdom, one falls into the very state of a śrāvaka. ^4-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-55]]

Whoever wishes to definitely emerge in this Buddha vehicle,
With an even mind toward all beings and the perception of them as parents,
Should strive with a beneficial attitude and a mind of loving-kindness,
And, being without harshness and straightforward, speak gentle words. ^4-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-56]]

The elder Subhūti fully requested of the Protector of the World:
"Please teach the signs of those free from afflictions, an ocean of qualities,
And how those of great power will not turn back again;
Thus, may the Victor grant a prophecy of just a fraction of their qualities." ^4-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-57]]

Free from the perception of diversity, they speak words endowed with reason,
And do not rely on other śramaṇas and brahmins;
These wise ones have constantly abandoned the three lower realms at all times,
And they strive intensely on the path of the ten virtuous actions. ^4-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-58]]

Without material motives, they teach the Dharma to beings,
Taking singular delight in the Dharma and always endowed with gentle words;
They are fully endowed with alertness while going, walking, sleeping, and sitting,
And they walk looking ahead only the length of a yoke, their minds free from delusion. ^4-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-59]]

Making themselves clean and wearing clean clothes, the three are isolated and pure;
Without desire for gain, these great bulls always desire the Dharma;
Having passed beyond the realm of Māra, they do not depend on others;
Meditating on the four meditative concentrations, they do not abide in those meditative concentrations. ^4-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-60]]

They do not desire fame, and their minds are not entangled by anger.
Even if they become householders, they are always unattached to all things.
For the sake of livelihood, they do not seek resources by terrible means.
They do not practice mantras for fierce actions, nor mantras for bringing women under one's control. ^4-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-61]]

They do not prophesy whether beings with the karma of desire will become male or female.
Utterly secluded, devoted to the perfection of wisdom, they are free from conflict and dispute, and their mind of loving-kindness is firm.
Desiring omniscience, they always possess a mind inclined toward the teachings. ^4-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-62]]

They completely abandon the borderlands of barbarian peoples.
Without doubt about their own stage, they are always like Mount Meru.
They sacrifice even their lives for the sake of the Dharma and strive intensely in yoga.
One should know that these are the signs of the irreversible. ^4-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-63]]

Form, feeling, perception, volition, and consciousness
Are profound, by nature signless, and utterly peaceful.
Just as one might search for the bottom of the ocean with an arrow,
When they are examined with wisdom, the depth of the aggregates is not found. ^4-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-64]]

Any bodhisattva who has thus become profound,
And in the ultimate, unattached vehicle where the aggregates,
Elements, and sense bases do not exist, realizes this Dharma—
What perfectly accomplished merit is there beyond theirs? ^4-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-65]]

For example, a man who engages in the phenomena of desire,
Having made an appointment with a woman, failing to meet her—
However many conceptual thoughts his mind engages in for a single day,
The bodhisattva will obtain the merit accumulated over that many eons. ^4-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-66]]

Even if any bodhisattva, for many billions of eons,
Practices generosity toward arhats and pratyekabuddhas and maintains ethical discipline,
For whoever speaks the Dharma endowed with the supreme perfection of wisdom,
That generosity and ethical discipline do not match a fraction of the goodness of speaking it. ^4-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-67]]

Any bodhisattva who meditates on supreme wisdom,
And arising from that, teaches the untainted Dharma,
And dedicates that for the sake of beings as a cause for awakening—
There is no virtue equal to that in the three worlds. ^4-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-68]]

Knowing that merit, too, is hollow and likewise empty,
Insubstantial, baseless, and without essence,
If one thus engages in the wisdom practice of the Sugatas,
While engaging, one fully grasps immeasurable merit. ^4-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-69]]

Spoken by the Buddha, extensively set forth and clearly taught,
Knowing all these Dharmas to be nothing but mere expressions,
Even if one were to speak of them for many tens of millions and hundreds of billions of eons,
In the dharmadhātu, there is no exhaustion and no increase. ^4-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-70]]

Whatever is called the perfection of the Victors,
Those phenomena, too, are fully proclaimed to be mere names.
A bodhisattva who dedicates without conceit
Will not decline and will touch the supreme awakening of a Buddha. ^4-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-71]]

Just as, by the first contact of the flame with the wick of an oil lamp,
The wick is not consumed, yet without it, it is not consumed,
And by the final contact with the flame, the wick is not consumed,
Yet without the final flame, the wick is not consumed. ^4-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-72]]

By the first mind, supreme awakening is not touched,
Yet without it, one is not able to touch it.
By the final mind, peaceful awakening is not attained,
Yet without it, one is not able to attain it. ^4-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-73]]

From a seed, a sprout, a flower, and a fruit arise,
And when it ceases, it is not that the tree is absent—just as,
Likewise, the first mind is also the cause of awakening,
And when it ceases, it is not that awakening is absent. ^4-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-74]]

By drops of water, from the first to the last,
A vase is gradually, little by little, completely filled—just as,
Likewise, the first mind is also the cause of supreme awakening,
And by gradually perfecting virtuous qualities, one becomes a Buddha. ^4-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-75]]

Practicing the phenomena of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness,
They do not touch nirvāṇa and do not engage in signs—
Just as a skilled boatman, though going back and forth,
Dwells on neither shore, nor does he dwell in the ocean. ^4-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-76]]

A bodhisattva practicing in this way has no conceit, thinking, "I
Will receive a prophecy from the ten powers and will touch awakening,"
Nor do they fear awakening, thinking, "There is nothing at all here";
To practice in this way is to practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^4-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-77]]

Seeing the wilderness paths of the world, along with famine and disease,
They are without fear and then put on their armor;
Always striving until the final limit of the future and fully understanding,
They do not generate even a speck of a weary mind. ^4-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-78]]

The bodhisattva who practises the wisdom of the Victors
Knows these aggregates to be empty and unborn from the beginning.
While not in meditative equipoise, they engage in compassion for the realm of sentient beings,
And even in the meantime, they do not decline from the qualities of a Buddha. ^4-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-79]]

For example, a skilled person endowed with all qualities,
Who is powerful, invincible, diligent, and knows the methods of the arts,
Who has mastered archery and many fields of craft,
And who knows how to conjure illusions and greatly desires the welfare of beings— ^4-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-80]]

Having fully gathered his father, mother, and wife,
He sets out upon a wilderness path where many enemies dwell;
And emanating many brave and courageous men,
He travels safely and returns home once again—just as, ^4-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-81]]

Likewise, at that time, the skilled bodhisattva also
Generates great compassion for all realms of sentient beings,
And having utterly passed beyond the four Māras and the two levels,
Abides in the supreme samādhi and does not touch awakening. ^4-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-82]]

Wind is supported on space, and on that is supported the mass of water;
On that is supported this great earth, and on that are supported beings.
Such is the cause for sentient beings engaging in their karma.
Reflect on this meaning: upon what does space abide? ^4-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-83]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva who abides in emptiness,
Supported by aspirations that know sentient beings among beings,
Demonstrates many various kinds of activities,
Does not touch nirvāṇa, and does not abide in emptiness. ^4-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-84]]

Whenever a skilled and brilliant bodhisattva
Practices this supreme samādhi of peaceful emptiness,
During that time they do not cultivate signs;
Dwelling in signlessness, they practice peace, utter peace. ^4-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-85]]

For a bird flying in the sky, there is no place to abide;
It does not abide there, nor does it fall to the ground.
Likewise, a bodhisattva who practices in the doors of liberation
Does not touch nirvāṇa, nor do they engage with signs. ^4-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-86]]

For example, a man trained in archery shoots an arrow into the sky,
And by continuously following it with other arrows in succession,
He leaves no opportunity for the previous arrow to fall to the ground;
Should that person wish it, that arrow will fall to the ground. ^4-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-87]]

Likewise, those who practice the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Acting through wisdom, skillful means, strength, and miraculous powers,
As long as their roots of virtue are not complete,
For that long, they do not attain that supreme emptiness. ^4-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-88]]

For example, a monk endowed with the supreme power of miracles
Abides in the sky and simultaneously emanates a miraculous display,
Demonstrating walking, pacing, lying down, and sitting;
He feels no weariness, and he has no fatigue. ^4-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-89]]

Likewise, a skilled bodhisattva dwelling in emptiness,
Having perfected primordial wisdom and miraculous powers, abides nowhere;
They display an infinite variety of activities to beings,
And for ten million eons, they feel no weariness and have no fatigue. ^4-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-90]]

For example, just as someone standing at a great precipice
Holds two parasols in both hands and leaps into the sky,
Casting their body into the great abyss,
They do not fall, but travel for as far as it may be. ^4-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-91]]

Likewise, the wise bodhisattva, abiding in compassion,
Fully holds the two parasols of skillful means and wisdom;
Contemplating phenomena as empty, as signlessness, and as wishless,
They do not touch nirvāṇa, yet they see phenomena. ^4-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-92]]

For example, just as someone desiring jewels goes to a jewel island,
And, having found jewels, returns home again,
That merchant does not live happily all alone
While leaving their host of family and friends unhappy. ^4-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-93]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva who has gone to the jewel island of emptiness
And attained meditative concentration, faculties, and powers,
Does not delight merely in touching nirvāṇa alone
While leaving sentient beings in suffering. ^4-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-94]]

For example, a merchant seeking profit, in order to know the route,
Goes to whatever intervening cities, towns, and villages there are;
He does not dwell there, nor does he dwell on the jewel island,
And the wise one does not dwell at home, but becomes skilled in the path. ^4-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-95]]

Likewise, the brilliant bodhisattva is skilled in all
The knowledge and liberation of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas;
They do not dwell in them, nor do they dwell in the primordial wisdom of a Buddha,
And they do not dwell in the unconditioned, but know the ways of the path. ^4-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-96]]

Whenever, having connected with beings through loving-kindness,
They practice the samādhis of emptiness, signlessness, and the wishless,
Could it be designated that they will attain nirvāṇa,
Or that they are conditioned? That is impossible. ^4-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-97]]

For example, just as an emanated human body is not invisible,
And can even be designated by a name,
Likewise, a bodhisattva who practices the doors of liberation
Can even be designated by a name. ^4-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-98]]

If, when thoroughly questioned about their conduct and faculties,
A bodhisattva does not fully teach the Dharma of emptiness and signlessness,
And does not explain the Dharma of the irreversible stage,
They should be known as unprophesied. ^4-98

## Showing the complete realization of the peak ^5-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-1]]

Chapter Five shows the complete realization of the peak:
The stage of an arhat, the primordial wisdom of a pratyekabuddha,
And the three realms, they do not desire even in a dream;
Seeing the Buddhas teaching the Dharma to beings,
This should be known as the prophecy of irreversibility. ^5-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-2]]

Seeing in a dream sentient beings dwelling in the three lower realms,
In that instant they make an aspiration to cut the continuum of the lower realms,
And they pacify a mass of fire through the blessing of truth;
This should be known as the prophecy of irreversibility. ^5-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-3]]

The many harmful spirits and diseases in the human world
Are pacified through the blessing of truth by those endowed with benefit and compassion;
Yet they remain without conceit and do not give rise to pride—
They should be understood as having the prophecy of irreversibility. ^5-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-4]]

However, if those various blessings of truth
Are accomplished, and conceit arises thinking, "I have a prophecy,"
Or if they become conceited at being prophesied by other bodhisattvas,
Those abiding in conceit should be known as having small intelligence. ^5-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-5]]

When Māra approaches on the basis of names,
He speaks thus: "These are the names of you and your parents,
And of your lineage back to the seventh generation of ancestors;
And when you become a Buddha, this will be your name." ^5-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-6]]

"Whatever ascetic practices and yoga you will come to possess,
He declares, 'Your former qualities and conduct were also like this.'
Any bodhisattva who hears such words and becomes conceited
Should be known as fully incited by Māra and having small intelligence. ^5-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-7]]

Though they thoroughly rely upon very isolated villages, cities, and mountain valleys,
And upon forests, isolated places, and woodland retreats,
A bodhisattva who praises themselves and deprecates others
Should be known as fully incited by Māra and having small intelligence. ^5-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-8]]

Constantly dwelling in villages, provinces, and towns,
Except for striving for awakening and the ripening of sentient beings,
They do not generate other desires for the state of an arhat or pratyekabuddha;
This is taught as the solitude of the sons of the Sugata. ^5-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-9]]

Whoever, in a mountain cleft of five hundred yojanas,
Filled with snakes, abides for many tens of millions of years,
That bodhisattva who does not know this solitude
Acquires superior pride and abides in mixture. ^5-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-10]]

Whoever despises a bodhisattva who strives for the benefit of beings
And has attained meditative concentration, powers, faculties, liberations, and samādhi,
Thinking, "This one does not practice forest solitude,"
The Victor has declared that they abide in the domain of Māra. ^5-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-11]]

Whether one abides in a village or else in a forest,
If one is free from the mind of the two vehicles and certain of supreme awakening,
This is the solitude of those engaged in the welfare of beings;
A bodhisattva who measures them ruins themselves. ^5-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-12]]

Therefore, a wise one who seeks supreme awakening
And possesses strong intention, having definitely destroyed pride,
Just as a multitude of sick people rely on a doctor to be cured,
Should rely on a spiritual friend without negligence. ^5-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-13]]

Buddhas and bodhisattvas entered into supreme awakening,
Along with the perfections, should be relied upon as spiritual friends.
They are the instructors, and this is the stage of accomplishment;
By these two causes, the awakening of a Buddha is swiftly realized. ^5-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-14]]

Victors of the past, the future, and those abiding in the ten directions—
The path of them all is this perfection and no other.
For those entered into supreme awakening, this perfection is
Explained as the illumination, lamp, light, and supreme teacher. ^5-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-15]]

Just as the perfection of wisdom has emptiness as its characteristic,
Knowing the characteristics of all these phenomena to be similar to that,
If one thoroughly knows phenomena to be empty and signless,
Practising in that way is the wisdom practice of the Sugatas. ^5-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-16]]

Sentient beings, fully conceptualizing, desire food,
And those with minds attached to saṃsāra wander perpetually.
Both self and the phenomena of self are unreal and empty,
Yet the foolish themselves tie knots in the sky. ^5-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-17]]

For example, just as someone who, through a suspicious perception, thinks they have taken poison,
Even if that poison has not entered inside, they will faint;
Likewise, the foolish, asserting self and what belongs to self,
Through the unreal perception of that self, are continually born and die in impermanence. ^5-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-18]]

Just as there is grasping, so is complete affliction taught;
Not apprehending self and what belongs to self is said to be complete purification;
In this, there is nothing that becomes afflicted or completely pure,
And the bodhisattva realizes the perfection of wisdom. ^5-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-19]]

However many sentient beings there are in Jambudvīpa without exception,
Even if all of them were to generate the mind of supreme awakening,
And practise generosity for many tens of millions of millennia,
And dedicate it all as a cause for awakening for the benefit of beings, ^5-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-20]]

Whoever diligently applies themselves to the supreme perfection of wisdom,
And acts in accordance with it for even a single day,
That aggregate of generosity cannot match a fraction of that merit;
Therefore, one should always engage in wisdom without negligence. ^5-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-21]]

The yogin who practices the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Though generating great compassion, has no perception of sentient beings;
At that time, the wise one becomes the object of offering for all beings,
And always meaningfully consumes the alms of the realm. ^5-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-22]]

Any bodhisattva who, in order to liberate
The sentient beings of gods and humans and the three lower realms long connected here,
Desires to show the broad path to the other shore to the realm of sentient beings,
Should strive day and night in the perfection of wisdom. ^5-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-23]]

If a person who had never previously found a supreme jewel
Finds it at another time, but out of joy
Becomes careless right after finding it and loses it,
Because it is lost, longing for the jewel, they endure constant suffering. ^5-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-24]]

Likewise, one who has entered supreme awakening should not abandon the yoga
Of the perfection of wisdom, which is like a jewel;
Just as one who has found a jewel, taken it up, and wrapped it with great diligence,
And swiftly goes on their way, attains peace. ^5-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-25]]

When the sun, freed from clouds and arrayed in a lattice of light rays,
Rises, destroying all the dense, pitch-black darkness,
It eclipses all fireflies and all living beings,
Along with the hosts of stars and all the light of the moon. ^5-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-26]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva who practices the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Practicing emptiness and the signless,
The wise one, having destroyed the dense darkness of views, outshines all beings,
Arhats, pratyekabuddhas, and many bodhisattvas. ^5-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-27]]

For example, a prince who desires the purpose of giving away wealth
Becomes the chief of all and is worthy to be approached.
If even as he is, he brings joy to many sentient beings,
What need is there to mention when he obtains skillful means and is established in the kingdom! ^5-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-28]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva skilled in practicing wisdom
Bestows the nectar of immortality and brings joy to gods and humans.
If even as they are, they strive to benefit many sentient beings,
What need is there to mention when they are established as a Dharma King! ^5-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-29]]

At that time, Māra becomes afflicted by a thorn,
Tormented by sorrow, suffering, mental distress, and weakness.
Thinking, "How can this bodhisattva's mind be made to falter?"
He burns the directions and hurls meteors to display terrifying sights. ^5-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-30]]

When those wise ones possess intense resolve,
And behold the supreme meaning of the perfection of wisdom day and night,
At that time, their body, mind, and speech move like a bird in the sky;
How could the associates of the Dark One find an opportunity? ^5-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-31]]

When bodhisattvas fight and quarrel,
And possess angry minds in mutual discord,
At that time, Māra rejoices and becomes supremely happy,
Thinking, "Those two will become distant from the primordial wisdom of the Victors." ^5-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-32]]

Those two will become distant and like flesh-eating demons;
Both will cause their own commitments to degenerate.
How could there be awakening for those who are hostile and devoid of patience?
At that time, the Māras along with their factions will rejoice. ^5-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-33]]

If any bodhisattva who has not obtained a prophecy
Becomes angry and initiates a dispute with one who has obtained a prophecy,
For however many moments of mind possessing the fault of barrenness there are,
For that many eons they must don the armor from the beginning anew. ^5-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-34]]

"Buddhas touch awakening through the perfection of patience."
Generating mindfulness, thinking, "This mind is not good,"
They confess individually and restrain themselves in the future,
And do not rejoice in it—they train here in the qualities of a Buddha. ^5-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-35]]

When training, whoever does not assert any training at all,
Does not apprehend a trainee or the phenomena of training,
And does not conceptualize these two—training and non-training—
Whoever trains like that trains here in the qualities of a Buddha. ^5-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-36]]

Whichever bodhisattva knows this training in this way,
Will never become one with degenerate training or faulty ethical discipline;
In order to obtain the qualities of a Buddha, they train in this;
They are skilled in the higher training, and possess non-apprehension. ^5-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-37]]

Training in this way in the wisdom of the wise light-makers,
They do not generate even a single non-virtuous mind;
Just as, when overwhelmed by the rays of the sun moving in the sky,
Darkness does not remain in the space before it. ^5-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-38]]

For those who have trained in the perfection of wisdom,
All the perfections come to be included here;
Just as the sixty-two views are included within the view of the transitory collection,
Likewise, it is taught that these perfections are included here. ^5-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-39]]

For example, when the life faculty ceases,
All other faculties, however many there are, cease;
Likewise, for the supreme wise ones who practice wisdom,
All perfections are taught to be gathered herein. ^5-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-40]]

Wise bodhisattvas train in whatever qualities śrāvakas possess,
And likewise in all the qualities of pratyekabuddhas;
They do not abide in them, nor do they generate desire for them;
Thinking, "This is what I must train in," therefore they train. ^5-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-41]]

For those who have fully entered into supreme, irreversible awakening,
When one rejoices from the depths of one's heart in their generation of the mind of awakening,
The Mount Meru of the three thousandfold world system could be measured by weighing it on a scale,
But the virtue of that rejoicing is not like that. ^5-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-42]]

However many sentient beings there are who seek virtue and desire to be of benefit,
One also rejoices in the aggregate of merit of them all;
Therefore, once they have attained the qualities of the Victors,
They give the gift of Dharma to the world in order to exhaust suffering. ^5-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-43]]

Any bodhisattva who, being non-conceptual, fully knows all phenomena
As empty, signless, and free from elaboration,
Does not seek awakening through a dualistic wisdom;
That yogin strives in the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^5-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-44]]

The element of space and what contradicts that space
Do not exist, and no one has attained it;
Likewise, the bodhisattva skilled in the practice of wisdom
Is like space, and practices total peace. ^5-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-45]]

In the midst of people, an illusory person does not think,
"I must please these people," yet still does so;
Though seen displaying various miraculous powers,
They have no body, no mind, and not even a name. ^5-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-46]]

Likewise, those who practice wisdom never think,
"Having realized awakening, I will completely liberate beings";
Endowed with various births and many activities,
Though displaying them like an illusion, they practice non-conceptually. ^5-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-47]]

For example, an emanation of a Buddha performs the deeds of a Buddha,
Yet in doing so, not even the slightest pride arises;
Likewise, the bodhisattva skilled in practicing wisdom
Displays all activities like an emanation and an illusion. ^5-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-48]]

A skilled carpenter crafts figures resembling men and women,
And they too perform all activities;
Likewise, the bodhisattva skilled in practicing wisdom
Performs all activities through non-conceptual primordial wisdom. ^5-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-49]]

To those skilled in practicing thus, many hosts of gods
Join palms, bow down, and pay homage;
And however many Buddhas there are in the world systems of the ten directions,
They completely proclaim garlands of praise for their qualities. ^5-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-50]]

However many sentient beings there are in realms equal to the Ganges,
If all of them were to take up the intention and become Māras,
And even if they emanated that many more from every single hair,
All of them could not create obstacles for the wise. ^5-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-51]]

Through four causes, the wise bodhisattva is strong,
Difficult for the four Māras to overcome and impossible to shake:
Dwelling in emptiness, not forsaking sentient beings,
Acting exactly as they speak, and possessing the blessing of the Sugatas. ^5-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-52]]

When this perfection of wisdom, the Mother of the Tathāgatas,
Is taught, whichever bodhisattva aspires to it,
And exerts themselves in accomplishing it with sincere intent,
That noble one should be known to have entered into omniscience. ^5-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-53]]

They do not dwell in the dharmadhātu or suchness,
But, for example, like a cloud in space, they dwell without dwelling.
A mantra-holder, traveling in space without a dwelling, wishing to dwell,
Through the power of mantra, brings forth out-of-season flowers and empowers the place as they wish. ^5-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-54]]

Practicing thus, the wise and brilliant bodhisattva
Does not apprehend that which realizes, nor the qualities of a Buddha;
Does not apprehend an expounder, one who desires the Dharma, or one who views;
This is the dwelling of those who desire peace and delight in qualities. ^5-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-55]]

Except for the liberation of the arhats and the Tathāgatas,
This dwelling is the supreme and unsurpassed among all
The dwellings of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas in peaceful samādhi,
Profoundly peaceful and endowed with bliss, however many there are. ^5-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-56]]

Though birds dwell in the sky, they do not fall;
Though fish dwell in water, they do not suffocate and die;
Likewise, the bodhisattva, through the powers of meditative concentration,
Having reached the far shore, dwells in emptiness and does not pass into nirvāṇa. ^5-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-57]]

Whoever wishes to reach the pinnacle of the qualities of all sentient beings,
And touch the supreme, most marvelous primordial wisdom of a Buddha,
And give the most excellent, supreme, and holy gift of Dharma,
Should rely on this supreme dwelling of the benefactors. ^5-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-58]]

When the Guides fully proclaim the trainings, this training
Is supreme and unsurpassed among all trainings, however many there are.
Any wise person who wishes to go to the far shore of all trainings
Should train in this perfection of wisdom, the training of the Buddhas. ^5-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-59]]

This is the supreme treasure of Dharma and the holy Dharma treasury;
It is the Buddha family, the treasury of happiness and joy for beings.
The Protectors of the World of the past, the future, and the ten directions—
All of them arise from this, yet the dharmadhātu is never exhausted. ^5-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-60]]

However many trees, fruits, flowers, and forests there are,
They all perfectly arise and emerge from the earth;
Yet the earth is neither exhausted nor increased,
It does not diminish, is non-conceptual, and has no weariness. ^5-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-61]]

The sons of the Buddha, the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, and the gods,
And however many phenomena of happiness and joy there are for all beings—
They all arise from the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Yet wisdom is neither exhausted nor increased. ^5-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-62]]

However many sentient beings there are—inferior, middling, and supreme—
The Sugata taught that they all arise from ignorance.
When conditions assemble, the machine of suffering fully arises,
Yet that machine of ignorance is neither exhausted nor increased. ^5-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-63]]

However many primordial wisdoms, approaches, gateways, skillful means, and roots there are,
All these arise from the supreme perfection of wisdom;
When conditions assemble, the machine of karma fully emerges,
Yet the perfection of wisdom has no exhaustion and no increase. ^5-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-64]]

Any bodhisattva who, regarding dependent origination,
Knows it to be unborn and unexhausted through this wisdom,
Just as a cloudless sun dispels darkness by radiating light,
Will destroy dense ignorance and attain the self-arisen state. ^5-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-65]]

Endowed with great power, they also dwell in the four meditative concentrations,
Yet they do not rely upon them, nor do they dwell in them;
Nevertheless, these four meditative concentrations, along with their branches,
Become the support for attaining supreme, sublime awakening. ^5-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-66]]

Having attained supreme wisdom and abiding in the meditative concentrations,
They also experience the four sublime formless samādhis;
These meditative concentrations are beneficial for supreme, sublime awakening,
Yet the bodhisattva does not train in order to exhaust the outflows. ^5-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-67]]

This is a wondrous marvel of those who accumulate qualities:
Abiding in meditative concentration and samādhi, they realise them as signless;
If the bodies of those who abide in them should perish,
They are reborn in the desire realm exactly according to their intentions. ^5-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-68]]

For example, it is like a person of Jambudvīpa who had never before
Visited the supreme city of the gods in the realm of the gods, but later goes there;
Having seen those realms held by the gods,
He does not become attached when he returns here. ^5-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-69]]

Likewise, holding the supreme qualities of a bodhisattva,
Exerting themselves in yoga and abiding in the samādhis of meditative concentration,
They again abide in the desire realm without attachment;
Like a lotus in water, they do not abide in the qualities of the foolish. ^5-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-70]]

The great beings act only to bring sentient beings to maturity,
To purify the fields, and to complete the perfections;
Thinking, "The qualities of awakening and the perfections would diminish,"
They do not seek birth in the formless realm. ^5-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-71]]

For example, just as if a person were to find treasures of jewels,
But did not generate a mind of desire for them,
And at another time completely took them,
Yet having taken them and gone home, did not become attached— ^5-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-72]]

Likewise, wise bodhisattvas who bestow joy and
Bliss, having attained the four peaceful samādhis of meditative concentration,
Relinquish the attainment of the samādhis of meditative concentration endowed with the bliss of meditative concentration,
And endowed with compassion for beings, enter again into the desire realm. ^5-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-73]]

If a bodhisattva abides in samādhi and meditative concentration,
But generates a mind desiring the vehicle of an arhat or pratyekabuddha,
They are not in meditative equipoise; they become agitated and have a distracted mind;
And the qualities of a Buddha completely decline, like a boatman whose boat is wrecked. ^5-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-74]]

Moreover, form and sound, and likewise smell and taste,
Touch—the five qualities of desire—even if they intensely engage in them,
If they are free from the vehicle of the arhat and pratyekabuddha and delight in the mind of awakening,
The hero should be known to be constantly in meditative equipoise. ^5-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-75]]

For the sake of other sentient beings and persons, the perfectly pure bodhisattva
Manifestly strives and acts in the perfection of diligence;
For example, just as a servant woman drawing water is powerless, under the power of her master,
Likewise, the heroes proceed under the power of all sentient beings. ^5-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-76]]

Whether she is scolded or even constantly beaten,
The servant woman does not speak a word in reply to her master;
Thinking, "He will even kill me,"
With an extremely terrified mind, she is overcome with fear. ^5-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-77]]

Likewise, those who have set out on the path to supreme awakening for the sake of awakening
Should abide as if they were servants to all beings;
From this, awakening is attained and the qualities perfected;
Fire arising from grass and wood burns that very grass and wood. ^5-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-78]]

Letting go of your own happiness, with a mind free of expectations,
Manifestly strive day and night for the needs of other sentient beings;
Just as a mother attends to her only child,
Abide without weariness, from the depths of your heart. ^5-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-79]]

Whatever bodhisattva wishes to cycle in saṃsāra for a long time,
A yogin who strives for the benefit of sentient beings and the purification of the field,
If not even an atom of a weary mind arises,
They are endowed with the perfection of diligence and are without negligence. ^5-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-80]]

An unwise bodhisattva counts tens of millions of eons;
If they perceive it as long and perceive it as suffering, and so accomplish awakening,
When accomplishing the authentic Dharma, they will experience suffering for a long time;
Through this, their perfection of diligence diminishes and they are lazy. ^5-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-81]]

From the first time they generate the mind of supreme awakening,
Until unsurpassed awakening is attained,
If they consider this in their minds as merely a single day and night,
The clear-minded wise ones should be known as having commenced diligence. ^5-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-82]]

If someone says to them, "After demolishing Mount Meru,
You will attain supreme awakening,"
And they generate a weary mind and even consider its magnitude,
At that time, the bodhisattva has become lazy. ^5-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-83]]

But if they think, "What is difficult about a measure such as this?"
And generate the mind to crush Mount Meru to dust in a mere instant,
The wise bodhisattva has commenced diligence,
And will, before long, attain the supreme awakening of a Guide. ^5-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-84]]

Thinking, "Having completely ripened beings, I will act for their benefit,"
If they exert themselves with body, speech, and mind,
Because they abide in the perception of a self, they have become lazy;
They become as far from omniscience as the sky is from the earth. ^5-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-85]]

Whenever they have no perception of body, mind, and sentient beings,
And having completely turned away from perception, they practice the non-dual Dharma,
This, the Benefactor has taught, is the perfection of diligence
Of those who desire the supreme, peaceful, and unwaning awakening. ^5-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-86]]

If they hear others speaking ill and using harsh words,
Thinking, "I am happy," the wise bodhisattva rejoices.
Who speaks? Who hears? By what, what is said, and for what reason?
Endowed with the supreme perfection of patience, they are wise. ^5-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-87]]

Whatever bodhisattva possesses the excellent dharma of patience—
Whoever fills the three thousandfold world system with jewels and offers them to the Buddhas, the Knowers of the World,
And to arhats and pratyekabuddhas,
That mass of generosity cannot match a fraction of their merit. ^5-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-88]]

The body of one who abides in patience becomes completely pure;
Possessing the thirty-two marks and boundless power and strength,
They proclaim the supreme Dharma of emptiness to sentient beings;
Wandering beings will rejoice in this wise one endowed with patience. ^5-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-89]]

If some sentient beings take a packet of sandalwood,
And with great respect rub it on the bodhisattva,
While another scatters burning coals on their head—towards both
That bodhisattva should generate an equal and even mind. ^5-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-90]]

Having practiced patience in that way, the wise bodhisattva,
If they dedicate that generation of the mind to supreme awakening,
That hero, through patience towards the entire world, outshines the arhats,
The pratyekabuddhas, and however many there are in the realm of sentient beings. ^5-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-91]]

The one who practices patience should also generate the mind thinking this:
'In hell, among animals, and in the world of Yama, there is much suffering;
If I have helplessly experienced harm caused by sensory pleasures,
Why should I not now practice patience for the sake of awakening?' ^5-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-92]]

'Whips, sticks, weapons, killing, binding, and beating,
Severing of the head, and cutting off ears, hands, feet, and nose—
I will bear with patience whatever sufferings there are in the world!'
Thinking this, the bodhisattva abides in the perfection of patience. ^5-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-93]]

Through ethical discipline, those who desire peace become noble;
Abiding in the domain of the ten powers, their ethical discipline is unimpaired.
They follow all the activities of restraint, whatever they may be,
And, for the benefit of all sentient beings, dedicate them to awakening. ^5-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-94]]

If one desires to touch the awakening of an arhat or a pratyekabuddha,
One is lacking ethical discipline, unwise, and likewise one's conduct degenerates.
But if one dedicates it to the supreme peace of awakening,
Even while engaged in sensory pleasure, one abides in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-95]]

Whatever phenomena certainly cause the qualities of awakening to arise,
This is the purpose of ethical discipline for those endowed with the phenomena of quality.
Whatever phenomena cause the awakening of those who benefit others to completely degenerate,
The Guide has stated that this is lacking ethical discipline. ^5-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-96]]

Even if a bodhisattva indulges in the five qualities of desire, enjoying sensory pleasure,
If they have gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the noble Sangha,
And direct their mind to omniscience, thinking, "I must become a Buddha,"
One should understand that this wise one abides in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-97]]

Even if one practices the ten paths of virtuous action for ten million eons,
If one generates desire for the state of an arhat or a pratyekabuddha,
At that time, a flaw arises in one's ethical discipline and one's ethical discipline degenerates;
That generation of the mind is much heavier than even a defeat. ^5-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-98]]

Guarding ethical discipline and dedicating it to supreme awakening,
Having no conceit on account of it, and not praising oneself,
And completely abandoning the perception of a self and the perception of sentient beings—
Such a bodhisattva is said to abide in the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-98

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-99]]

If a bodhisattva practicing the path of the Victors
Thinks, "These sentient beings possess ethical discipline, and these lack ethical discipline,"
By engaging in diverse perceptions, they are utterly lacking ethical discipline;
Their ethical discipline degenerates, and they lack completely pure ethical discipline. ^5-99

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-100]]

Whoever has no perception of a self and no perception of sentient beings,
Being free from perception and desire, how could there be non-restraint?
Whoever has no conceit regarding restraint and lack of restraint,
This was taught by the Guide to be the ethical discipline of restraint. ^5-100

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-101]]

Any pure sentient being who thus possesses ethical discipline
Has no fixating regard for anything pleasant or unpleasant;
Giving away their head, hands, and feet, they do not even have a discouraged mind,
And giving away all their possessions, they are always free from attachment. ^5-101

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-102]]

Knowing that phenomena are by nature selflessness and insubstantial,
If they give away even their own flesh without a discouraged mind,
At that time, what need is there to mention giving away external objects?
For them to act with miserliness is impossible. ^5-102

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-103]]

With a perception of a self, they are attached to things through grasping at things as mine;
How could fools have the intention to give?
Miserly ones will be born in the realm of hungry ghosts,
And even if they are born as humans, they will then be impoverished. ^5-103

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-104]]

At that time, the bodhisattva, knowing sentient beings to be impoverished,
Becomes intent on giving and always gives freely.
The four continents, beautifully adorned, are like a glob of spit;
Having given them away they become joyful, whereas attaining the continents is not like that. ^5-104

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-105]]

The wise and brilliant bodhisattva,
To all those sentient beings, however many there are in the three realms of existence,
Thinking, "May this generosity be given!" practises generosity, and
Dedicates that too to supreme awakening for the sake of beings. ^5-105

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-106]]

Having given generosity, they do not dwell on entities;
They never have any hope for a ripening result;
Being thus skilled in giving, they give away everything,
And having given a little, it becomes much and immeasurable. ^5-106

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-107]]

However many sentient beings there are without exception in the three states of existence,
Taking them as the utmost extent of what can be conceived, if all of them
To the Knowers of the World, the Buddhas, arhats, and pratyekabuddhas,
Give gifts for infinite eons and properly seek to be a śrāvaka— ^5-107

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-108]]

If any wise bodhisattva, skilled in means,
Rejoices in their basis of meritorious action
And dedicates it to supreme awakening for the sake of sentient beings,
By dedicating it, they outshine all beings. ^5-108

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-109]]

Even a great heap of glass jewels
Is completely outshone by a single precious lapis lazuli;
Likewise, all the vast aggregates of generosity of all beings
Are outshone by the rejoicing bodhisattva. ^5-109

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-110]]

If a bodhisattva, having given gifts to beings,
Does not claim the merit of it as "mine" and is unsparing of the entity,
From that, their root of virtue will increase with great power,
Like the radiant, cloudless moon disk during the waxing fortnight. ^5-110

## Teaching the gradual path. ^6-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-1]]

Sixth topic: teaching the gradual path.
Through the bodhisattva's generosity, the destiny of hungry ghosts is cut off;
Poverty and likewise all afflictions are cut off;
While practicing, one will obtain infinite, abundant resources;
Through generosity, one fully ripens sentient beings who are suffering. ^6-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-2]]

Through ethical discipline, one abandons the manifold forms of animal existence
And the eight unfree states, and thereby one always finds leisure;
Through patience, one will obtain a fine, supreme, and vast form;
Golden-colored and beautiful, one becomes worthy to be beheld by beings. ^6-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-3]]

Through diligence, virtuous qualities will not decline,
And one will find the treasury of the Victors, infinite primordial wisdom;
Through meditative concentration, one abandons the despised qualities of desire,
And fully accomplishes knowledge, superknowledges, and samādhi. ^6-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-4]]

Through wisdom, having fully known the nature of phenomena,
One will completely transcend the three realms without exception;
As the Supreme Leader of Humans, having turned the precious wheel,
One teaches the Dharma to beings in order to exhaust suffering. ^6-4

## Teaching the Dharmakāya ^8-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-1]]

Chapter Eight: Showing the Dharmakāya.

Fully embraces a perfectly pure field and pure sentient beings;
Fully embraces the Buddha lineage and also the Dharma lineage;
Likewise, fully embraces the lineage of the Sangha and all qualities. ^8-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-2]]

The supreme physician who heals the diseases of wandering beings
Spoke this teaching on wisdom, endowed with the path to awakening;
Its name is the Collection of Precious Qualities, the path to awakening,
And this path was explained so that all sentient beings may attain it. ^8-2
