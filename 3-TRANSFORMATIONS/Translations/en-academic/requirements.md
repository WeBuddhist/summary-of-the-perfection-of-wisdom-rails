# Requirements — Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā, English, academic

## Audience and register
Students and practitioners reading a careful study translation; university readers of Buddhist texts (see `audience.md`). Faithful, precise, readable English; no archaism.

## Source and grounding
- Translate from the Tibetan. Use the aligned Sanskrit and the aligned commentary passages to settle the meaning of each verse; the two machine drafts (DharmaMitra, Gemini) may be consulted but are not authorities.
- Where the commentaries read a verse differently, follow the reading they share; if they truly split, follow the Tibetan's most literal sense.
- Translate the whole verse, in order: no omissions, no additions, no summary.
- Never add explanations, glosses or notes in the translation itself.

## Vocabulary
- Every term in `termbase.md` is rendered with its locked academic rendering, inflected for number and grammar as needed. Where a term has several senses, the sense assigned to that occurrence is used.
- Sanskrit kept only where the termbase keeps it, with IAST diacritics; proper names in IAST (Subhūti, Śāriputra, Maitreya).

## Form and style
- A four-line Tibetan verse becomes four lines of English free verse (no rhyme, no metre), separated by line breaks, following the Tibetan line order wherever English syntax allows. Longer or shorter verses keep their own line count. Prose blocks (title, homage, colophon) stay prose.
- Standard modern English punctuation; each line must read naturally and the verse must parse as a whole.
- Keep the Buddha's voice: the verses are a sūtra in verse spoken by the Buddha, teaching about the bodhisattva who practises the perfection of wisdom. Keep his direct addresses (for example to Subhūti), his comparisons and similes, and his rhetorical questions as questions.
- Where a verse states a wrong view in order to reject it, the view must stay recognisable as rejected; do not present it as the teaching.
- Headings: translate the Tibetan title of each of the eight topics literally and concisely (for example "Showing all-aspect omniscience").
