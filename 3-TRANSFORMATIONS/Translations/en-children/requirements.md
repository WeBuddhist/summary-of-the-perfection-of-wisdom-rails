# Requirements — Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā, English, children's version

## Audience and register
Children aged 8–12, reading on their own or being read to (see `audience.md`). Warm, clear, simple English that a ten-year-old understands on first hearing.

## Source of meaning
- Each verse is retold from the academic translation of the same verse, checked against the Tibetan. Keep the meaning of every verse: who speaks, who is addressed, what is described, what is compared, what is taught, what is denied.
- Do not add ideas, morals, explanations or examples that the verse does not contain. Do not drop a point the verse makes.
- Keep one children's passage per source verse, with the same id, in the same order.

## Vocabulary
- Use the locked children's rendering from `termbase.md` for every listed term, every time.
- No Sanskrit except names; names without diacritics (Subhuti, Shariputra, Maitreya). "Buddha" stays "Buddha".
- Never make a deep idea false to make it simple. The hard ideas here are emptiness, signlessness, and not grasping at "a bodhisattva" or at the perfection of wisdom itself. Emptiness is not "nothing"; not apprehending a bodhisattva does not mean "bodhisattvas don't exist"; not clinging to the perfection of wisdom does not mean it is worthless.
- Mara and his tricks may be described plainly, as the verse describes them, without added frightening detail.

## Style
- Each verse becomes a short passage of plain sentences (mostly under 15 words); no verse form is needed. Everyday words. Active voice.
- Keep the Buddha as the speaker, with his addresses (for example "Subhuti, …") and his comparisons; children can follow a teacher talking to a student.
- Questions stay questions. Where a verse states a wrong view in order to reject it, make clear that it is being rejected ("Some think …, but that is not so").
- Headings: short and plain.
