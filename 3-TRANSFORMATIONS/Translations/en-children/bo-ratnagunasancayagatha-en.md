## Teaching About Knowing Everything in Every Way ^1-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-1]]

Chapter one: Teaching about knowing everything in every way. Many rivers flow here in Jambudvipa. They make flowers, fruits, healing plants, and forests grow. They all depend on the serpent king. This is the lord of the serpent spirits who lives in Anavatapta. All this growth comes from the great power of that lord of the serpent spirits. ^1-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-2]]

Whatever teaching the listeners of the Victorious One share, they explain it clearly with good reasons. Their words bring great joy and good results. All of this comes from the power of the Thus-Gone One. ^1-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-3]]

Why is this? Because the Victorious One has shared many ways of teaching. His students practice these ways completely. After they master them, they teach exactly what they practiced. They do this by the power of the Buddha, not by their own strength. ^1-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-4]]

An awakening hero does not hold onto perfect wisdom. They do not hold onto an awakening hero or the wish to awaken. When they hear this, they are not confused or afraid. That awakening hero practices the wisdom of the Well-Gone Ones. ^1-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-5]]

There is no form, no feeling, no idea, and no intention. For that person, awareness does not have even a tiny speck of a place to rest. They do not rest on any things. They practice without resting anywhere. Without grasping, they reach the awakening of the Well-Gone Ones. ^1-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-6]]

A wandering seeker named Shrenika used his knowledge to destroy the heaps that no one can grasp. An awakening hero understands things in this exact same way. They do not touch the peace beyond sorrow. Instead, they stay right there in wisdom. ^1-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-7]]

They also ask, "What is this wisdom? Whose is it? Where does it come from?" They see that all these things are empty. They look at them closely without feeling sad or afraid. That awakening hero is close to awakening. ^1-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-8]]

Some do not truly understand. They see form. They focus on the heaps of feeling, intention, and awareness. They might even think, "These heaps are empty." But that awakening hero is still practicing with signs. They lack faith in the state that is never born. ^1-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-9]]

Others do not practice in form, feeling, idea, or intention. They do not practice in awareness. They practice without staying anywhere. They do not grasp at what is called "practicing." Instead, they rely on wisdom. With a mind that knows what is never born, they touch the highest, peaceful deep calm. ^1-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-10]]

Any awakening hero who stays here like this is completely at peace. The Thus-Gone Ones of the past have given a prediction about them. They are not proud. They never think, "I am resting in calm," or "I have stood up." Why? Because they fully understand the nature of things. ^1-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-11]]

By practicing like this, they practice the wisdom of the Well-Gone Ones. They truly understand that not practicing is the real practice. They do not grasp at any of the things they practice. This is the practice of the highest perfect wisdom. ^1-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-12]]

What does not exist is called non-existent. Unwise people imagine this. They think that things exist or do not exist. But existing and not existing are not real things. Any awakening hero who knows this will surely break free. ^1-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-13]]

Someone here knows the five heaps are like an illusion. They do not treat the illusion as one thing and the five heaps as another. They are free from any different idea and practice in perfect peace. This is the practice of the highest perfect wisdom. ^1-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-14]]

Someone with a wise friend and clear insight will never be afraid. They will not be scared to hear the Mother of the Victorious Ones. But someone with a bad friend just relies on others. They are like an unbaked clay pot that falls apart when touched by water. ^1-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-15]]

You might ask, "Why is this person called an awakening hero?" They cut off clinging. They want to completely end clinging to everything. They touch the awakening of a Victorious One, which is a state of not clinging. Because of this, they get the name awakening hero. ^1-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-16]]

You might ask, "Why is this person called a great hero?" It is because they become the highest in the large crowd of living beings. They cut through the strong, wrong views in the realm of living beings. That is why they are called a great hero. ^1-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-17]]

They have great giving, great intelligence, and great power. They have entered the highest Great Vehicle of the Victorious Ones. They put on the great armor and defeat the tricks of Mara. For these reasons, they are called a great hero. ^1-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-18]]

Imagine a magician creates a fake crowd at a crossroads and then cuts off all their heads. The awakening hero knows that all beings are just like those illusions. Because they know this, the awakening hero has no fear. ^1-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-19]]

Form, idea, feeling, intention, and awareness are not tied up or set free. They do not truly exist. You must enter into awakening this way without losing heart. This is the best armor for holy persons. ^1-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-20]]

Why is this called the Great Vehicle of Awakening? Once you ride in it, it carries all living beings beyond sorrow. This vehicle is like space. It is a great palace that cannot be measured. It is the highest vehicle that brings clear joy, happiness, and bliss. ^1-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-21]]

Whoever rides in it cannot be found going in any direction. They are taught to go to peace beyond sorrow, but no going is found. Think of a dead person who has nowhere to go. For this reason, that person is completely called peace beyond sorrow. ^1-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-22]]

You cannot find the awakening hero in the earlier limit, the later end, or the present. The past, present and future are pure. That awakening hero is not made by causes and is free from tangled thinking. This is the practice of the highest perfect wisdom. ^1-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-23]]

A wise and knowing awakening hero thinks about what is never born. They practice in this way. They develop great compassion without the idea of living beings. This is the practice of the highest perfect wisdom. ^1-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-24]]

Someone might hold the idea of living beings and the idea of suffering. They might think, "I will help beings and end their suffering." That makes them an awakening hero who only imagines a self and living beings. This is not the practice of highest perfect wisdom. ^1-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-25]]

You should know that all living beings are just like the self. Know that all things are just like all living beings. Do not think about arising or no arising. This is the practice of highest perfect wisdom. ^1-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-26]]

People in the world have many names for things. But you must let go of their arising and passing away. Then you will gain the highest, deathless deep knowing. There is nothing greater than this. That is why it is called perfect wisdom. ^1-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-27]]

Any awakening hero who practices this way without doubt has true wisdom. You should know they rest in sameness. They completely understand that all things are with no nature of their own. This is the practice of highest perfect wisdom. ^1-27

The second chapter, teaching knowing all the paths. ^1-28

## Teaching knowing all the paths. ^2-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-1]]

Whoever does not get stuck on form does not get stuck on feeling. They do not get stuck on any idea or intention. Whoever does not get stuck on awareness rests in the true nature of things. This is the practice of perfect wisdom, the highest perfection. ^2-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-2]]

You do not hold onto what is lasting or always changing. You do not hold onto happiness or suffering, or what is nice or not nice. You do not hold onto a self or no self. You do not hold onto the way things really are, or emptiness. You do not hold onto getting the fruit. You do not hold onto the level of a worthy one. You do not hold onto the level of a solitary Buddha, or even the level of a Buddha. ^2-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-3]]

The Guide does not stay in the realm not made by causes. He also does not stay in things made by causes. He practices by not staying anywhere. In the same way, an awakening hero rests on a base through a stay without staying. The Victorious One taught that a stay without staying is the true way to stay. ^2-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-4]]

Some people wish to be a listener of the Well-Gone One. Others wish to become a solitary Buddha. Some wish to be a King of the Teaching, a Buddha. But they cannot reach these goals without relying on this patience. It is like trying to cross a river without finding a way to the other shore. ^2-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-5]]

Think of the teaching, the listening, and what is taught. Think of getting the fruit. Think of the solitary Buddha and the Protector of the World. Think of the peace beyond sorrow reached by wise and clear minds. The Thus-Gone One taught that all these things are like an illusion. ^2-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-6]]

Four kinds of persons are not afraid of this. The first is a child of the Victorious Ones who is skilled in the truth. The second is someone who is never turning back. The third is a worthy one who has washed away dirt and dropped all doubt. The fourth is someone fully held by a wise friend. ^2-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-7]]

A wise and smart awakening hero practices like this. They do not train in the level of a worthy one. They do not train in the level of a solitary Buddha. For the sake of knowing everything, they train following a Buddha's qualities. Whoever does not train in learning and not learning, that is real training. ^2-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-8]]

They do not train to make form grow or shrink. They do not train to hold onto form. They do not train to hold onto different things. Still, they train and fully hold onto knowing everything. This sure way out is the training of those who love good qualities. ^2-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-9]]

Form is not wisdom. There is no wisdom in form. Awareness, an idea, feeling, and intention are not wisdom. There is no wisdom in them, either. This is exactly like the element of space. There is no difference at all. ^2-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-10]]

The nature of something to hold onto is boundless. Whatever the nature of living beings is, that is also boundless. The nature of the element of space is also boundless. The wisdom of the Knower of the World is boundless, too. ^2-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-11]]

The Guide has clearly taught that an idea is this shore. By breaking down and leaving behind every idea, you cross to the far shore. Those who become free from any idea reach the far shore. They stay in the word of the Teacher. ^2-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-12]]

The Teacher could stay for as many ages as there are grains of sand. He could clearly say the words 'living beings.' But since they are pure from the very start, how could living beings ever arise? This is the practice of the highest perfection, perfect wisdom. ^2-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-13]]

When I spoke the truth of this highest perfection, the highest being of the past spoke to me. He said, 'In the future, you will become a Buddha.' I received this prediction. This is what the Victorious One said. ^2-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-14]]

The Protectors practice this perfect wisdom. This perfection keeps people safe. If anyone respects, remembers, and truly understands it, they will not be hurt by poison, weapons, fire, or water. Mara and Mara's side will never find a chance to harm them. ^2-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-15]]

Imagine someone builds shrines out of the seven treasures. They build them for a Well-Gone One who has passed into the final peace beyond sorrow. They make offerings there. Imagine they build as many shrines as there are grains of sand in the Ganges river. Imagine these shrines fill thousands of millions of lands. ^2-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-16]]

Imagine all the living beings in those endless millions of lands do no other work. Instead, they make offerings for an age or longer. They offer heavenly flowers, sweet incense, and oils in the past, present and future. ^2-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-17]]

The ten powers of the Guides will come from this wisdom. So, someone might write this Mother of the Well-Gone Ones in a book. They might keep it and honor it with flowers and sweet oils. If they do, they gain much more stored-up goodness. Building and making offerings to all those shrines does not even match a tiny part of it. ^2-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-18]]

The perfect wisdom of the Victorious Ones is a great spell of knowledge. These words of knowing calm the sadness and suffering in every realm of living beings. Every Protector of the World from the past and in the ten directions trained in this perfection. By learning it, each became a perfect doctor and an unsurpassed king of healers. ^2-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-19]]

Wise people act with benefit and compassion. They train in these words of knowing and touch awakening. There is happiness made by causes and happiness not made by causes. You should know that all that happiness comes from this. ^2-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-20]]

Seeds planted in the earth will sprout. When the right conditions come together, many different shapes are born. The five perfections and all the good qualities of awakening are the same. All of them are born from perfect wisdom. ^2-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-21]]

Whenever a ruler of the whole world travels, the seven treasures and his army follow his path. Wherever the perfect wisdom of a Victorious One goes, every good quality also travels along that path. All these qualities follow this perfection of wisdom. ^2-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-22]]

A Victorious One asked Shakra a question, and Shakra replied. He said, 'Imagine there were as many Buddha-lands as grains of sand in the Ganges river. Even if they were all completely filled with the relics of Victorious Ones, I would still choose this perfect wisdom. I would take this very perfection of wisdom.' ^2-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-23]]

If you ask why, it is not because I lack respect for a relic. People honor these relics because they are filled with wisdom. Think of a person who serves a king. People show them respect because they rely on the king. In the same way, a relic of a Buddha is respected because it relies on perfect wisdom. It relies on this perfection of wisdom. ^2-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-24]]

A precious jewel has every good quality and is priceless. Whatever box it rests in deserves respect. Even if the jewel is taken out, people still take joy in the box. This is because those good qualities belong to that precious jewel. ^2-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-25]]

Supreme perfect wisdom has great good qualities. Because of this perfection, the relics of the Victorious Ones are honored even after they pass into the peace beyond sorrow. Anyone who wants to hold the good qualities of the Victorious Ones should take up this perfect wisdom. This is freedom. ^2-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-26]]

Wisdom comes before giving generosity. It also comes before good conduct, patience, steady effort, and deep focus. Wisdom holds them all together so that these qualities of goodness are not wasted. This shows that all things share a single way. ^2-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-27]]

For example, there are billions of trees in Jambudvipa. They have many different forms and kinds. We might talk about counting their many shadows. But the shadows themselves are not really different from one another. ^2-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-28]]

In the same way, the five perfections of the Victorious Ones get the very name of perfect wisdom. You can dedicate them for the sake of knowing everything. When you do, all six perfections share one taste. This one taste is called awakening. ^2-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-29]]

An awakening hero might not fully understand. They might teach that form, idea, feeling, intention, and awareness are always changing. But if they do, they act on a made-up idea. Wise people never destroy things. ^2-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-30]]

In this practice, you do not take anything as form or feeling. You do not find any idea. You do not take anything as awareness. You do not find any intention. You fully know that all things are never born and are empty. This is the practice of supreme perfect wisdom. It is the highest perfection. ^2-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-31]]

Imagine someone travels to lands as many as the grains of sand in the Ganges. They help all the living beings there become a worthy one. But suppose someone else writes down this perfect wisdom and gives the book to a great person. That second person earns much more stored-up goodness. Sharing this perfection of wisdom is much better. ^2-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-32]]

Why is that? The best speakers train in this. They show that all things are emptiness. When a listener hears this, they quickly reach freedom. This is how someone reaches the awakening of a solitary Buddha and a Buddha's awakening. ^2-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-33]]

If there is no sprout, a tree could never grow in the world. Without a tree, how could branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit ever appear? In the same way, without the wish to awaken, a Victorious One would never appear in the world. Without a Victorious One, how could the fruits—like Shakra, Brahma, and listeners—ever appear? ^2-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-34]]

When the sun shines its light, living beings go to work. It is the same when wise people bring the wish to awaken into the world. They do this to know true awakening. Because of their deep knowing, living beings gain every good quality and wonderful quality. ^2-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-35]]

Think about Lake Anavatapta. If the lord of the serpent spirits did not live there, how could rivers flow here in Jambudvipa? If there were no rivers, no flowers or fruits could grow. Without rivers, the ocean would not have many forms of jewels. ^2-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-36]]

It is the same here. Without the wish to awaken, how could the deep knowing of the Well-Gone One appear in all these worlds? Without deep knowing, good qualities do not grow, and there is no awakening. Then a Buddha's qualities, which are like a great ocean, would not exist. ^2-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-37]]

Think of all the glowing creatures in this world. Imagine all the light they shine together. A single ray of light from the sun is far better. All the light from those creatures cannot match even a tiny part of the sun's light. ^2-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-38]]

All the listeners might gather a huge pile of stored-up goodness. They build this through generosity, good conduct, and meditation. But an awakening hero only needs a single thought to rejoice. The stored-up goodness of all those listeners cannot match a tiny part of the awakening hero's rejoicing. ^2-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-39]]

Think of the billions of Buddhas from the past. Think of those living right now in endless millions of lands. Think of all the Protectors of the world who have passed into the peace beyond sorrow. They all share the jewel teaching to end suffering. ^2-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-40]]

Think of the time from when they first make the wish for highest awakening until the true teaching of those Guides comes to an end. Think of all the stored-up goodness those Victorious Ones have during that time. Think of everything that is filled with the perfections and a Buddha's qualities. ^2-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-41]]

Think also of the goodness of the children of the Buddha and the listeners. This includes the stained goodness and the goodness free of impurity from both learners and ones who have finished learning. An awakening hero gathers all this goodness together and rejoices. Then they dedicate it all to awakening to help all beings. ^2-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-42]]

When they dedicate, the idea of a mind might arise. There might also be the idea of awakening and the idea of living beings. Because of these ideas, the mind stays in views and becomes attached to these three things. Because they treat them as something to hold onto, they do not truly dedicate. ^2-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-43]]

You might know that these things stop and fade away. The goal you dedicate them to fades away too. One of these things never dedicates to another of these things. If you understand this perfectly, then you truly dedicate. ^2-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-44]]

If you make a sign of it, you do not really dedicate it. But if there are no signs, it is the dedication to awakening. Think of eating excellent food that is mixed with poison. The Victorious One taught that seeing good things or any quality as something to hold onto is just like that. ^2-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-45]]

So, you should practice how to dedicate like this. The Victorious Ones fully know goodness. They know every way it appears, where it comes from, and its defining feature. You must rejoice in that goodness. Then you dedicate it in exactly the same way. ^2-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-46]]

If you dedicate stored-up goodness to awakening like this, it has no poison. You stay close to the Buddha. You speak exactly what the Victorious One has said. A hero who dedicates in this way shines very bright. They outshine every awakening hero in the world who is one who holds onto something. ^2-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-47]]

Imagine millions of blind people with no guide. They do not even know the path. How could they ever find the city? Without wisdom, these five perfections have no eyes. Because they have no guide, they cannot reach awakening. ^2-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-48]]

But when these practices are perfectly guided by wisdom, they gain eyes. Then they finally earn the name of perfections. Think of a beautiful painting that is missing its eyes. The artist earns no payment until the eyes are painted on. ^2-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-49]]

Wisdom breaks down things made by causes, things not made by causes, and good and bad things. You do not find even a tiny speck left. In the world, this perfection is known as perfect wisdom. It is like space, which does not rest on anything at all. ^2-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-50]]

Someone might think, "I will practice the wisdom of the Victorious One. I will free billions of living beings touched by suffering." An awakening hero seeking awakening who holds onto the idea of living beings is not practicing the highest perfection, perfect wisdom. ^2-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-51]]

Any awakening hero who practiced this perfection well in the past and had no doubts will right away form the idea of the Teacher when they hear about it now. They will quickly reach peaceful awakening. ^2-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-52]]

A person may have honored billions of Buddhas in the past. But they might not trust the perfect wisdom of the Victorious One. When a person with little understanding hears about this perfection, they will give it up. By giving it up, they will be left with no shelter and will go to the hell without rest. ^2-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-53]]

So, if you want to reach the highest deep knowing of a Buddha, you must build trust in this Mother of the Victorious Ones! For example, it would be wrong to act like a merchant who travels to an island of jewels but returns with no goods at all. ^2-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-54]]

You should know that the purity of form is the purity of the fruit. The purity of the fruit and form becomes the purity of knowing everything. The purity of knowing everything, the purity of the fruit, and the purity of form are equal to the element of space. They are not separate and cannot be cut apart. ^2-54

## Showing knowing every foundation. ^3-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-1]]

Chapter three: showing knowing every foundation. Through perfect wisdom, a hero acts anywhere. They go entirely beyond the three realms, but they do not enter freedom. Their mental trouble is gone, but they still show birth. They are free from getting old, getting sick, and dying, but they still show dying. ^3-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-2]]

These beings are stuck in the mud of name and form. They wander in the cycle of rebirth like a spinning wheel of wind. Confused beings are like deer caught in a trap. But those with wisdom fly freely like birds in the sky. ^3-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-3]]

Whoever acts with pure conduct does not get caught up in form. They do not get caught up in awareness, idea, feeling, and intention. Practicing in this way, they drop all clinging. Finding freedom from clinging, they practice the wisdom of the Well-Gone Ones. ^3-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-4]]

The wise and bright awakening hero practices in this way. They cut off clinging, and they move among beings without clinging. They are like the sun shining brightly after being freed from an eclipse by Rahu. They are like a blazing fire burning up grass, wood, and forests. ^3-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-5]]

All things are totally pure and pure by nature. When an awakening hero looks through perfect wisdom, they do not find a doer. They also do not find things. This is the highest practice of perfect wisdom. ^3-5

## Teaching full understanding in every way. ^4-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-1]]

Chapter Four: Teaching full understanding in every way. Shakra, king of the gods, asked the Victorious One, "How does an awakening hero work hard in the practice of wisdom?" He replied, "They do not work hard at all in the heaps and elements. Not working hard in the heaps is exactly how an awakening hero works hard." ^4-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-2]]

Some living beings hear that these things are like magic creations and illusions. They have no doubt. They keep working at their training. You should know they entered the Great Vehicle long ago. They have already served billions of Buddhas. ^4-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-3]]

Imagine a person walking on a wild path for many leagues. Then they see beautiful forests where the cowherds work. They think, "These are signs that a village or city is near." They feel relieved and have no fear of robbers. ^4-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-4]]

It is just like that when you seek awakening. Anyone who hears this perfect wisdom of the Victorious Ones feels relieved. They become fearless. They are not on the level of a worthy one, nor the level of a solitary Buddha. ^4-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-5]]

Imagine a person traveling to see the ocean. If they still see trees, forests, and mountains, the ocean is far away. But when they no longer see these signs, they think, "The great ocean is near." They have no doubt about it at all. ^4-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-6]]

Just like that, those who fully enter the highest awakening hear this perfect wisdom of the Victorious Ones. The Guide may not have given them a direct prediction yet. But they still know, "Before long, I will touch a Buddha's awakening." ^4-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-7]]

In the beautiful springtime, old leaves fall from the trees. Before long, those branches grow new leaves, fruits, and flowers. It is the same for anyone holding this perfect wisdom in their hands. Before long, they will reach the awakening of the Guides. ^4-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-8]]

Think of a pregnant mother who feels pain. People say her time to give birth has arrived. It is the same when an awakening hero hears the wisdom of the Victorious Ones. If they feel joy and strong hope, they will quickly touch awakening. ^4-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-9]]

When practicing the highest perfect wisdom, a meditator does not see form grow or shrink. They do not see things, non-things, or the expanse of all things. They do not even touch peace beyond sorrow. Someone like this truly lives in wisdom. ^4-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-10]]

A person practicing here does not make up ideas about a Buddha's qualities. They do not form ideas about any power, base of magical power, or peaceful awakening. Being without fixed ideas and free from made-up thoughts, they practice through blessing. This is how to practice the highest perfect wisdom. ^4-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-11]]

Subhuti asked the Buddha, the Moon of Speakers. He asked, "What is an obstacle for people who love good qualities?" The Teacher answered, "There will be many obstacles. For now, I will explain just a few of them." ^4-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-12]]

When someone writes down this perfect wisdom of the Victorious Ones, many kinds of clever speech will appear. But they will vanish like lightning, without helping beings at all. This is Mara's work. ^4-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-13]]

When this is explained, some people will have doubts. They will think, "The Guide did not even say my name. He did not talk about my family, birthplace, or clan." They will stop listening and walk away. This is Mara's work. ^4-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-14]]

If people do not know this, they throw away the root. In their confusion, they look for branches and leaves instead. This is like finding an elephant and then looking for its footprints. Hearing the perfect wisdom and then searching for other scriptures is exactly like that. ^4-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-15]]

Someone might find the best food with a hundred wonderful flavors. But then they look for bad food instead. An awakening hero might find this perfection. If they then look for awakening through the level of a worthy one, they are exactly like that. ^4-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-16]]

They will want respect and gain. With a mind wanting to look around, they will become close to householders. After leaving the teaching, they will do things against the teaching. Leaving the path and going on a wrong path is Mara's work. ^4-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-17]]

At that time, eager and faithful people will go to listen to this best teaching. But they will see that the speaker of the teaching is too busy with other things. Then they will leave without any joy, feeling unhappy. ^4-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-18]]

At that time, Mara's work will appear. People will upset many monks so they stop holding on to this perfect wisdom. Many other obstacles will also come up. ^4-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-19]]

Priceless jewels are very hard to find. They are rare, and they are always in danger. It is the same with the perfect wisdom of the Victorious Ones. This precious teaching is also rare and always in danger. ^4-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-20]]

Some living beings have just entered the vehicle. Their understanding is small, and they have not yet found this rare jewel. Mara will work hard to make obstacles for them. But the Buddhas of the ten directions will work hard to protect them. ^4-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-21]]

When a mother with many children gets sick, they all feel sad and take care of her. In the same way, the Buddhas of the worlds in the ten directions keep this holy wisdom in mind. She is their Mother in every world system. ^4-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-22]]

Think of every Protector of the World from the past and those in the ten directions right now. Those who will appear in the future also come from this. She is the Mother who gives birth to the Victorious Ones, the Teachers of the World. She also shows how other living beings think and act. ^4-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-23]]

Think of the way the world really is. Think of the way things really are for worthy ones. Think of the way things really are for a solitary Buddha, and the way things really are for the children of the Victorious Ones. All these are just one thing. They are free of real things, and they are not different. They are simply the way things really are. The Thus-Gone One knows the perfect wisdom. ^4-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-24]]

Wise people may stay in the world. They may pass into the peace beyond sorrow. Either way, the flawless true nature of things remains. This is the emptiness of things. An awakening hero realizes the way things really are. Because of this, a Buddha is called a Thus-Gone One. ^4-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-25]]

Guides with the ten powers rely on perfect wisdom as a beautiful garden. This is their home. They completely save living beings from suffering and the three lower realms. Even so, they never hold the idea of living beings. ^4-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-26]]

Think of a fearless lion resting in a mountain cave. It lets out a roar and scares many small wild animals. In the same way, the Lion among People relies on perfect wisdom. He lets out a roar in the world and scares many followers of another path. ^4-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-27]]

Think of the sun shining in the sky. Its rays dry up the earth and perfectly show all forms. In the same way, the King of the Teaching relies on perfect wisdom. He dries up the river of craving and strong wanting. He perfectly shows the teaching. ^4-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-28]]

You do not see forms. You do not see feelings. You do not see ideas. You do not see intentions. You do not see awareness, mind, or the thinking mind. The Thus-Gone One taught that this is seeing the teaching. ^4-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-29]]

Living beings say, "I see space." But how do you see space? Think about what this means. The Thus-Gone One taught that seeing things is just like that. This kind of seeing cannot be explained with any other example. ^4-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-30]]

Anyone who sees in this way sees all things. Think of a king who sits in calm balance while his helpers do everything. It is the same for the deeds of a Buddha and all the things of a listener. Perfect wisdom does them all. ^4-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-31]]

A king might not visit the towns and lands of his kingdom. But he still collects all the wealth from his country. The true nature of things for an awakening hero is like that. It does not go anywhere, but it gathers every good quality of the level of a Buddha. ^4-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-32]]

Some people have strong trust in the Well-Gone Ones and awakening heroes. They set their minds on the highest perfect wisdom. They will pass right by the two levels of the listener and the solitary Buddha. They will quickly reach the unbeatable awakening of the Victorious Ones. ^4-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-33]]

Think of a boat that breaks apart on the ocean. A person in the water might not grab onto a dead body, grass, or floating wood. They will drown in the middle of the water and never reach the shore. But anyone who holds onto those things will reach the far side of the ocean. ^4-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-34]]

In the same way, someone might be full of trust and gain confidence. But they might fail to follow the Mother, perfect wisdom. ^4-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-35]]

If the good qualities of their clever ways are fully guided by wisdom, they will quickly reach the wonderful awakening of the Well-Gone Ones. ^4-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-36]]

Think about carrying water in an unbaked clay pot. It is weak and will break quickly. But a baked clay pot will not break on the way. The water will reach your home safely. ^4-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-37]]

In the same way, an awakening hero might have a lot of trust. But without wisdom, they will quickly fail. If their trust is guided by wisdom, they will succeed. They will go past the two levels and reach the highest awakening. ^4-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-38]]

Think of a badly built boat on the ocean. It will break apart and sink with all its treasure and merchants. But a well-built boat will stay safe. It will reach the shore with all its treasure. ^4-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-39]]

In the same way, an awakening hero might be full of trust. But without wisdom, they will quickly fall away from awakening. If they have the highest perfect wisdom, they will stay safe. They will reach the awakening of the Victorious Ones. ^4-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-40]]

Imagine a person who is a hundred and twenty years old, very old and in pain. Even if they stand up, they cannot walk alone. But if people hold them up on both sides, they can walk easily. They do not need to fear falling. ^4-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-41]]

In the same way, an awakening hero might have weak wisdom. Even if they start their journey, they will fall away. But if they are supported by clever ways and great wisdom, they will stay safe. They will reach the awakening of a Thus-Gone One. ^4-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-42]]

Some awakening heroes are on the level of a beginner. They set out for the highest awakening of a Buddha with a strong aim. These excellent students are devoted to their teacher. They should always rely on wise teachers. ^4-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-43]]

Why is that? The good qualities of the wise come from these teachers. They teach perfect wisdom. The Victorious One has the highest of all good qualities. He said, "A Buddha's qualities depend on the wise friend." ^4-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-44]]

This is how you should teach a beginner. Tell them, "Dedicate generosity, good conduct, patience, and steady effort to awakening! Dedicate deep focus and wisdom to awakening, too! Do not hold on to the heaps of awakening. Do not have any clinging to the supreme!" ^4-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-45]]

Those who practice like this are oceans of goodness. They are moons among speakers. They are the refuge, the defender, and the dwelling place for beings. They are the support, the mind, and the island. They are Guides who help everyone. They are bright lamps who stay completely calm. They speak the highest teaching. ^4-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-46]]

These famous heroes put on armor for hard deeds. This is not the armor of the heaps, elements, and sense doors. They are free from the idea of the three vehicles. They have no holding on. They are never turning back. They never waver, and their nature is perfectly calm. ^4-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-47]]

They have these qualities and are free from tangled thinking. They are free from doubt, hesitation, and unsure minds. They hold the true meaning. When they hear perfect wisdom, they do not shrink back. They do not rely on others. You should know them as never turning back. ^4-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-48]]

This teaching of the Guides is deep and hard to see. No one has realized it, and no one has reached it. So, when the Kind Helper reached awakening, he paused. He thought, "Who among all the living beings will understand this?" ^4-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-49]]

Living beings love their dwelling places. They want the things we sense. Foolish people keep holding on, lost in darkness. But the teaching to reach has no dwelling place. It involves no holding on. This is why it clashes with the world. ^4-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-50]]

The element of space in the east and the south is endless. It is endless in the west and the north, too. It is above and below. It fills all the ten directions. Space is exactly the same everywhere. It has no differences at all. ^4-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-51]]

There is the way things really are in the past and future. There is the way things really are right now. There is the way things really are for the worthy ones. There is the way things really are for all things and for the Victorious Ones. The way things really are has no differences at all. ^4-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-52]]

The awakening of the Well-Gone Ones is free from separate things. An awakening hero might want to reach this. They must use clever ways and practice the perfection of wisdom. Without the wisdom of the Guides, they will not reach it. ^4-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-53]]

Imagine a huge bird that is one hundred and fifty leagues big. Its wings are tired. It has no strength or skill. If it jumps from the home of the Thirty-Three down to this Jambudvipa, it will surely crash and be ruined. ^4-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-54]]

You could practice the five perfections of the Victorious Ones for billions of ages. You could constantly make huge, endless wishes in the world. But if you lack clever ways and wisdom, you will just fall into the state of a listener. ^4-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-55]]

Anyone who wants to truly ride in this Buddha's vehicle must have a fair mind toward all beings. You should see them as your parents. Try to help them with a mind of loving-kindness. Be honest, never be mean, and always speak gentle words. ^4-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-56]]

The elder Subhuti asked the Protector of the World a question. He said, "Please teach me the signs of those who are free from mental trouble. They have an ocean of good qualities. How do these powerful people avoid turning back? May the Victorious One give a prediction about just a tiny part of their good qualities." ^4-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-57]]

They are free from the idea of differences, and they speak sensible words. They do not rely on other holy wanderers or priests. These wise people always avoid the three lower realms. They work very hard on the path of the ten good actions, doing the ten good deeds. ^4-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-58]]

They teach the teaching to beings without wanting any reward. They find great joy in the teaching. They always speak gentle words. They are staying alert while going, walking, sleeping, and sitting. They walk looking ahead only the length of a yoke. Their minds are free from confusion. ^4-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-59]]

They make themselves clean and wear clean clothes. The three are kept quiet and pure. These great leaders do not want to gain things. They always want the teaching. They have passed beyond the world of Mara. They do not depend on anyone else. They practice the four kinds of deep focus, but they do not stay stuck in that deep focus. ^4-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-60]]

They do not want fame. Their minds are not tangled in anger. Even if they live as a householder, they never cling to anything. To earn a living, they do not do terrible things. They do not use words of power to hurt others or to control women. ^4-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-61]]

They do not predict if beings caught in desire will be born as boys or girls. They stay quiet and focus on perfect wisdom. They do not fight or argue. Their mind of loving-kindness is strong. Because they want the state of knowing everything, their minds always turn to the teachings. ^4-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-62]]

They stay completely away from any rough borderland. They have no doubts about their path. They always stand as firm as Mount Meru. They would even give up their lives for the teaching. They work very hard at their training. You should know these are the signs of never turning back. ^4-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-63]]

Form, feeling, idea, intention, and awareness are very deep. By nature they are without signs and completely peaceful. Imagine trying to find the bottom of the ocean by shooting an arrow. In the same way, when you look with wisdom, you cannot find the bottom of these heaps. ^4-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-64]]

Think of an awakening hero who has become this deep. They ride in the highest, free vehicle. In the deepest truth, no heap, element, or sense door exists. When they understand this teaching, they perfect their stored-up goodness. What stored-up goodness could ever be greater than theirs? ^4-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-65]]

Think of a man caught up in things of wanting. He plans to meet a woman, but they miss each other. Think of how many busy thoughts fill his mind in a single day. An awakening hero will gain goodness built up over exactly that many ages. ^4-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-66]]

An awakening hero might practice generosity toward worthy ones and solitary Buddhas. They might keep good conduct for billions of years. But imagine someone else who speaks the teaching of the supreme perfect wisdom. That generosity and good conduct do not even match a tiny part of the goodness of speaking this teaching. ^4-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-67]]

An awakening hero might meditate on supreme wisdom. Then they get up and share the pure teaching. They dedicate this for the sake of all beings to help them reach awakening. In all the three worlds, there is no goodness equal to that. ^4-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-68]]

You must know that stored-up goodness is hollow and empty. It has no solid base and is without a core. If you practice the wisdom of the Well-Gone Ones like this, you will gather stored-up goodness that cannot be measured. ^4-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-69]]

The Buddha has clearly and widely explained the teaching. But you must know that all of this teaching is just words. Someone might speak about it for billions of ages. Even so, the expanse of all things never runs out and never grows larger. ^4-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-70]]

People talk about the perfection of the Victorious Ones. But even those things are said to be just a name. An awakening hero must dedicate their goodness without any self-importance. Then they will not fall back, and they will reach the highest awakening of a Buddha. ^4-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-71]]

Think about an oil lamp. When a flame first touches the wick, that first touch does not burn up the wick. Yet without it, the wick would not burn. When the flame touches the wick for the last time, that last touch does not burn up the wick either. Yet without that last flame, the wick would not burn. ^4-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-72]]

The first thought does not touch highest awakening. But without that first thought, you cannot touch it. The final mind does not reach peaceful awakening. But without that final mind, you cannot reach it. ^4-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-73]]

A seed grows into a sprout, then a flower, and then fruit. When the seed is gone, the tree is still there. In the same way, the first thought in your mind is the cause of awakening. When that first thought is gone, it does not mean awakening is gone. ^4-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-74]]

Drop by drop, a vase slowly fills with water. In the same way, the first thought in your mind is the cause of the highest awakening. By slowly building up every good quality, you perfect the quality of goodness and become a Buddha. ^4-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-75]]

They practice the things of emptiness, being without signs, and without wishes. They do not touch peace beyond sorrow, and they do not follow signs. They are like a skilled boatman going back and forth. He does not stay on either shore, and he does not stay in the ocean. ^4-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-76]]

An awakening hero who practices this way is not proud. They do not think, "I will get a prediction from the ten powers and I will touch awakening." They are also not afraid of awakening. They do not think, "There is nothing here at all." To practice this way is to practice the wisdom of the Well-Gone Ones. ^4-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-77]]

They see the wild paths of the world, along with hunger and sickness. They are not afraid, and they put on their armor. They always work hard until the very end of the future. They understand everything fully, and they never feel even a speck of a weary mind. ^4-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-78]]

The awakening hero practices the wisdom of the Victorious Ones. They know these heaps are empty and never born from the start. When they are not resting in deep thought, they show compassion for the realm of living beings. Even then, they do not lose a Buddha's qualities. ^4-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-79]]

Imagine a skilled person who has every good quality. They are strong, unbeatable, hard-working, and know many arts. They are experts at archery and crafts. They know how to make an illusion and deeply want the good of beings. ^4-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-80]]

This person takes his father, mother, and wife on a journey. They walk on a wild path full of enemies. The skilled person magically makes many brave men to protect them. They travel safely and return home again. It is just like that. ^4-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-81]]

In the same way, the clever awakening hero grows great compassion for the whole realm of living beings. They completely pass beyond the four Maras and the two lower levels. They stay in the highest deep calm. Yet, they do not touch awakening. ^4-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-82]]

Wind rests on space. A mass of water rests on the wind. This great earth rests on the water. All beings rest on the earth. This is the cause for living beings doing their deeds. Think about this meaning: what does space rest on? ^4-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-83]]

In the same way, the awakening hero rests in emptiness. They are held up by a wish that understands living beings among all beings. They show many different actions. They do not touch peace beyond sorrow. They also do not just stay in emptiness. ^4-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-84]]

A skilled and bright awakening hero practices this highest deep calm of peaceful emptiness. At that time, they do not build up signs. Resting without signs, they practice pure and perfect peace. ^4-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-85]]

Think of a bird flying in the sky. It has no place to rest up there, but it does not fall to the ground. An awakening hero practicing the doors to freedom is just like that bird. They do not touch peace beyond sorrow, and they do not grasp at signs. ^4-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-86]]

Think of a trained archer shooting an arrow into the sky. He shoots more arrows right behind it in a fast line. His new arrows keep the first one from falling to the ground. That first arrow will only fall when the archer wants it to. ^4-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-87]]

In the same way, someone might practice the highest perfect wisdom. They act using wisdom, clever ways, strength, and magic powers. They will not reach that highest emptiness until their roots of goodness are fully complete. ^4-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-88]]

Think of a monk who has the highest power of magic. He stays up in the sky and shows a wonder all at once. He appears to be walking, pacing, lying down, and sitting. He never gets tired, and he feels no weariness. ^4-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-89]]

In the same way, a skilled awakening hero rests in emptiness. They have perfected deep knowing and magic powers, but they do not stay anywhere. They show an endless number of actions to beings. Even after ten million ages, they never get tired or weary. ^4-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-90]]

Imagine someone standing at the edge of a high cliff. They hold two umbrellas in their hands and jump into the sky. They dive into the deep space below, but they do not fall. Instead, they glide through the air for a long way. ^4-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-91]]

In the same way, the wise awakening hero rests in compassion. They hold the two umbrellas of clever ways and wisdom. They see that things are empty, being without signs, and without wishes. They do not touch peace beyond sorrow, yet they still see things. ^4-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-92]]

Imagine a person who wants jewels. They travel to a jewel island and bring the treasures back home. That merchant does not enjoy the happiness all alone while leaving their family and friends sad. ^4-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-93]]

In the same way, an awakening hero goes to the jewel island of emptiness. There they gain deep focus, abilities, and powers. They do not just enjoy touching peace beyond sorrow by themselves. They do not leave other living beings behind in suffering. ^4-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-94]]

Think of a merchant who travels to learn the route. They visit every city, town, and village along the way. They do not stay there, and they do not stay on the jewel island. This wise person does not stay at home either, but becomes skilled in the path. ^4-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-95]]

In the same way, a brilliant awakening hero is skilled in the knowledge and freedom of a listener and a solitary Buddha. But they do not stay there, and they do not stay in a Buddha's deep knowing. They do not stay in what is not made by causes, but they understand the ways of the path. ^4-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-96]]

These heroes connect with beings through loving-kindness. They practice the deep calm of emptiness, being without signs, and without wishes. Could anyone say they will just reach a peace beyond sorrow? Or that they are made by causes? That is impossible. ^4-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-97]]

Think of a magically created human body. It is not invisible, and you can still call it by a name. In the same way, an awakening hero practices the doors to freedom. And they can still be called by a name. ^4-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-98]]

Someone might ask an awakening hero about their behavior and ability. What if the hero does not fully share the teaching of emptiness and being without signs? What if they do not explain the teaching of the level of never turning back? Then you should know they are not predicted. ^4-98

## The Full Understanding of the Peak ^5-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-1]]

Chapter Five shows the full understanding of the peak. These heroes do not want the level of a worthy one, the deep knowing of a solitary Buddha, or the three realms. They do not want them even in a dream! Instead, they see the Buddhas sharing the teaching with beings. You should know this is a prediction of never turning back. ^5-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-2]]

In a dream, they might see living beings stuck in the three lower realms. Right in that moment, they make a wish to put an end to the lower realms. They put out a huge fire through the blessing of truth. You should know this is a prediction of never turning back. ^5-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-3]]

There are many harmful spirits and sicknesses in the human world. A person with compassion can calm them through the blessing of truth. Even so, they feel no self-importance and do not show pride. You should know they have a prediction of never turning back. ^5-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-4]]

But maybe someone performs a blessing of truth and feels self-importance. They might proudly think, 'I have a prediction!' Or they might feel self-importance when other awakening heroes give them a prediction. Anyone who holds onto this self-importance has little understanding. ^5-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-5]]

Mara might approach you and use names to trick you. He will say, 'These are the names of you and your parents. These are the names of your ancestors for seven generations. When you become a Buddha, this will be your name.' ^5-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-6]]

Mara will tell you about your strict practices and your training. He will say, 'You had these same good qualities in the past.' An awakening hero might hear this and feel self-importance. If they do, they are being pushed by Mara and have little understanding. ^5-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-7]]

Someone might live far away in quiet villages, cities, and mountain valleys. They might stay deep in the forest or in hidden retreats. Even so, an awakening hero might praise themselves and put down others. You should know that such a person is pushed by Mara and has little understanding. ^5-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-8]]

They might always live right in the middle of busy villages and towns. But they only work toward awakening and helping living beings grow. They do not wish to become a worthy one or a solitary Buddha. This is what is called the quiet aloneness of a son of the Well-Gone One. ^5-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-9]]

An awakening hero might live for millions of years in a snake-filled mountain valley five hundred leagues wide. But if they do not understand true quiet aloneness, they will only end up feeling better than others and living mixed up in the world. ^5-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-10]]

An awakening hero might work hard for the benefit of beings. They might gain deep focus, powers, abilities, freedoms, and deep calm. But someone might look down on them and think, "This person does not practice in the quiet forest." The Victorious One has said that anyone who thinks this is living in Mara's domain. ^5-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-11]]

A person might live in a village or in a forest. If they are free from the mind of the two vehicles and sure of the highest awakening, this is the quiet aloneness of those who work for the good of beings. An awakening hero who judges them only ruins themselves. ^5-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-12]]

Therefore, a wise person seeking the highest awakening must have a strong purpose and completely destroy their pride. Think of how sick people rely on a doctor to get well. In the same way, this wise person should carefully rely on a wise friend. ^5-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-13]]

Buddhas and awakening heroes who have entered the highest awakening, along with the perfections, should be relied upon as wise friends. They are the teachers, and this is the level of accomplishment. By these two causes, a Buddha's awakening is quickly reached. ^5-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-14]]

Think of the Victorious Ones of the past, the future, and those living in the ten directions. This perfection is the path for all of them, and there is no other. For those who have entered into the highest awakening, this perfection is explained as their brightness, lamp, light, and greatest teacher. ^5-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-15]]

The defining feature of perfect wisdom is that it is empty. All things have this same defining feature. If you clearly know things are empty and without signs, you practice well. This is the wisdom practice of the Well-Gone Ones. ^5-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-16]]

Living beings have many thoughts and want food. Those attached to the cycle of rebirth wander forever. Both self and things of a self are not real and empty. Still, the unwise tie knots in the sky. ^5-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-17]]

For example, someone might have the idea that they swallowed poison. Even if no poison went inside them, they still faint. In the same way, the unwise believe in self and what belongs to self. Because of this false idea, they are born and die in a world that is always changing. ^5-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-18]]

Where there is clinging, I teach there is complete mental trouble. When you do not find self and what belongs to self, this is becoming completely clean. In truth, nothing gets mental trouble or becomes pure. Knowing this, the awakening hero realizes perfect wisdom. ^5-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-19]]

Imagine every single one of the living beings in Jambudvipa. Suppose they all form the wish for highest awakening. Suppose they practice generosity for millions of years. Then they dedicate all this as a cause of awakening to help beings. ^5-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-20]]

Now think of someone who tries hard to practice the highest perfect wisdom. They follow it for just one day. All that generosity from before cannot match a tiny part of this person's stored-up goodness. Therefore, you should always practice wisdom and never be lazy. ^5-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-21]]

A meditator who practices the highest perfect wisdom feels great compassion. Yet, they do not hold the idea of living beings. At that time, this wise person becomes a worthy recipient for all beings. They always consume their alms in a meaningful way. ^5-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-22]]

An awakening hero wants to set free the living beings in the god and human worlds and the three lower realms. To show these living beings the wide path to the other shore, they must strive day and night in perfect wisdom. ^5-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-23]]

Imagine a person who finally finds the highest jewel. Out of joy, they become careless and lose it right away. Because it is lost, they miss the jewel. They endure constant suffering. ^5-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-24]]

In the same way, someone seeking the highest awakening must not give up their training in perfect wisdom. Perfect wisdom is like a jewel. Think of a person who finds a jewel, wraps it carefully, and walks swiftly on their way to reach peace. ^5-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-25]]

The sun comes out from behind the clouds with a net of bright rays. As it rises, it destroys all the deep, pitch-black darkness. The sun outshines all the fireflies and all living creatures. It also outshines the stars and the light of the moon. ^5-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-26]]

In the same way, an awakening hero practices the highest perfect wisdom. They practice emptiness and act without signs. This wise person destroys the thick darkness of views. They outshine all beings, worthy ones, solitary Buddhas, and many other awakening heroes. ^5-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-27]]

Think of a prince who wants to give away his riches. He becomes a great leader, and people want to be near him. Even as a prince, he brings joy to many living beings. Imagine how much more he will do when he learns clever ways and becomes the king! ^5-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-28]]

In the same way, an awakening hero who is skilled in practicing wisdom gives the nectar of deathlessness. This brings joy to gods and humans. Even now, they work hard to help many living beings. Just imagine how much more they will do when they become a King of the Teaching! ^5-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-29]]

When this happens, Mara feels like he has been poked by a sharp thorn. He is filled with sadness, suffering, worry, and weakness. He wonders how he can make this awakening hero give up. Trying to scare them, he sets fires all around and throws flaming stars from the sky. ^5-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-30]]

These wise ones have very strong minds. Day and night, they look at the highest meaning of perfect wisdom. Then, their body, mind, and speech move as freely as a bird in the sky. How could any helper of the Dark One ever find a chance to stop them? ^5-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-31]]

But sometimes awakening heroes fight and argue. They get mad at each other and do not get along. When this happens, Mara rejoices and becomes very happy. He thinks, 'Those two will drift far away from the deep knowing of the Victorious Ones.' ^5-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-32]]

Those two will drift far away and act like flesh-eating monsters. They will both break their own promises. How could anyone reach awakening if they are angry and have no patience? At that time, the Maras and all their friends will celebrate. ^5-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-33]]

Suppose an awakening hero who has not yet received a prediction gets angry. If they start a fight with one who has received a prediction, they lose time. For every single moment they hold onto this bad thought, they must put on their armor and start over for that many ages. ^5-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-34]]

A Buddha reaches awakening through the perfection of patience. Good learners use careful attention and think, "This mind is not good." They own up to their mistakes and stop themselves from repeating them. They do not enjoy bad thoughts. In this way, they train in a Buddha's qualities. ^5-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-35]]

When doing their training, they do not cling to the training itself. They do not grasp at a learner or at the things to train in. They do not hold on to the ideas of training or not training. Anyone who practices like this is training in a Buddha's qualities. ^5-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-36]]

Any awakening hero who knows this training will never ruin it or lose their good conduct. They practice this to gain a Buddha's qualities. They are skilled in the higher training, and they know there is nothing to hold onto. ^5-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-37]]

By training in the wisdom of the wise light-makers, they do not have a single thought that is not good. Think of the sun moving across the sky. When the sun's bright rays shine, darkness cannot stay in the space before it. ^5-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-38]]

When someone trains in perfect wisdom, every other perfection is included right there. All sixty-two views are included inside the mistaken view of a self. In the same way, the Buddha teaches that all the other perfections are included inside this one. ^5-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-39]]

When the life force stops, every other ability stops too. In the same way, for the great wise ones who practice wisdom, all the perfections are gathered right here. ^5-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-40]]

Wise awakening heroes train in every good quality of a listener. They also train in all the good qualities of a solitary Buddha. They do not get stuck in them or wish for them. They just think, "This is what I must learn," and so they train. ^5-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-41]]

Some beings have fully entered into awakening with no turning back. If you truly rejoice in their making the wish to awaken, your goodness is huge. You could weigh Mount Meru of this vast system of worlds on a scale. But you cannot weigh the goodness of that rejoicing. ^5-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-42]]

Many living beings look for goodness and want to help others. One should also rejoice in the heap of stored-up goodness of them all. Because of this, once they gain every good quality of the Victorious Ones, they give the gift of teaching to the world to end suffering. ^5-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-43]]

An awakening hero without made-up thoughts fully knows all things. They know things are empty, without signs, and free from tangled thinking. They do not seek awakening with a wisdom that splits things in two. That meditator works hard in the highest perfect wisdom. ^5-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-44]]

The element of space and anything that goes against space do not exist. No one has ever attained it. In the same way, the awakening hero skilled in the practice of wisdom is like space. They practice complete peace. ^5-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-45]]

Think of a person made by magic standing in a crowd. This illusion does not think, "I must make these people happy." But they do it anyway! People see them showing off many magic powers. Yet they have no body, no mind, and not even a name. ^5-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-46]]

In the same way, those who practice wisdom never think, "After I reach awakening, I will completely free all beings." They take on many kinds of births and do many things. They show these things like an illusion. Yet they practice without fixed ideas. ^5-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-47]]

For example, a magic copy of a Buddha performs a Buddha's deed. But it feels no pride at all. In the same way, an awakening hero is skilled in practicing wisdom. They show all their actions like a magic creation and an illusion. ^5-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-48]]

A skilled carpenter makes figures that look like men and women. These figures can do all kinds of tasks. In the same way, an awakening hero is skilled in practicing wisdom. They do all their actions through deep knowing free of thoughts. ^5-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-49]]

When someone is skilled in practicing like this, a huge crowd of gods will put palms together. They bow down and show respect. There are many Buddhas in the world systems of the ten directions. All these Buddhas speak endless praise for their good qualities. ^5-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-50]]

Think of all the living beings in worlds as many as the Ganges. Suppose they all decided to become Maras. Suppose each one made even more Maras from every single hair! Even then, they could not make obstacles for the wise. ^5-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-51]]

A wise awakening hero is strong because of four causes. The four Maras cannot beat them or shake them. First, they rest in emptiness. Second, they never give up on living beings. Third, they do exactly what they say. Fourth, they have the blessing of the Well-Gone Ones. ^5-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-52]]

This perfect wisdom is the Mother of the Thus-Gone Ones. When it is taught, an awakening hero might wish to learn it. If they try hard to master it with a true heart, you should know something about this noble person. They have stepped onto the path of knowing everything. ^5-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-53]]

They do not stay stuck in the expanse of all things or the way things really are. Instead, they rest without resting, just like a cloud in the sky. Think of a holder of magic words flying through the air. If they want to rest, they use words of power to grow flowers out of season. Then they bless the spot just as they please. ^5-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-54]]

When practicing like this, a wise and bright awakening hero does not cling to the mind that understands. They do not cling to a Buddha's qualities. They do not grasp at a teacher, a person who wants the teaching, or someone with a certain view. This is how people live when they want peace and love every good quality. ^5-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-55]]

A listener or a solitary Buddha can rest in deep calm. Their rest is very peaceful and full of joy. But the way an awakening hero rests is even better than all of those. It is the very best, beaten only by the freedom of a worthy one or a Thus-Gone One. ^5-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-56]]

Birds live in the sky, but they do not fall. Fish live in the water, but they do not drown. In the same way, an awakening hero uses the power of deep focus to reach the far shore. They rest in emptiness, but they do not slip away into peace beyond sorrow. ^5-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-57]]

Some may wish to reach the highest good qualities of all living beings. They may want to touch a Buddha's deep knowing, which is supreme and wonderful. They may wish to give the best and holiest gift of teaching. To do this, they should rely on this perfect home of the helpers. ^5-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-58]]

The Guides teach about many trainings. But this training is the highest and best of them all. A wise person may wish to go to the far shore of all trainings. To do this, they should train in perfect wisdom. This is the training of the Buddhas. ^5-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-59]]

This is the supreme treasure of teaching and the holy treasury of the teaching. It is the Buddha family. It is a treasury of happiness and joy for beings. The Protectors of the world live in the past, the future, and the ten directions. All of them are born from this. Yet the expanse of all things never runs out. ^5-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-60]]

Think of all the trees, fruits, flowers, and forests. They all perfectly arise and come from the earth. Yet the earth itself is never used up, and it does not grow larger. It never shrinks. It is without fixed ideas, and it never grows tired. ^5-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-61]]

Think of the children of the Buddha, the listeners, the solitary Buddhas, and the gods. Think of all the happy and joyful things for beings. All of them arise from supreme perfect wisdom. Yet this wisdom is never used up, and it never grows larger. ^5-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-62]]

There are low, medium, and supreme living beings. The Well-Gone One taught that they all arise from ignorance. When helping causes come together, the machine of suffering fully arises. Yet that machine of ignorance is never used up, and it never grows larger. ^5-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-63]]

There are many kinds of deep knowing, approaches, gateways, clever ways, and roots. They all come from supreme perfect wisdom. When helping causes gather, the machine of deeds starts to work. Yet perfect wisdom never runs out, and it never grows larger. ^5-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-64]]

An awakening hero looks at things arising from causes together. Using this wisdom, they know things are never born and never end. A bright sun without clouds chases away the dark. Just like that sun, the hero destroys thick ignorance and reaches the state that has arisen by itself. ^5-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-65]]

These strong heroes rest in the four kinds of deep focus. But they do not lean on this deep focus or get stuck there. Even so, these four kinds of deep focus and every part of them help. They are the support for reaching supreme awakening. ^5-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-66]]

After reaching supreme wisdom, the awakening hero rests in deep focus. They also feel the four states of formless deep calm. This deep focus helps them reach supreme awakening. Yet the awakening hero does not train just to end their inner flaws. ^5-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-67]]

Here is a great wonder about those who gather every good quality. They rest in deep focus and deep calm, but they see these states are without signs. When their bodies finally die, they are born again in the desire realm exactly where they want to be. ^5-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-68]]

Imagine a person from Jambudvipa. They have never visited the great city of the gods. Later, this person travels there. They see the wonderful places owned by the gods. But when they return home, they do not feel attached to them. ^5-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-69]]

Awakening heroes hold the highest good qualities. They work hard at their training. They rest in deep focus and deep calm. Then they stay in the desire realm again without clinging to it. Like a lotus growing in water, they do not follow the ways of the unwise. ^5-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-70]]

The great heroes act only to bring living beings to maturity. They make the lands pure and finish the perfections. They think, "My good qualities of awakening and my perfections would fade away." So, they do not try to be born in the formless realm. ^5-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-71]]

Think of a person who finds a treasure of jewels. They do not feel any greed for it. Later, they take the treasure with them. Even after taking it home, they do not cling to it. ^5-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-72]]

In the same way, wise awakening heroes give joy and happiness to others. They reach the four peaceful states of deep focus and deep calm. But they let go of the happiness of that deep focus. Full of compassion, they enter the desire realm again. ^5-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-73]]

Suppose an awakening hero rests in deep calm and deep focus. But then they wish for the vehicle of a worthy one or a solitary Buddha. Then they are not settled in meditation. They become restless and have a wandering mind. A Buddha's good qualities completely fade away from them. They are like a boatman whose boat has crashed. ^5-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-74]]

They might experience form, sound, smell, taste, and touch. They might fully enjoy these five pleasures of the senses. But they stay away from the vehicle of the worthy one and the solitary Buddha. Instead, they find joy in the wish to awaken. You should know this hero is always resting in steady stillness. ^5-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-75]]

A pure awakening hero works hard in the perfection of steady effort for the sake of other living beings and persons. Think of a servant woman drawing water. She has no power of her own and must follow her master's orders. In the same way, these heroes act under the power of all living beings. ^5-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-76]]

Even if her master scolds or beats her all the time, the servant woman never talks back. She thinks, "He might even kill me!" Her mind is terrified, and she is overcome with fear. ^5-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-77]]

In the same way, those who walk the path to highest awakening for the sake of awakening should live like servants to all beings. This is how they reach awakening and perfect their good qualities. When a fire sparks from grass and wood, it burns up that very grass and wood. ^5-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-78]]

Let go of your own happiness and expect nothing in return. Work hard day and night for the needs of other living beings. Just as a mother takes care of her only child, you should help from the bottom of your heart without ever getting tired. ^5-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-79]]

An awakening hero might wish to stay in the cycle of rebirth for a long time. Such a meditator works hard to help living beings and to finish the cleansing of the land. If not even a tiny speck of tiredness enters their mind, they have the perfection of steady effort and are never careless. ^5-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-80]]

An unwise awakening hero counts tens of millions of ages. If they think reaching awakening will take a long time and bring suffering, they will feel suffering for a long time while practicing the true teaching. Because of this, their perfection of steady effort shrinks, and they are lazy. ^5-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-81]]

Think about the time from when they first form the wish for highest awakening until they reach the awakening that nothing surpasses. If they feel like that whole long time is just a single day and night, you should know that these clear-minded, wise people have begun their steady effort. ^5-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-82]]

Someone might tell them, "You will reach highest awakening only after you smash Mount Meru to pieces." If they feel tired just thinking about how big that mountain is, then that awakening hero has become lazy. ^5-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-83]]

But suppose they think, "What is so hard about that?" and decide to crush Mount Meru to dust in a single moment. That wise awakening hero has started their steady effort. Before long, they will reach the highest awakening of a Guide. ^5-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-84]]

They might think, "I will help beings grow ready, and then I will work for their good." If they work hard at this with their body, speech, and mind, but still hold onto the idea of a self, they have actually become lazy. They are as far away from knowing everything as the sky is from the earth. ^5-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-85]]

Whenever they have no idea of a body, a mind, or living beings, they turn away from all ideas and practice the teaching that is not two. The Benefactor taught that this is the perfection of steady effort for those who want the highest, peaceful awakening that never fades away. ^5-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-86]]

If they hear others saying bad things and using harsh words, the wise awakening hero thinks, "I am happy," and feels joy. They ask, "Who is speaking? Who is hearing? How is this said, what is being said, and why?" Having the highest perfection of patience, they are truly wise. ^5-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-87]]

Imagine someone fills a vast system of worlds with jewels. They offer these to the Buddhas, the Knowers of the World. They also give them to worthy ones and solitary Buddhas. This mass of generosity is great. But it cannot match even a tiny part of the stored-up goodness of an awakening hero who has true patience. ^5-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-88]]

The body of someone who rests in patience becomes completely pure. They gain the thirty-two marks and endless great strength. They speak the highest teaching of emptiness to living beings. Beings everywhere will feel joy to see this wise person who has patience. ^5-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-89]]

Suppose some living beings take sweet-smelling sandalwood. They rub it onto an awakening hero with great respect. At the same time, someone else drops burning coals on the hero's head. The awakening hero should keep an equal, calm mind toward both of them. ^5-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-90]]

The wise awakening hero practices patience in this way. After making the wish to awaken, they dedicate it to the highest awakening. By showing patience to the entire world, this hero shines brighter than the worthy ones. They outshine the solitary Buddhas and everyone in the realm of living beings. ^5-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-91]]

The person who practices patience should also think like this: 'There is much suffering in hell, as an animal, and in the world of Yama. I have helplessly suffered harm while chasing a pleasure of the senses. Why should I not practice patience now for the sake of awakening?' ^5-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-92]]

'People might use whips, sticks, and weapons. They might beat me, tie me up, or try to kill me. They might even cut off my head, ears, hands, feet, and nose. But I will face all the suffering in the world with patience!' Thinking this, the awakening hero rests in the perfection of patience. ^5-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-93]]

Through good conduct, those who want peace become noble. They stay in the realm of the ten powers, and their good conduct is perfect. They follow all the actions of self-control. To help all living beings, they dedicate these actions to awakening. ^5-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-94]]

Someone might want to reach the awakening of a worthy one or a solitary Buddha. This person is unwise and is lacking good conduct, and their behavior falls apart. But someone else might dedicate their actions to the highest peace of awakening. Then, even while enjoying the pleasure of the senses, they stay in the perfection of good conduct. ^5-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-95]]

Some things always make the good qualities of awakening appear. For people with good qualities, this is the goal of good conduct. Other things ruin the awakening of those who try to help others. The Guide says this is lacking good conduct. ^5-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-96]]

An awakening hero might enjoy the five pleasures of the senses. But they have gone for refuge to the Buddha, the teaching, and the noble community. They turn their minds toward knowing everything. They think, "I must become a Buddha." You should understand that this wise person stays in the perfection of good conduct. ^5-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-97]]

Someone might practice the ten paths of good action for ten million ages. But they might desire to become a worthy one or a solitary Buddha. When they do, a flaw appears in their good conduct, and their good conduct breaks down. Making the wish to awaken in that way is much worse than a defeat. ^5-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-98]]

An awakening hero guards their good conduct and will dedicate it to the highest awakening. They feel no self-importance about this and do not praise themselves. They completely let go of the idea of a self and the idea of living beings. It is said that this awakening hero stays in the perfection of good conduct. ^5-98

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-99]]

If an awakening hero walking the path of the Victorious Ones thinks, "These living beings are ones with good conduct, and those are lacking good conduct," they are completely lacking good conduct themselves because of this idea. Their own good conduct falls apart. They do not have perfectly pure good conduct. ^5-99

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-100]]

Some have no idea of a self and no idea of living beings. Since they are free from any idea and free from wanting, how could they be not holding back? They have no self-importance about their self-control or their lack of self-control. The Guide taught that this is the good conduct of self-control. ^5-100

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-101]]

Any pure living being who is one with good conduct does not focus on anything pleasant or unpleasant. Giving away their head, hands, and feet, they do not have a discouraged mind. Giving away everything they own, they are always free from holding on. ^5-101

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-102]]

They know that things have a nature of no self and are not solid. If they give away even their own flesh without a discouraged mind, why even mention giving away outer objects? It is impossible for them to act with stinginess. ^5-102

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-103]]

With an idea of a self, fools get attached by thinking of things as mine. How could they ever want to give? A stingy one will be born in the realm of hungry ghosts. Even if they are born as a human, they will be very poor. ^5-103

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-104]]

At that time, the awakening hero knows that living beings are poor. They focus on giving and always give freely. To them, the beautiful four continents are just like a blob of spit. They feel joyful when giving them away, but getting the continents would never bring such joy. ^5-104

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-105]]

The wise and bright awakening hero thinks of all the living beings in the three realms of existence. They practice generosity, thinking, "May this generosity be given!" They dedicate this to highest awakening for the sake of all beings. ^5-105

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-106]]

After they practice generosity, they do not hold onto things. They never hope for a result that ripens. Because they are so skilled at giving, they give away everything. When they give just a little, it becomes huge and cannot be measured. ^5-106

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-107]]

Imagine all the living beings in the three kinds of life. What if every single one of them gave gifts to a Knower of the World? Imagine they gave gifts to the Buddhas, the worthy ones, and the solitary Buddhas. Imagine they did this for endless ages while trying to become a listener. ^5-107

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-108]]

If a wise awakening hero, skilled in clever ways, rejoices in a good deed that stores up goodness and dedicates this stored-up goodness to the highest awakening for the sake of living beings, they outshine all beings by dedicating it. ^5-108

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-109]]

Think of a huge pile of glass jewels. It is completely outshone by a single precious lapis lazuli. It is the same with the huge heap of generosity from all those beings. It is totally outshone by a single awakening hero who chooses to rejoice. ^5-109

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-110]]

When an awakening hero gives gifts to beings, they do not say, "This good deed is mine!" They give away the object without holding back. Because of this, their root of goodness will grow with great power. It grows just like a bright, cloudless moon getting fuller in the night sky. ^5-110

## Teaching the step-by-step path. ^6-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-1]]

This is the sixth topic: teaching the step-by-step path. An awakening hero's generosity stops them from having a life as a hungry ghost. It ends poverty and all mental trouble. While practicing, they gain endless wealth. Through generosity, they help to fully mature living beings who face suffering. ^6-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-2]]

Through good conduct, they avoid a life as an animal. They leave behind the eight unfree states. Then they always find the freedom to practice. Through patience, they gain a great and beautiful body. They become golden and lovely, and others are happy to see them. ^6-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-3]]

Through steady effort, every quality of goodness stays strong. They find the treasury of the Victorious Ones, which is endless deep knowing. Through deep focus, they leave behind all bad desires. They completely gain knowledge, special knowing, and deep calm. ^6-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-4]]

Through wisdom, they fully know the nature of things. They completely go beyond all three realms. As the Supreme Leader of People, they turn the precious wheel. They give the teaching to beings to end their suffering. ^6-4
