# Like a Precious Wish-Fulfilling Jewel, the Ocean of Perfections: A Commentary on the Noble Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom ^0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^0-1]]

Herein is contained the commentary on the Noble Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom entitled Like a Precious Wish-Fulfilling Jewel, the Ocean of Perfections. ^0-1

Vajra body accomplished from the glory of the Mount Meru of the two accumulations, speech proclaiming the sixty branches like the voice of Brahmā, victorious mind profound, vast, and hard to fathom; I bow at the feet of that great sage, the lion, who possesses these and is supreme. I bow to the gurus of the lineage who realize the meaning of the supreme, wondrous perfection of wisdom from the entire ocean-like treasury of the Piṭakas in the sky of the profound and vast enlightened intent of the Jinas. The path traversed by all Sugatas of the three times, from among the eighty-four thousand true Dharmas of the supreme vehicle, is this jewel-like perfection of wisdom. Previously, those who wished to be wise did not know this essence and did not find this meaning of the supreme vehicle; therefore, for this very supreme meaning that is hard to find, a bodhisattva like me, Rangjung Dorje, shall flawlessly write down these words of the supreme meaning. Regarding this, there are two ways of explaining this sutra of the perfection of wisdom, of which this one teaches the genuine meaning based on experience according to the explanation method of the kusulu yogis. Regarding the Verse Summary of the Precious Qualities, a summary of the general preliminaries and its specific defining characteristics is taught. First, regarding those: the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha generated the mind directed toward awakening; in the middle, he accumulated the accumulations for three countless eons; and finally, he became manifestly and completely awakened, and until saṃsāra is emptied, he effortlessly and unceasingly accomplishes the inconceivable welfare of sentient beings. If one asks how all the dharmas spoken from the mouth of that Teacher's true Dharma are summarized, they are subsumed into two: the Dharma of transmission and the Dharma of realization. Accordingly, it is explained in the Abhidharmakośa: 'The true Dharma of the Teacher is of two kinds, having the nature of transmission and realization.' The Dharma of transmission also has two: it is subsumed into words and treatises. Accordingly, it is said in the Saṃdhinirmocanasūtra: 'All dharmas are subsumed into two: words and treatises, the well-spoken and commentaries on its intent.' Here, when treated in terms of the Buddha's words, it should be understood through three topics: the referent of the words, the definition of the words, and the classification of the instances of the words. Regarding the first from among the three: through the phrase 'the words were bestowed,' such as 'Listen...', it is asserted as words. Alternatively, the second applies to 'whatever speech the Buddha himself directly spoke as inconceivable piṭakas of Dharma from the condition of self.' First, regarding the classification by way of time, there are three types: the time of turning the first wheel of Dharma, the middle, and the manner of turning the final one—these are the three types. While there are inconceivable ways of turning the wheel of Dharma, the first word is called the turning of the wheel of Dharma of the four truths, which are the piṭakas of the true Dharma of Vinaya. The middle word is the turning of the wheel of Dharma of marklessness, which among the piṭakas is the piṭaka of the true Dharma of Sūtra. The final word is the turning of the wheel that thoroughly ascertains the ultimate, which is the piṭaka of the true Dharma of Abhidharma. Also, because it is the Buddha's word, there are three types: words spoken from the mouth, words of blessing, and words of authorization. Among them, the first is what is spoken from the very mouth of the Blessed One, the Lord of Sages; that is called his word. The second is when, through the blessing of the Blessed One himself, sounds resound from pieces of wood, pebbles, and the like; this is also called his word. The third is when the Tathāgata himself gave authorization, saying 'Speak!', to his retinue, such as the eight supreme great śrāvakas, and furthermore to bodhisattvas such as Avalokiteśvara; what they spoke also counts as his word. Also, regarding the Buddha's words, when classified by way of the subject matter, there are two: words of provisional meaning and words of definitive meaning. Regarding the first, the Vinaya and so forth are called words of provisional meaning. The One Hundred Thousand and so forth, the perfections of wisdom, are called words of definitive meaning. When classified by way of the means of expression, there are the twelve branches of scripture, such as sūtras. When classified by way of superior and inferior, there are two: the superior words of the Mahāyāna and the inferior words of the Hīnayāna. When classified by way of antidotes, the three piṭakas are taught: the Vinaya piṭaka as the antidote to desire, the Sūtra piṭaka as the antidote to hatred, and the Abhidharma piṭaka as the antidote to ignorance. The eighty-four thousand gateways of Dharma arising from them are taught as the antidotes to the eighty-four thousand mental afflictions. When classified by way of cause and effect, there are two: the words of Sūtra, which take the cause as the path, and the words of Tantra, which take the result as the path. Taking the cause as the path involves relying on the five perfections to seek the perfection of wisdom, as it is asserted; in this very perfection of wisdom it is taught: 'Dedicate generosity, discipline, patience, and likewise diligence, concentration, and wisdom completely to awakening.' The assertion of taking the result as the path is that by relying on wisdom itself, the five perfections are accomplished. Having thus taught merely the presentation of the general defining characteristics, if one asks what its specific classification is: among the three types of words, this is the word spoken from the mouth, spoken in a single breath. When treated in terms of the subject matter, it is of definitive meaning. When treated in terms of the means of expression, it belongs to the class of verses. From among superior and inferior, it is superior. From among the three antidotes, it is Sūtra. Regarding cause and effect, among the three times of the cause, it is the middle word, the turning of the wheel of Dharma of marklessness, which is the summary of its specific defining characteristics. In the language of India: Āryaprajñāpāramitāsañcayagāthā. In the language of Tibet: 'phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa sdud pa tshigs su bcad pa (The Noble Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom). Homage to Noble Mañjuśrī! So it is said. First, from 'In the language of India' up to 'Homage' is the excellent title, the meaning of the beginning. If one divides the languages of India, there are four types of languages. From among the four, it is specifically Sanskrit, the exalted language of the gods. Furthermore, applying it to this context, 'rgya' refers to the net of the five kinds of poisonous afflictions. As for 'gar', it is called 'gar' because of dancing away from them. As for 'skad du' (in the language), it is called 'skad du' because it is Sanskrit, the language of the gods, which is exalted above all other types of language. Furthermore, if one asks what that Sanskrit language of the gods is, it is 'Āryaprajñāpāramitāsañcayagāthā'. Moreover, if one asks what purpose there is in stating this: there is the purpose of stating the Indian language at the beginning, the purpose of establishing the Indian language as an authentic source, and the purpose of laying down habitual tendencies for Sanskrit, the language of the gods. As for 'In the language of Tibet', it is explained by translating from that very Sanskrit language of the gods. If one asks how it is translated: from the meaning of the title up to its complete conclusion. When the title itself is translated, 'Ārya' is Noble, 'Prajñā' is Wisdom, 'pāramitā' is Perfection, 'sañcaya' is Summary, and 'gāthā' is Verse. When those are combined, it is called 'The Noble Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom'. Furthermore, regarding that, there is the division by way of the method of assigning a title, and the identification of what is specifically assigned. Generally, as for the reasons for assigning a title: there are titles assigned based on an example, such as the Śālistamba Sūtra; titles assigned based on the ślokas, such as the One Hundred Thousand and the Twenty-Five Thousand; titles assigned based on chapters, such as the Eighth Chapter; titles assigned based on fascicles, such as 'fascicles or paths'; titles assigned based on location, such as the Laṅkāvatāra; titles assigned based on the author, such as Dharmottara's Pramāṇa; and titles assigned based on the requester, such as the Questions of the Old Woman or the Questions of Suvikrāntavikrāmī. From among those, this one is a title assigned based on meaning. Now, explaining the meaning of this very title: 'Noble' means having abandoned the faults such as the five kinds of poisons, and being endowed with excellent qualities, hence 'Noble'. 'Wisdom' is being exalted above others; knowing the conventional as illusion-like and the ultimate as such is wisdom. 'Perfection' is going to the other shore by being free from the concepts of the near shore, the extreme of cyclic existence. 'Summary' is gathering all the precious qualities, like a lodestone. 'Verse' is not being prose but being verses in the indravajrā meter, having eleven syllables. Furthermore, regarding this, if others say that there is no certainty in the meaning of the title, there is certainty, for this very text says: 'The name is the Precious Summary of Qualities, the path of awakening; / This path is explained so that all sentient beings may attain it.' Well then, if one asks what purpose there is in stating the Indian and Tibetan languages, the meaning of the title, and so forth in this way: there is the purpose of remembering the kindness of the translators, the purpose of knowing how to read the pronunciation, and the purpose of knowing translation if one accumulates many such instances of stating the Indian language at the beginning. Up to this point is the summarized meaning of the title. Although some state regarding this that it is an interpolation by the translators, it is stated following what was stated by some previous masters. As for 'Homage to Noble Mañjuśrīkumārabhūta!', homage is paid because he is the tutelary deity of the translators and paṇḍitas, in accordance with the subject matter. ^0-3

## The Perfect Setting Which Is the Cause for the Arising of the Sutra ^1-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^1-1]]

From 'Then' up to 'spoke': 'Then' means after the speaking of the One Hundred Thousand, the Eight Thousand, and so forth. 'Conquering' (bcom) refers to the four types of māras and all afflictions. Moreover, it is conquering the māra of the deity's son, the māra of the afflictions, the māra of the Lord of Death, the māra of the aggregates, and so forth. 'Endowed' (ldan pa) means being endowed with the six, such as lordship. Moreover, it is said: 'Endowed with the excellences of glory, renown, exalted wisdom, / Lordship, beautiful form, / And joyous effort: / One with these six is called "endowed".' By this, the Bhagavan's lordship and so forth are of the nature of being absolutely pure thus, devoid of all conceptual latencies; in order to effortlessly strive for the benefit of himself and others, he is also said to be endowed with the five types of exalted wisdom. That which is the excellence of renown and glory is the exalted wisdom of equality. This is because he is endowed with being the glory of all beings. Since the excellences of lordship and beautiful form are the very source of exalted wisdoms other than that, they are applied to the mirror-like [exalted wisdom]; that which is exalted wisdom is discriminating [exalted wisdom]; and joyous effort becomes the all-accomplishing exalted wisdom through the perfections themselves. For that very reason, he is 'endowed', and he is endowed with others as well. 'Transcended' ('das pa) is having gone beyond the near shore of cyclic existence, or all the eighty-four thousand afflictions indicated by it. By such a one, if one asks what kind of objects were relied upon: in order to bring great joy to gods, humans, and demigods, and laymen and laywomen, and monks and nuns, and so forth—those four retinues—at that time of teaching these six perfections such as the perfection of wisdom, he spoke and taught these three hundred verses in the indravajrā meter. ^1-1

### Teaching the Dharma by Way of Example and Meaning ^2-2-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-4]]

Thus it is stated. As for the example, it states 'Jambudvīpa' and so forth: In this Jambudvīpa, the four rivers Gaṅgā, Vakṣu, Sindhu, and Sītā flow along with their five hundred tributaries, and furthermore, however many inconceivable rivers such as those flow, and by the power of that, however many flowers, fruits, medicinal herbs, forests, and so forth are produced—all of those abide in dependence upon that nāga king residing in Lake Anavatapta. Therefore, for that reason, they arise from the power, glory, and merit of that lord of nāgas. Just like that example, however many śrāvakas of the Bhagavan, the Tathāgata himself, there are, such as Subhūti, however much Dharma they teach and extensively explain, and speak to other sentient beings endowed with pleasant words through sound reasoning, and similarly, being supremely distinguished from cyclic existence, attain the bliss of actualising nirvāṇa, and attain its fruit, and attain the fruits of a stream-enterer, a once-returner, a non-returner, and an arhat—all of those arise from the power of that being, the Tathāgata. If one asks why: with regard to whatever manner the Victor taught the turning of the wheel of Dharma of selflessness, those who became the disciples of the Buddha, the supreme of humans—the eight supreme great śrāvakas and so forth—having trained manifestly and having actualised the path and the fruit, extensively and authentically teach others as well, just as they themselves had trained. If one wonders from whose power this arose, it arises from the power of the blessings of the teacher, the Buddha himself; it did not arise from the power of those śrāvakas and so forth. If one asks why: it is because the Victor has thoroughly realised the wisdom of selflessness and reached perfection that it arises from him. ^2-4

### Teaching How to Train in the Six Perfections ^2-3-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-5]]

Regarding the meaning of these [verses] such as "..." and so forth, there are three [parts]: the nature of the six perfections, the sequence of practicing them, and the fruit of practicing them. First, there are three: the individual essence of each of the six perfections, the certainty of their number, and the certainty of their sequence. Regarding the first, there are also three [aspects]: By way of the definiendum, regarding what is called dānapāramitā, because it dispels poverty, it is called generosity. Its definition is the volition of non-attachment. The divisions of its basis of definition are the generosity of material things, of Dharma, and of giving fearlessness as refuge. The definiendum of the second is what is called śīlapāramitā; by the meaning of attaining coolness from the torment of afflictions, it is ethical discipline. The definition is the volition, along with its seeds, of turning away from misconduct. The divisions of its basis of definition are the ethical discipline of vows, of gathering virtuous dharmas, and of acting for the benefit of sentient beings. The definiendum of the third is what is called kṣāntipāramitā; by the meaning of bearing with anger, it is patience. The definition is being able to endure. The divisions of its basis of definition are the patience of voluntarily taking on suffering, of disregarding harm, and of being certain about the Dharma. The definiendum of the fourth is what is called vīryapāramitā; it is the joyous effort that engages in supreme qualities. The definition is manifest delight in virtue. The divisions of its basis of definition are armor, engagement, and not being satisfied. The definiendum of the fifth is what is called samādhipāramitā dhyānapāramitā; by the meaning of holding the mind, it is concentration. The definition is one-pointedness on an object. The divisions of its basis of definition are abiding in bliss, manifestly accomplishing qualities, and manifestly engaging in the purpose of concentration; thus it is concentration. The definiendum of the sixth is what is called prajñāpāramitā; by the meaning of knowing the ultimate truth, it is wisdom. The definition is the ultimate mind having reached the supreme limit. The divisions of its basis of definition are divided by way of the dominant condition: the wisdom of hearing, of thinking, and of meditating. Second, regarding the certainty of their number, there is the certainty of number in relation to accomplishing the purpose: the first three accomplish higher elevation, and the latter three accomplish definitive goodness; moreover, from among the three trainings, [the first three] are the training of ethical discipline, the last two are the trainings of mind and wisdom, and joyous effort acts as a companion to both. Furthermore, also in relation to acting for the benefit of oneself and others: the first three are for one's own benefit, the last two are for the benefit of others, and since joyous effort acts as a companion to both, their number is certain as six. Third, regarding the certainty of their sequence, from among three manners, by way of the sequence of cause and effect: because generosity gathers resources, ethical discipline isolated from desires arises; by turning away from harm through that and abiding in the four dharmas that make one an ascetic, patience arises; because one is thus endowed with patience, joyous effort that manifestly accomplishes virtue arises; because one is endowed with effort, concentration that places the mind one-pointedly on an object arises; and from the mind thus abiding altogether, the wisdom of superior insight realizing selflessness arises. Moreover, because it is said "ethical discipline is superior to generosity," there is the sequence of inferior and superior. Moreover, because ethical discipline is subtler than generosity, and so forth, there is the sequence of subtle and coarse. Thus they are certain. Now, it is this very manner of how the six perfections are taught. This should also be applied subsequently on their respective occasions. ^2-5

From "form is not" up to "abides in wisdom." This, perfectly speaking, is that the five aggregates are not established as form with the color and shape of apparent reality; likewise, feeling is also not [established], and for that bodhisattva acting from the wisdom in which experiencing, the discrimination that apprehends characteristics, and the volition that manifestly forms are not [established], there is not even an atom of an abiding place of truth for consciousness, and likewise, since he does not abide in all those entirely afflicted dharmas of cyclic existence and the completely purified dharmas of nirvāṇa, acting without abiding in the ultimate, the sugatas attain awakening on the ground of acting in the samādhi where all dharmas are thus ungrasped. Thus it is said: Regarding this from "The notion of self" up to "then become poor": Those without wisdom who have the notion of the truth of a self grasp at the entities of body and resources as "mine", and due to that attachment, how could those fools who do not know suchness have a mind of giving gifts? They do not. The fully ripened result of those misers who thus do not give is that they will be born in the abode of hungry ghosts. If, having emerged from there, they are born as humans, even then, due to the result corresponding to the cause, they will "become poor". ^2-302

###### Rejoicing in the Generosity of Others ^2-4-2-5-2-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-303]]

Thus it is said: ^2-303

###### Joyful Giving Due to Being Embraced by Compassion ^2-4-2-5-2-1-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-304]]

Regarding this from "At that time, the bodhisattva" up to "is not like that": At that time, bodhisattvas, having known that by not giving gifts sentient beings become hungry ghosts and poor, are seized by compassion, so their intention becomes inclined toward giving gifts, and in application they constantly give exceptionally without holding back. The reason for being capable of giving in that way is: having known that all the resources of the well-adorned four continents are like spit and dust, giving them to others and giving them away brings joy, whereas when others give to oneself, generating joy upon obtaining the continents "is not like that". ^2-304

###### Embraced by Skillful Means ^2-4-2-5-2-2-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-305]]

Thus it is said: Regarding this from "The bodhisattva" up to "dedicates to the supreme": Furthermore, if a bodhisattva who is skilled in means and has a clear mind gives a gift even to a single recipient, but with the intention, thinking "By the merit of having given this gift of resources, may it benefit all however many sentient beings there are in the three existences," that merit of giving is also dedicated to the goal to be attained, "supreme" awakening, in order to accomplish the welfare of beings. ^2-305

###### Giving for a Vast Purpose ^2-4-2-5-2-3-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-306]]

Thus it is said: Regarding this from "Having given gifts" up to "if one seeks well": Furthermore, "having given gifts", they do not abide in or become attached to material entities, and the way in which that bodhisattva never has any hope for the fully ripened result is: all those who are thus skilled in giving gifts give without attachment and hope regarding cause and result; and because the power of their intention is great, even giving a small cause results in a good, manifold, and immeasurable result. Since there are however many numbers of sentient beings without exception in the three existences—above the earth, below the earth, and on the earth—taking the limit of thorough examination, if all of them, having given excellent material gifts to the objects of generosity—the Buddhas who know the world, the arhats, and the pratyekabuddhas—for an infinite eon of time, "if one seeks well" the result of a śrāvaka, that is an inferior mind. ^2-306

###### Outshining Others ^2-4-2-5-2-4-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-307]]

Thus it is said: Regarding this from "The bodhisattva" up to "outshines": Furthermore, if whatever wise bodhisattva who is skilled in means because merit arises even without entities and application, having rejoiced in the entities of meritorious actions of those in saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, thoroughly dedicates that merit arisen from rejoicing as the cause of the most supreme awakening for the welfare of sentient beings, by that thorough dedication they outshine the virtue of all beings from śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas on down; for example, even if there is a great heap of glass jewels, a single precious lapis lazuli outshines all those collections of glass jewels, and just like that example, even the vast heaps of giving—with their vast objects, times, and entities—of all beings from śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas on down will be outshone by the bodhisattva's virtue arisen from rejoicing. ^2-307

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-308]]

Thus it is said: ^2-308

###### Summarizing the Merit of Generosity ^2-4-2-5-4-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-309]]

Regarding this from "If the bodhisattva" up to "like the waxing [moon]": Praising unattached giving by means of an example, if whatever bodhisattva, having given gifts to beings, does not appropriate the merit of having given gifts as their own, and has no holding back or miserliness regarding the entities to be given, those roots of virtue arisen from that unattached giving will increase into great power; for example, it is like the maṇḍala of the luminous, cloudless moon increasing higher and higher during the waxing phase. Up to there is taught the section on the sequential training detailing the meaning of how to train in the six perfections of the Mahāyāna. From teaching the result of training in that way, since the result of sequential meditation arises simultaneously in the mind-stream, the seventh section, the essence of the application of instantaneous manifest and complete awakening, is: whichever non-apprehension of the three—basis, path, and cause of the dharmakāya—shows the capacity of the mind is called the culmination, which means meditating on all white dharmas simultaneously. As it is also said: "Because even each one, such as giving, / Properly subsumes all / Undefiled ones, the Sage / Manifestly and completely awakens in a single instant." Thus, now here it is taught by way of six benefits; and according to Jñānapāda, the single line of verse below, "having completely perfected this dharma", teaches the seventh section, and from there on down is asserted as the eighth. For this, four divisions are taught. ^2-309

#### The Result of Practicing According to the Teachings ^2-4-3-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-310]]

Thus it is said: The bodhisattva ^2-310

##### Individual Temporary Results ^2-4-3-1-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-311]]

By means of this from "[The bodhisattva]" up to "will thoroughly transcend": By the giving of a bodhisattva practicing the six perfections as taught above, the continuity of birth of inferior beings going to the state of hungry ghosts is cut off, and even if born as humans, the poverty of resources and likewise all obscurations of afflictions are cut off in continuity, and when practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva in saṃsāra, they will attain infinite resources, and by giving gifts, having gathered sentient beings who suffer from poverty, they fully ripen them. By moral discipline, they abandon the essence of the manifold beings of the animal class and the eight states of non-leisure, and thereby they will constantly find leisure and the ten dharmas of endowment. The eight states of non-leisure are: "Hell-beings, hungry ghosts, and animals, / Barbarians, long-lived gods, / Those with wrong views, those empty of a Buddha, / And the mute—these are the eight non-leisures." From the ten endowments, the five personal endowments are: "Born in a central land, attaining a human body, / Having intact faculties, faith in the source, / And actions whose limits are not wrong." The five endowments from others are: "A Buddha having appeared, and having taught the dharma by him, / The teaching remaining for a long time," and thus it is said, "Entering it, and compassion for the welfare of others causing entry." By meditating on patience which is in accord with the body of pristine awareness, they will attain an excellent, supreme, and vast form capable of accomplishing the welfare of others, and by the example of being pleasing to the minds of others, like the color of gold, they will become attractive and worthy of being looked at by beings. By diligence which increases virtue, the white qualities that accomplish the welfare of others will not diminish, and they will find the resultant dharma of infinite pristine awareness and, in the end, the inexhaustible treasury of the precious qualities of a Victor. By concentration wherein the mind abides in bliss, since one is degraded if attached to the five qualities of desire, one will abandon them, and the divine eye, the recollection of past states, the exhaustion of defilements, the awareness of meaning, higher knowledges, and samādhis are accomplished. By the perfection of wisdom that realizes selflessness, having thoroughly known the nature of dharmas as emptiness, sentient beings of the three realms without exception "will thoroughly transcend" saṃsāra. The result preceded by the three causes in that way is the meaning of the eighth section, the result. ^2-311

##### Teaching the Common Results ^2-4-3-2-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-312]]

Second, teaching the common result: As for the essence of the dharmakāya, all purified dharmas of nirvāṇa are fully ripened, and because the realization of the non-apprehended abiding nature has reached its culmination, it becomes the basis for the perfection of the welfare of others. As it is also said: "The essence body together with the enjoyment body, / And likewise the other, the emanation body, / And the dharmakāya together with its activities— / Are perfectly expressed as the four types." Thus, here the dharmakāya together with its activities is taught by way of the four bodies. ^2-312

Thus it is said: Regarding this from "the leader of men" up to "completely apprehends": In accomplishing the welfare of others, "the leader of men," having attained Buddhahood, causes the precious wheel of dharma to be turned, and in order for the purpose of exhausting suffering, also thoroughly teaches the dharma to beings. ^2-313

Thus it is said: Thus, through listening to, contemplating, and meditating on this non-apprehending dharma of the path of the perfections, having completely perfected the result, that bodhisattva, by means of completely pure enlightened activity, "completely apprehends" the outer field of the vessel and the pure mind-streams of the inner contents, sentient beings, and also "completely apprehends" the mind-stream of the Buddhas, and the mind-stream of the dharma of transmission and realization, and likewise "completely apprehends" the mind-stream of the saṅgha who practices, and all virtuous dharmas. Up to there, the teaching of the nature of the dharma taught by that having been completed, ^2-314

### Summarizing the Meaning Taught by the Teacher ^2-5-0

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-315]]

Thus it is said: Regarding this from "healing the illness of beings" up to "taught this": The purpose of those taught above is "the illness of beings"; in order to introduce into the dharmatā of emptiness those individuals who do not know this non-apprehending dharmatā and have engaged in error, that omniscient teacher who "heals", who has become the supreme physician, "taught this", which is endowed with the profound path of awakening that teaches the wisdom that knows non-apprehension, and taught it perfectly. Up to there, the nature of the six perfections, the perfection of wisdom and so forth, has been taught. ^2-315

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-316]]

Thus it is said: Like a wish-fulfilling precious jewel that fulfills desires, gathering all the qualities of all the Buddhas of the three times, it is the perfect path of the great bodhisattvas which gathers all the qualities of the speech of the Tathāgatas, and because the essence of sentient beings is pervasively and primordially pure for all sentient beings, in order for all sentient beings to attain this path, the Tathāgata himself taught the meaning perfectly. ^2-316

![[bo-rangjung-dorje-tika#^2-317]]

Thus it is said: From the commentary on the noble Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom, like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel, called The Ocean of Perfections: this is the sequence of practicing the profound meaning of the supreme mind. In general, although there are inconceivable ways of explaining this sutra of the perfection of wisdom, because this mostly teaches the ultimate, by way of the system of teaching the five perfections incidentally to the perfection of wisdom, the perfection of wisdom acts as an accompaniment prior to the perfection of giving and likewise the five perfections; and although by way of other systems it sequentially begins from giving, and the perfection of wisdom follows by way of the sequence of principal and secondary, here it is because without the wisdom of selflessness one does not know how to enter sequentially. If one asks why that is so: wisdom precedes. If one asks why that is so: this is also taught in that way by the sutra of the Verse Summary of Precious Qualities in general, and specifically it is also explained: 'wisdom precedes the giving of gifts, and moral discipline, patience, diligence, and concentration are likewise.' Then, if one asks whether this perfection of wisdom does not arise subsequently, that is not so. It arises equally with the five perfections, and if categorized by way of the manner of realization, it also arises subsequently. That is the general presentation of the perfection of wisdom. The sutra of the precious Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom is completed. ^2-317
