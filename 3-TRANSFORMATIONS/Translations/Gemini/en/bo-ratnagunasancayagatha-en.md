## Teaching on the Knowledge of All Aspects ^1-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-1]]

Chapter One: The Teaching on the Knowledge of All Aspects.
However many rivers flow in this Jambudvīpa,
Producing flowers, fruits, medicinal herbs, and forests,
All depend upon the nāga king, the lord of nāgas, dwelling in Anavatapta;
It is the majestic power of that lord of nāgas. ^1-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-2]]

However much the śrāvakas of the Jina teach the Dharma,
Explaining and expressing it logically,
Creating supreme noble bliss and attaining its results,
All of that is the power of the person of the Tathāgata. ^1-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-3]]

Why is this? Because whatever methods of Dharma the Jina has taught,
The supreme of humans, having become his disciples, have trained in them,
And having actualized them, they teach according to what they have learned.
They do so by the Buddha's power, not by the power of their own strength. ^1-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-4]]

One who does not apprehend the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Does not apprehend the bodhisattva, and does not apprehend the mind of awakening;
Having heard this, the one who is without delusion and without fear,
That bodhisattva practices the wisdom of the Sugata. ^1-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-5]]

There is no form, no feeling, no perception, and no volition;
Consciousness does not dwell in them even slightly.
Not dwelling in all phenomena, they practice without dwelling,
And without grasping, they attain the awakening of the Sugatas. ^1-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-6]]

Just as the wanderer Śreṇika, through his knowledge,
Understood the aggregates as unapprehendable and subject to destruction;
Whatever bodhisattva understands phenomena in that way
Does not contact nirvāṇa, and dwells in wisdom. ^1-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-7]]

Furthermore, examining, "What is this wisdom, and whose is it?
From where does it come?" they realize all these phenomena to be empty.
Having closely examined, the one who is without despondency and without fear,
That bodhisattva is close to awakening. ^1-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-8]]

If, lacking understanding, one perceives form,
And practices regarding the aggregates of feeling, volition, and consciousness,
Even if they think "these aggregates are empty," the bodhisattva
Practices in signs, and does not have faith in the unborn state. ^1-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-9]]

Whoever does not practice regarding form, feeling, perception, or volition,
And does not practice regarding consciousness, practices without dwelling.
Not apprehending what is called "practicing," they rely on wisdom.
Possessing the intelligence of the unborn, they contact the supreme, peaceful samādhi. ^1-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-10]]

Whatever bodhisattva dwells here thus in peace,
Is prophesied by the Tathāgatas of the past.
They have no conceited thought, "I am in meditative equipoise" or "I have arisen."
Why is this? Because they completely understand the nature of phenomena. ^1-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-11]]

Practicing in this way, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas.
Because they thoroughly know that non-practicing is practicing,
Whatever phenomena they practice are also not apprehended.
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-12]]

Whatever does not exist is called "non-existent."
Fools, examining this, conceive of existence and non-existence.
Both existence and non-existence are non-existent phenomena;
Whatever bodhisattva knows this will definitely emerge. ^1-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-13]]

Whoever here knows the five aggregates to be like an illusion,
And does not make illusion one thing and the aggregates another,
Free from diverse perceptions and practicing in perfect peace—
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-14]]

Possessing spiritual friends and possessing profound insight,
Hearing the Mother of the Jinas, they will not be afraid.
Whoever possesses bad friends and depends upon others,
Is like an unfired clay vessel that collapses when it contacts water. ^1-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-15]]

Why are they called a "bodhisattva"?
They sever attachment, desiring the exhaustion of attachment to all things.
They contact the unattached awakening of the Jinas;
Therefore, they obtain the name of "bodhisattva." ^1-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-16]]

Why are they called a "great being"?
Because they become the supreme among great multitudes of sentient beings, and sever the great views of the realms of sentient beings;
Therefore, they are proclaimed to be a "great being." ^1-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-17]]

Great giving, great intelligence, and great power,
Entering into the supreme Great Vehicle of the Jinas,
Donning the great armor and subduing the illusions of Mara;
Therefore they are proclaimed to be a "great being." ^1-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-18]]

For example, if a magician manifests an illusion at a crossroads,
Beheading tens of millions of ordinary beings;
Whatever those to be killed are like, so too the bodhisattva,
Thoroughly knowing all beings to be like an illusion, has no fear. ^1-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-19]]

Form and perception, feeling and volition,
And consciousness are not bound, not liberated, and do not exist.
Entering into awakening in this way, without a discouraged mind,
This is the supreme armor of the noble persons. ^1-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-20]]

Why is this called the Great Vehicle of Awakening?
Mounting it, all sentient beings pass beyond sorrow.
This vehicle is a great immeasurable palace like space,
The supreme vehicle, which brings about the attainment of joy, happiness, and bliss. ^1-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-21]]

Whoever mounts it does not apprehend traveling in any direction.
Although said to go to nirvana, the going is not apprehended.
Just as an extinguished fire has nowhere to go,
For this reason it is proclaimed to be "nirvana." ^1-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-22]]

Bodhisattvas do not apprehend the past limit, the future limit,
And the present occurrence; the three times are pure.
They are unconditioned and free from conceptual proliferation;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-23]]

Whenever a wise, understanding bodhisattva
Reflects on non-arising and practices in this way,
Generating great compassion without the perception of sentient beings,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-24]]

If they generate the perception of sentient beings and the perception of suffering,
Thinking, "I shall act for the benefit of beings and eliminate their suffering,"
They are a bodhisattva who conceptualizes self and sentient beings;
This is not the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-25]]

Knowing all sentient beings to be like oneself,
Knowing all dharmas to be like all sentient beings,
Not conceptualizing both non-arising and arising,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-26]]

However many names of dharmas are proclaimed in the world,
Having abandoned arising and passing away regarding all of them,
One attains the supreme, deathless pristine wisdom, than which there is no other;
Therefore, this is called the perfection of wisdom. ^1-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-27]]

Any bodhisattva who practices in this way without doubt,
Endowed with wisdom, should be known to abide in equality;
Thoroughly knowing dharmas to be without nature,
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^1-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^1-28]]

The second chapter, teaching the knowledge of all paths. ^1-28

## Teaching on the Knowledge of All Paths ^2-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-1]]

Whoever does not abide in form, does not abide in feeling,
Whoever does not abide in perception, does not abide in volition,
And whoever does not abide in consciousness, abides in the nature of phenomena;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-2]]

Permanent and impermanent, happiness and suffering, attractive and unattractive,
Self and selflessness, suchness and likewise emptiness,
Attaining the fruit, and not abiding in the level of an arhat,
Not abiding in the level of a pratyekabuddha, and likewise not in the level of a buddha. ^2-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-3]]

Just as the Guide does not abide in the unconditioned realm,
Likewise not abiding in the conditioned, practicing without abiding;
Thus the bodhisattva abides in an abiding that is without abiding;
Abiding without abiding, this is taught as abiding by the Victorious One. ^2-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-4]]

Whoever thinks they will become a śrāvaka of the Sugata,
Or a pratyekabuddha, and likewise wishes to become a Dharma King,
Cannot attain it without relying on this patience,
Just as one cannot see a crossing to go from this shore to the other. ^2-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-5]]

The one who teaches the Dharma, the one who listens, and whatever is to be taught,
The attainment of the fruit, the pratyekabuddhas, and likewise the Protectors of the World,
And whatever nirvāṇa is attained by the brilliant wise ones—
All these are taught by the Tathāgata to be like an illusion. ^2-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-6]]

These four kinds of persons are not terrified by this:
The children of the Victorious One who are wise in the truth, those who are irreversible,
Arhats who have cleared away stains and abandoned doubt,
And fourth, those perfectly embraced by spiritual friends. ^2-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-7]]

A wise, understanding bodhisattva who practices in this way
Does not train for the level of an arhat, nor the level of a pratyekabuddha;
For the sake of omniscience itself, they train in the qualities of a buddha;
Whoever does not train in training and no-more-training, that is training. ^2-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-8]]

It is not for the sake of the increase, complete deterioration, or complete apprehension of form;
Not training for the sake of completely apprehending various dharmas,
Yet training and completely apprehending omniscience itself—
Whatever definite emergence this is, this is the training that rejoices in qualities. ^2-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-9]]

Form is not wisdom, in form there is no wisdom;
Consciousness, perception, feeling, and volition—these
Are not wisdom, nor is there wisdom in these;
This is equal to the realm of space, without difference. ^2-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-10]]

The nature of objects of apprehension is boundless;
Whatever the nature of sentient beings is, that too is boundless;
The nature of the realm of space is also boundless;
The wisdom of the Knower of the World is also boundless. ^2-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-11]]

The Guide has proclaimed that perception is this shore;
Destroying and abandoning perception, one goes to the other shore;
Those who are free from perception, attaining this,
Have reached the other shore and abide in the Teacher's word. ^2-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-12]]

Even if the Teacher were to remain for as many
Aeons as there are grains of sand, and proclaim the word "sentient being,"
Since they are pure from the very beginning, how could a sentient being arise?
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-13]]

When I, with this supreme perfection of wisdom,
Spoke in conformity, at that time the supreme being of the past,
Saying, "In a future time you will become a buddha"—
"I was prophesied," thus the Victorious One declared. ^2-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-14]]

The perfection of wisdom, in which the Protectors practice—
Whoever respects, apprehends, and masters it,
Will not be harmed by poison, weapons, fire, or water,
And Māra and the factions of Māra will find no opportunity against them. ^2-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-15]]

If someone, of the completely parinirvāṇized Sugata,
Were to build stūpas from the seven precious things and make offerings, and with those stūpas of the Sugata, as numerous as the Ganges'
Sands, were to fill thousands of millions of fields; ^2-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-16]]

And if, in infinite millions of fully conceived fields,
All of the sentient beings, however many reside there,
Doing no other work, with divine flowers, supreme incense, and
Ointments, were to make offerings for aeons of the three times or even more— ^2-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-17]]

Whoever, regarding that from which the Guides of ten powers will arise,
Writes this Mother of the Sugatas in a book,
Retains it, and pays reverence with flowers and ointments,
The merit of building stūpas and making offerings cannot match a fraction of that. ^2-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-18]]

This perfection of wisdom of the Victors is a great mantra;
It pacifies the dharmas of sorrow and suffering of the many realms of sentient beings.
Whichever Protectors of the World are in the past, and whichever are in the ten directions,
Having trained in this mantra, became unsurpassed kings of physicians. ^2-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-19]]

Those who practice the practices endowed with benefit and compassion,
The wise ones, having trained in this mantra, will touch awakening.
Whatever is conditioned happiness and unconditioned happiness,
One should know that all those forms of happiness arise from this. ^2-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-20]]

Seeds, having been sown and resting in the earth, will arise;
Having found the proper conditions, many kinds of forms are fully produced.
The five perfections and however many qualities of awakening there are,
All of them also are produced from the perfection of wisdom. ^2-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-21]]

Whichever path a Universal Monarch always travels upon,
By that very path, the seven precious things and all the troops proceed.
Wherever this perfection of wisdom of the Victors goes,
Along that very path, all qualities and dharmas will go. ^2-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-22]]

The Victor fully asked Śakra a question, and he replied:
"Even if there were buddha-fields as numerous as the sands of the Ganges,
And all of them were filled to the brim with the relics of the Victors,
I would take this very perfection of wisdom. ^2-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-23]]

"If asked why, it is not that I am disrespectful of the bodily relics;
But because they are fully permeated by wisdom, they are worshipped.
For example, just as a man relying on a king obtains reverence,
The relics of the Buddha rely on the perfection of wisdom. ^2-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-24]]

"A precious jewel, endowed with all qualities, is priceless;
Whichever casket it is placed in is worthy of homage;
Even if it is taken out, one takes joy in the casket;
Those qualities are those of that precious jewel." ^2-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-25]]

Likewise, through the qualities of the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Even though the Victors pass into nirvana, their relics gain worship.
Therefore, whoever wishes to hold the qualities of the Victors
Should take up the perfection of wisdom; this is liberation. ^2-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-26]]

Wisdom is what precedes giving;
Ethical discipline, patience, joyous effort, and concentration are likewise.
In order that virtuous dharmas are not wasted, it fully upholds them.
This demonstrates all dharmas as having a single way. ^2-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-27]]

For example, in Jambudvīpa there are thousands of tens of millions of trees,
Of various kinds, numerous types, and many forms;
Except when spoken of as a number of shadows,
There are no various shadows, nor are there differences. ^2-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-28]]

Likewise, these five perfections of the Victors also
Will attain the very name of the perfection of wisdom.
When fully dedicated for the sake of omniscience,
All these six become of one taste, called awakening. ^2-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-29]]

If a bodhisattva, while lacking full understanding,
Fully demonstrates form, perception, feeling, volition, and consciousness
As impermanent, he acts upon fabrication;
The wise never destroy dharmas. ^2-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-30]]

Where form is not, feeling is not, and perception is not apprehended,
Consciousness is not, and volition is not apprehended,
And all dharmas are fully known in the manner of being unarisen and empty:
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-31]]

Compared to someone who, in fields as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges,
Tames all however many beings reside there to arhatship,
Whoever writes down this perfection of wisdom
And gives the book to supreme beings has particularly exalted merit. ^2-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-32]]

If asked why: those supreme speakers, having trained in this,
Demonstrate all dharmas here as emptiness.
Hearing that, śrāvakas swiftly touch complete liberation,
And touch the awakening of pratyekabuddhas and the awakening of buddhas. ^2-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-33]]

If the emergence of a tree in the world never happens without a sprout,
How could branches, leaves, flowers, and fruits emerge on it?
If the emergence of a Victor in the world does not exist without the mind of awakening,
How could the fruits of Śakra, Brahmā, and śrāvakas emerge? ^2-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-34]]

When the sun's disk emits its web of light,
At that time beings exert themselves in doing their work; likewise,
When the mind of awakening emerges in the world for the sake of the knowledge of the wise,
Through pristine knowledge beings become endowed with virtuous dharmas. ^2-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-35]]

For example, if there were no nāga lord at Lake Anavatapta,
How could rivers flow here in Jambudvīpa?
If there were no rivers, flowers and fruits would not emerge,
And the ocean would also not have many forms of jewels. ^2-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-36]]

Likewise, here too, without the mind of awakening, how could
The pristine knowledge of the Sugata emerge in all these worlds?
Without pristine knowledge, there is no increase of qualities and no awakening,
And the buddha-dharmas, which are like the ocean, would also not exist. ^2-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-37]]

Compared to however many illuminating creatures there are in this world,
All emitting their lights in order to illuminate,
A single ray of light emitted by the sun's disk is far superior;
All the lights of the host of illuminating creatures cannot match it. ^2-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-38]]

Even if however many hosts of śrāvakas generate collections of merit,
Fully endowed with generosity, ethical discipline, and meditation,
Compared to a single mind of rejoicing of a bodhisattva,
The heap of merit of the hosts of śrāvakas cannot match it. ^2-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-39]]

Whatever tens of millions of hundreds of billions of buddhas of the past,
And whoever resides in many infinite thousands of tens of millions of fields,
And whatever protectors of the world who have passed into nirvāṇa,
Demonstrate the precious dharma in order to exhaust suffering: ^2-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-40]]

From when they first generated the mind for supreme awakening,
Until the time the holy dharma of those Guides is exhausted,
Whatever merit those Victors have during that time,
And whatever is endowed with the perfections and whatever buddha-dharmas— ^2-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-41]]

And whatever [virtues] of the sons of the buddhas, and the śrāvakas,
The virtues of learners and non-learners, with defilements and without defilements,
Having gathered them, the bodhisattva rejoices,
And dedicates them all for the sake of awakening, as a cause for the benefit of wandering beings. ^2-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-42]]

If, when dedicating, the notion of 'mind' arises,
If there is the notion of awakening, the notion of dedicating, and the notion of sentient beings,
Because of these notions, residing in views, the mind is attached to the three;
Since there are apprehensions, it does not amount to dedication. ^2-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-43]]

If in this way one knows that this dharma has ceased and is exhausted,
And that to which it is dedicated is also exhausted,
And knows that a dharma is never dedicated to a dharma,
Through knowing in that way, it is perfectly dedicated. ^2-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-44]]

If one acts upon signs, that is not dedication;
But if there are no signs, it is dedication to awakening.
Just like eating excellent food mixed with poison,
The Victor has declared that apprehending white dharmas is like that. ^2-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-45]]

Therefore, one should train in dedicating in this way:
Just as those Victors know that virtue—
Whatever its aspect, whatever its origin, whatever its defining characteristic—
Rejoicing in it, one perfectly dedicates it in exactly that way. ^2-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-46]]

If one perfectly dedicates merit to awakening in that way,
It is without poison, one does not abandon the Buddha, and speaks what the Victor has spoken.
Dedicating perfectly in that way, the hero outshines all
Of however many bodhisattvas there are in the world who possess apprehension. ^2-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-47]]

Hundreds of billions of blind people without a guide,
Not even knowing the way, how could they enter the city?
Without wisdom, these five blind perfections,
Being without a guide, are unable to touch awakening. ^2-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-48]]

When they are fully embraced by wisdom,
Then they gain eyes and obtain this name.
For example, an artwork that is completed but lacks eyes
Earns no payment as long as the eyes are not painted. ^2-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-49]]

When conditioned, unconditioned, and white and black dharmas
Are broken down by wisdom and not even a particle is apprehended,
In the world it is reckoned as the perfection of wisdom;
It is like space, which does not abide anywhere at all. ^2-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-50]]

If one thinks, "I shall practice the wisdom of the Victors,
And I shall liberate many hundreds of billions of sentient beings touched by suffering,"
The bodhisattva entirely imagines the notion of sentient beings;
This is not practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^2-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-51]]

When a bodhisattva practiced previously,
The wise one practicing this perfection has no doubt.
As soon as they hear it, they give rise to the notion of the Teacher;
They will swiftly realize peaceful awakening. ^2-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-52]]

Even if they honored hundreds of billions of buddhas when practicing previously,
If they do not have faith in the perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
Upon hearing it, those of small intelligence will abandon it;
Having abandoned it, becoming without refuge, they go to the Avīci hell. ^2-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-53]]

Therefore, if the supreme pristine wisdom of a buddha
One wishes to touch, generate faith in this Mother of the Victors!
For example, having gone to the island of jewels, a merchant
Returning bereft of goods—it is not fitting to become like that. ^2-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^2-54]]

The purity of form should be known as the purity of the fruit;
The purity of the fruit and form becomes the purity of omniscience.
The purity of omniscience, the purity of the fruit, and the purity of form
Are equal to the realm of space, indivisible and unseverable. ^2-54

## Teaching on the Knowledge of All Bases ^3-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-1]]

The third chapter: teaching the knowledge of all bases.
Through the perfection of wisdom, wherever the hero practices,
They completely transcend the three realms, yet are not liberated;
Though afflictions are cleared away, they display birth;
Though without aging, sickness, and death, they display death and transmigration. ^3-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-2]]

These wandering beings are attached to the mud of name and form;
They wander in the wheel of samsara like a wheel of wind.
Knowing that deluded beings are like deer caught in a snare,
The wise one roams like a bird in the sky. ^3-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-3]]

Whoever, with perfectly pure conduct, does not practice in form,
And does not practice in consciousness, perception, feeling, and volition,
Practicing like that, entirely abandoning all attachment,
Liberated from attachment, they practice the wisdom of the Sugatas. ^3-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-4]]

The wise and luminous bodhisattva practicing like that,
Having cut off attachment, goes among beings without attachment,
Remaining brightly like the sun freed from the eclipse of Rāhu,
And like a spreading fire burning grass, trees, and forests. ^3-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^3-5]]

All dharmas being naturally pure and entirely pure,
If the bodhisattva views the perfection of wisdom,
Not apprehending an agent and not apprehending all dharmas,
This is practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^3-5

## Teaching on the Clear Realization of All Aspects ^4-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-1]]

The fourth chapter: teaching the complete realization of all aspects.
The king of gods, Śakra, asked the Victor:
"How does a bodhisattva strive in the practice of wisdom?"
"They do not strive even a speck of dust in the aggregates and elements;
Not striving in the aggregates, that is the striving of a bodhisattva." ^4-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-2]]

Whoever, having heard that these dharmas are like emanations and illusions,
Without doubt, trains and continuously applies themselves,
That being should be known to have entered the Great Vehicle long ago,
And to have served many tens of millions of billions of Buddhas. ^4-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-3]]

When a person who has entered a wilderness path of many yojanas
Sees cowherds, boundary markers, and excellent forests,
They think, 'These are signs that a village or city is near';
They find relief and have no fear of thieves and robbers. ^4-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-4]]

Likewise, when seeking enlightenment, whoever attains the hearing
Of this perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
They attain relief and become fearless—
Not of the level of an arhat, nor the level of a pratyekabuddha. ^4-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-5]]

When a person traveling to see the water of the ocean
Sees trees, forests, and mountains, it is still far;
But when they do not see these signs, they think, 'The great ocean
Has become near,' and they have no doubt about it. ^4-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-6]]

Likewise, one who has fully entered supreme enlightenment
Should be known as hearing this perfection of wisdom of the Victors;
Even if they have not been directly prophesied by the Leaders,
They think, 'Before long, I will touch the enlightenment of the Buddhas.' ^4-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-7]]

In the excellent springtime, when trees shed their leaves,
Before long, the branches will produce leaves, fruits, and flowers;
Whoever attains this perfection of wisdom in their hands
Will attain the enlightenment of the Leaders before long. ^4-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-8]]

For example, when a pregnant woman is afflicted with pain,
It is said that the time for her to give birth has arrived;
Likewise, when a bodhisattva hears the wisdom of the Victors
And generates joy and aspiration, they will swiftly touch enlightenment. ^4-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-9]]

When practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom, the yogi
Does not see the increase or complete decrease of form;
Whoever does not see dharma, non-dharma, or the dharmadhātu,
And does not touch nirvāṇa, they abide in wisdom. ^4-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-10]]

Whoever practices here does not conceptualize the qualities of a Buddha;
They do not conceptualize the powers, the bases of miraculous power, or peaceful enlightenment;
Non-conceptualizing and free from concepts, they practice through blessing;
This is the practice of the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^4-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-11]]

Subhūti asked the Buddha, the moon among speakers:
What are the obstacles for those who delight in qualities?
The Teacher replied: There are many obstacles;
Of those, I will fully explain just a few for now. ^4-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-12]]

When copying this perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
Various differing modes of confidence will arise;
Yet without accomplishing the benefit of beings, swiftly like lightning,
They will completely fade away; this is the work of Māra. ^4-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-13]]

When this is taught, some will harbor doubts, thinking,
'The Leader has not fully pronounced my name here,
Nor fully pronounced my lineage, birthplace, or clan.'
They will not listen and will abandon it; this is the work of Māra. ^4-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-14]]

Not knowing thus, having discarded the root,
Out of delusion they will search for branches and leaves.
It is like finding an elephant but searching for the elephant's footprints;
Having heard the perfection of wisdom, searching for other sūtras is just like that. ^4-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-15]]

For example, just as someone who has found food with a hundred flavors,
Having found supreme food, searches for inferior food;
A bodhisattva who has found this perfection of wisdom
But seeks enlightenment through the level of an arhat is just like that. ^4-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-16]]

Desiring respect and desiring gain,
With a mind full of views, they become intimate with families;
Having forsaken the dharma, they will practice non-dharma activities;
Having discarded the path, going on wrong paths—this is the work of Māra. ^4-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-17]]

Those who at that time, having become eager and faithful,
Go to listen to this holy dharma,
Upon knowing that the speaker of dharma is busy with activities,
Will depart, devoid of joy and unhappy. ^4-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-18]]

At that time, this work of Māra will arise:
At that time, whoever disturbs many monks,
They will not uphold this perfection of wisdom;
And many other various obstacles will also arise. ^4-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-19]]

Whatever priceless jewels may be found,
They are rare, and there is also always much harm at all times.
Likewise, the supreme perfection of wisdom of the Victors,
This precious dharma, is rare, and there is also always much harm. ^4-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-20]]

Sentient beings of small intellect who have newly entered the vehicle,
Who have not found this rare jewel—
In order to create obstacles for them, Māra will become energetic;
But the Buddhas of the ten directions will apply themselves to fully protecting them. ^4-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-21]]

When a mother becomes sick and has many sons,
They all become unhappy and attend upon her.
Likewise, the Buddhas of the world systems in the ten directions also
Keep in mind this holy wisdom, which is their mother. ^4-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-22]]

Those protectors of the world in the past and in the ten directions,
And those who will appear in future times, arise from this.
She is the mother who gives birth to the Victors, the teachers of the world,
And she reveals the mental conduct of other sentient beings. ^4-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-23]]

The suchness of the world and the suchness of the arhats,
The suchness of the pratyekabuddhas and the suchness of the sons of the Victors:
Are oneness, devoid of entities, not other; they are suchness;
The perfection of wisdom is known by the Tathāgata. ^4-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-24]]

Whether the wise remain in the world or have passed into nirvāṇa,
This flawless dharmatā, the emptiness of dharmas, remains;
Bodhisattvas subsequently realize this suchness,
Therefore, the Buddhas are given the name Tathāgata. ^4-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-25]]

Relying on the perfection of wisdom as a pleasure grove,
This is the domain of the guides with the ten powers;
Even though they fully extract sentient beings from the suffering of the three lower realms,
They never have the notion of "sentient beings". ^4-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-26]]

For example, just as a fearless lion dwelling in a mountain ravine
Terrifies many small wild animals and lets out a roar,
The lion of men, relying on the perfection of wisdom,
Terrifies many tīrthikas and also lets out a roar in the world. ^4-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-27]]

For example, just as the rays of the sun dwelling in the sky
Dry up this earth and also clearly reveal forms,
Likewise, the dharma king, relying on the perfection of wisdom,
Dries up the river of craving and also clearly reveals the dharma. ^4-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-28]]

Not seeing forms, and also not seeing feelings,
Not seeing perceptions, and not seeing formations,
For whom consciousness, mind, and intellect are not seen—
The Tathāgata has taught that this is seeing the dharma. ^4-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-29]]

Sentient beings express in words, "Space is seen";
Examine this meaning: how is space seen?
The Tathāgata has taught that seeing the dharma is like that;
Seeing cannot be explained by any other example. ^4-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-30]]

Whoever sees in that way sees all dharmas.
Just as a king sits in equanimity while his ministers do everything,
Whatever deeds of the Buddhas and dharmas of the śrāvakas there are,
All of them are done by the perfection of wisdom. ^4-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-31]]

Just as a king does not go to villages and does not go to provinces,
But gathers all the revenue from his own realm,
The dharmatā of the bodhisattvas does not go anywhere,
But gathers all the qualities of the Buddha grounds. ^4-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-32]]

Whichever bodhisattva has firm faith in the Sugata,
And applies their mind to the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Having entirely transcended the two grounds of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
Will quickly attain the unconquerable enlightenment of the Victors. ^4-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-33]]

For example, when a boat entering the ocean breaks,
Whoever does not hold onto a corpse, grass, or wood,
Will perish in the middle of the water and will not reach the shore;
Whoever holds onto them will reach the other side of the ocean. ^4-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-34]]

Likewise, whoever is endowed with faith and has obtained pure trust,
But does not follow the mother, the perfection of wisdom, ^4-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-35]]

If they are fully embraced by the qualities of skillful means and wisdom,
They will quickly touch the supremely wondrous enlightenment of the Sugata. ^4-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-36]]

For example, if someone draws water in an unfired clay pot,
Because it is fragile, one should know it will quickly break.
Regarding breaking on the path when carrying water in a fired pot,
Without breaking, it will safely arrive at the house. ^4-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-37]]

Likewise, although a bodhisattva may have much faith,
If they are completely lacking in wisdom, they will quickly become unfit.
If their faith is fully embraced by wisdom,
Having entirely transcended the two grounds, they will attain supreme enlightenment. ^4-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-38]]

For example, a boat that has not been well-built, when in the ocean,
Will be destroyed along with its wealth and merchants.
If that boat has been well-built and is sturdy,
It will not be destroyed, and will reach the shore along with its wealth. ^4-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-39]]

Likewise, although a bodhisattva is fully infused with faith,
If they lack wisdom, they will quickly fall away from enlightenment.
If they are endowed with the supreme perfection of wisdom,
They will reach the unharmed, unequalled enlightenment of the Victors. ^4-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-40]]

An old and suffering person who has reached one hundred and twenty years of age,
Even if they stand up, they are not able to walk by themselves;
But if they are supported by people on both their left and right,
They will walk easily without the fear of falling. ^4-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-41]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva whose power of wisdom is small,
Even if they fully engage, will completely fall away.
If they are fully embraced by skillful means and supreme wisdom,
Without falling away, they will touch the enlightenment of the Tathāgata. ^4-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-42]]

Any bodhisattva abiding on the ground of a beginner,
Who has set out for the supreme enlightenment of a Buddha with a pure intention,
As good disciples possessing devotion to their guru,
They should always rely on wise gurus. ^4-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-43]]

If one asks why, it is because the qualities of the wise arise from them;
They teach the perfection of wisdom.
"The dharmas of a Buddha depend on spiritual friends"—
So spoke the Victor, who possesses the supreme of all qualities. ^4-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-44]]

"Completely dedicate generosity, discipline, patience, as well as diligence,
Concentration, and wisdom to enlightenment.
Do not hold supreme views by being attached to enlightenment or the aggregates"—
They should instruct beginners in that way. ^4-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-45]]

Practicing thus, those oceans of goodness, moons of speech,
Are the refuge, the defender, and the resting place of beings;
They are the support, the mind, the island, and the supreme guides seeking their welfare;
They are illuminating lamps, speakers of the supreme dharma, and are unperturbed. ^4-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-46]]

Those endowed with great renown don the armor that is difficult to do;
It is not the armor of the aggregates, elements, or sense bases.
They are free from the perception of the three vehicles and have no grasping.
They are irreversible, unwavering, and of unperturbed nature. ^4-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-47]]

They are endowed with such dharmas and are free from conceptual proliferation.
They are free from doubt, hesitation, and wavering, and possess the ultimate meaning.
Those who, having heard the perfection of wisdom, do not shrink back,
Should be known as irreversible and not reliant on others. ^4-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-48]]

This dharma of the Guides is profound and difficult to see;
It is not realized by anyone, nor is it to be attained.
Therefore, the Benefactor, the Compassionate One, having discovered enlightenment,
Showed little inclination, thinking, "Who among the multitudes of beings will understand?" ^4-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-49]]

Beings delight in resting places and desire sense objects;
Abiding in grasping, the unwise are foolish and obscured like darkness.
The dharma to be attained is without resting place and without grasping;
Therefore, conflict arises with the worlds. ^4-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-50]]

The element of space in the eastern direction and the southern direction,
And likewise in the western direction and the northern direction, is boundless;
Above, below, and in as many of the ten directions as there are, it exists;
There is no difference and there is no distinction. ^4-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-51]]

Whatever is the suchness of the past, the suchness of the future,
Whatever is the suchness of the present, the suchness of an arhat,
Whatever is the suchness of all dharmas, the suchness of a Victor—
All this suchness of dharmas is without distinction. ^4-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-52]]

The enlightenment of the Sugatas is free from differentiated dharmas;
Any bodhisattva who desires to attain this,
Even if endowed with skillful means, will not attain it without the guiding wisdom of the perfection of wisdom. ^4-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-53]]

If a bird with a large body of one hundred and fifty yojanas,
Whose wings are worn out, lacks strength and skill,
And jumps from the abode of the Thirty-Three to this Jambudvīpa,
In jumping itself, it will come to ruin there. ^4-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-54]]

Even if these five perfections of the Victors
Have been accomplished for many koṭis of niyutas of eons,
And one has constantly relied on the world with boundless, vast aspirations,
Lacking skillful means and devoid of wisdom, one will fall into the level of a śrāvaka. ^4-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-55]]

Whoever desires to definitely emerge in this vehicle of the Buddha,
With an even mind toward all beings, perceiving them as parents,
Should act with an attitude of benefit and a mind of loving-kindness,
Be free from rigidity, straightforward, and speak gentle words. ^4-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-56]]

The elder Subhūti thoroughly requested the Protector of the World:
"Ocean of Qualities, please teach the signs of those free from afflictions.
How those of great power will not turn back again—
Thus, may the Victor explain just a fraction of their qualities." ^4-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-57]]

Free from differentiated perceptions and speaking rational words,
Not relying on other śramaṇas and brahmins;
The wise always abandon the three lower realms at all times,
And vividly strive in the path of the ten virtuous actions. ^4-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-58]]

Without material motives, they subsequently teach the dharma to beings.
Singly rejoicing in the dharma, they are always endowed with gentle words.
They are fully endowed with clear comprehension when going, walking, sleeping, and sitting.
Looking ahead only the distance of a yoke, they walk without a wandering mind. ^4-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-59]]

Making themselves clean and wearing clean clothes, the three are isolated and pure;
Without desire for gain, the supreme leaders always desire the dharma.
Passed beyond the realm of Māra and not placing themselves under the control of others,
Meditating on the four dhyānas, they do not abide in those dhyānas. ^4-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-60]]

Not desiring fame, their minds are not wrapped by anger.
Even if they are householders, they are always unattached to all things.
For the sake of livelihood, they do not seek resources through appalling means.
They do not practice mantras of fierce actions, nor mantras for uniting men and women. ^4-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-61]]

They do not predict, saying, 'You will become a man or a woman engaged in actions of desire.'
In the utterly isolated perfection of wisdom, they are free from conflict and dispute, and their mind of loving-kindness is firm.
Desiring omniscience, they are steadfast and always endowed with a mind inclined toward it. ^4-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-62]]

They completely abandon the borderlands of uncivilized border-dwellers.
Without self-doubt, they are always like Mount Sumeru.
Giving up even their lives for the sake of the dharma, they vividly strive in yoga.
One should know that these are the signs of an irreversible one. ^4-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-63]]

Form, feeling, perception, volition, and consciousness
Are profound, naturally signless, and utterly peaceful;
For example, like searching for the bottom of the ocean with an arrow,
When examined with wisdom, the depth of the aggregates cannot be found. ^4-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-64]]

For any bodhisattva who realizes this profound
Vehicle, ultimate and unattached, wherein the aggregates,
Elements, and sense-sources do not exist—
What perfectly accomplished merit is there beyond that? ^4-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-65]]

For example, a man who engages in the phenomena of desire,
Having made an appointment with a woman and not meeting her,
However many discursive thoughts of his mind operate in a single day,
The bodhisattva will gain that many eons. ^4-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-66]]

Even if a bodhisattva, for many thousands of millions of eons,
Gives gifts to arhats and pratyekabuddhas and observes ethical discipline,
For anyone who teaches the dharma endowed with the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Giving and ethical discipline do not bear a fraction of the goodness of that teaching. ^4-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-67]]

Any bodhisattva who meditates on the supreme wisdom,
Arising from that, teaches the untainted dharma,
And dedicates that as a cause for awakening for the sake of beings,
There is no virtue equal to that in the three worlds. ^4-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-68]]

Knowing that merit, too, to be an accumulation and likewise empty,
Hollow, baseless, and coreless—
If one thus engages in the wisdom practice of the sugatas,
While engaging, one fully grasps immeasurable merit. ^4-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-69]]

Spoken by the Buddha, vividly practiced and vividly taught,
Knowing all these dharmas to be nothing more than mere expressions,
Even if they are expressed for many tens of millions and hundreds of billions of eons,
In the dharmadhātu, there is no depletion and no increase. ^4-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-70]]

Whatever is called the perfection of the victors,
Those dharmas too are fully proclaimed to be merely names.
A bodhisattva who fully dedicates without a conceited mind
Will without decline touch the supreme awakening of a buddha. ^4-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-71]]

By the first contact of the burning wick of an oil lamp,
The wick is not burned, yet without it, it is not burned.
By the final contact of the flame, the wick is not burned,
Yet without the final flame, the wick is not burned—just as, ^4-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-72]]

By the first mind, supreme awakening is not touched,
Yet without it, one is not able to touch it.
By the final mind, peaceful awakening is not attained,
Yet without it, one is not able to attain it. ^4-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-73]]

From a seed, a sprout, a flower, and a fruit arise,
And when it ceases, the tree is not absent there—just as,
Likewise, the first mind is also the cause of awakening,
And when it ceases, it is not that awakening is absent. ^4-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-74]]

By drops of water, a vase from the first to the last,
Is gradually, little by little, fully filled—just as,
Likewise, the first mind too is the cause of supreme awakening,
Gradually, perfecting white virtues, one becomes a buddha. ^4-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-75]]

Practicing the dharmas of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness,
Not touching nirvāṇa and not engaging in signs,
Just as a skilled boatman going back and forth,
Does not dwell on either shore, nor does he dwell in the ocean. ^4-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-76]]

A bodhisattva practicing in this way has no conceited mind thinking, "I
Am prophesied by the Ten-Powered One and will touch awakening,"
Nor do they fear awakening, thinking, "There is nothing at all here";
Practicing in this way is practicing the wisdom of the sugatas. ^4-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-77]]

Seeing the paths of the wilderness of the world, along with famine and disease,
They are without terror and then put on their armor;
Always striving until the final limit and vividly knowing,
They do not generate even a speck of dust of a weary mind. ^4-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-78]]

A bodhisattva engaging in the victors' perfection of wisdom,
Knows these aggregates to be empty from the beginning and unarisen;
Without resting in meditative equipoise, they enter into compassion for the realm of beings,
And in the meantime, they never decline from the buddha dharmas. ^4-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-79]]

For example, a skilled person endowed with all qualities,
Powerful, invincible, having made effort, knowing the rites of arts,
Who has reached the far shore of archery and many crafts,
Knowing how to conjure illusions, intensely desiring the welfare of beings, ^4-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-80]]

Having fully gathered his father, mother, and wives together,
Travels on a wilderness path with many enemies;
Then he emanates many brave and courageous men,
Travels safely, and returns home again—just as, ^4-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-81]]

Likewise, at that time, a skilled bodhisattva,
Generating great compassion for all realms of sentient beings,
Having utterly passed beyond the four māras and the two,
Dwells in the supreme samādhi and does not touch awakening. ^4-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-82]]

Wind is supported on space, on that is supported the mass of water,
On that is supported this great earth, on that are supported living beings;
Such is the cause of sentient beings engaging in karma;
Think about this meaning of space being a pervasive foundation! ^4-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-83]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva dwelling in emptiness,
Supported by aspirations knowing sentient beings among wanderers,
Demonstrates many various kinds of activities,
Does not touch nirvāṇa, and does not dwell in emptiness. ^4-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-84]]

Whenever a skilled and brilliant bodhisattva
Engages in this supreme samādhi of peaceful emptiness,
During that time they do not intensely cultivate signs;
Dwelling in signlessness, they practice peace and profound peace. ^4-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-85]]

For a bird flying in the air, there is no place to abide;
It does not abide there, nor does it fall to the ground.
Likewise, a bodhisattva practicing in the doors of liberation
Does not touch nirvāṇa and does not engage in signs. ^4-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-86]]

For example, a person trained in archery shoots an arrow into the sky,
And by connecting other arrows in succession behind it,
Does not give the previous arrow a chance to fall to the ground;
If that person wishes, that arrow will fall to the ground. ^4-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-87]]

Likewise, engaging in the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Thoroughly practicing by means of the power of wisdom, skillful means, and miracles,
As long as their roots of virtue are not perfected,
For that long they do not attain that supreme emptiness. ^4-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-88]]

For example, a monk endowed with the supreme power of miracles,
Abiding in space, simultaneously emanates magical creations,
Demonstrating walking, pacing, sleeping, and sitting;
He has no weariness, and he has no fatigue. ^4-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-89]]

Likewise, a skilled bodhisattva dwelling in emptiness,
Having reached the other shore of pristine wisdom and miracles, does not dwell there,
Demonstrates infinite kinds of activities to wanderers;
For ten million aeons they have no weariness and no fatigue. ^4-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-90]]

For example, a person standing at a great precipice,
Holding two umbrellas in both hands,
Jumps into the sky and casts their body into the great abyss,
Yet will not fall as long as they are traveling there. ^4-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-91]]

Likewise, a skilled bodhisattva dwelling in compassion,
Completely holding the two umbrellas of skillful means and wisdom,
Realizing that dharmas are empty, signless, and wishless,
Does not touch nirvāṇa, yet sees the dharmas. ^4-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-92]]

For example, a person desiring jewels, having gone to a jewel island,
And having found jewels, returns home again;
Just as that merchant does not merely live happily by himself there,
And leave his host of relatives and friends unhappy. ^4-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-93]]

Likewise, a bodhisattva who has gone to the jewel island of emptiness,
And attained the powers of the faculties of concentration,
Does not merely delight in touching nirvāṇa,
And leave sentient beings in suffering. ^4-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-94]]

For example, an ambitious merchant, in order to know the routes,
Goes to whatever intermediate cities, towns, and villages there are.
He does not dwell there, nor does he dwell on the jewel island;
The wise one does not dwell at home, but becomes skilled in the path. ^4-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-95]]

Likewise, a brilliant bodhisattva is skilled in all
The knowledges and liberations of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas;
They do not dwell in them, nor do they dwell in the Buddha's pristine wisdom;
They do not dwell in the unconditioned, but know the nature of the path. ^4-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-96]]

Whenever, having bound themselves with love for wanderers,
They practice the samādhis of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness,
Can it be designated that they will attain nirvāṇa,
Or that they are conditioned? They have no abiding. ^4-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-97]]

For example, just as an emanated human body is not invisible,
And it can also be designated by a name;
Likewise, a bodhisattva practicing in the doors of liberation
Can also be designated by a name. ^4-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^4-98]]

If, when completely questioned about their practice and faculties,
A bodhisattva, the dharmas of emptiness and signlessness
Does not completely teach, and the dharmas of the irreversible stage
Does not explain, they should be known as unprophesied. ^4-98

## Teaching on the Culminating Clear Realization ^5-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-1]]

Chapter Five: Teaching the Clear Realization of the Peak.
The stage of an arhat and the pristine wisdom of a pratyekabuddha,
And the three realms, they do not desire even in dreams;
Seeing the buddhas and teaching the dharma to wanderers,
They should be known as irreversible and prophesied. ^5-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-2]]

In a dream, seeing sentient beings abiding in the three lower realms,
In that instant they make aspirations to cut off the stream of the lower realms.
Through the blessing of truth, the masses of fire are pacified;
They should be known as irreversible and prophesied. ^5-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-3]]

In the human world, there are many bhūta demons and diseases;
Possessing benefit and compassion, they pacify them through the blessing of truth.
Yet they have no conceit and do not produce pride;
They should be known as irreversible and prophesied. ^5-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-4]]

However, if through those various blessings of truth
Being accomplished, the conceit arises thinking, "I am prophesied,"
Or if a bodhisattva is conceited about being prophesied by another,
Abiding in conceit, they should be known as having small intelligence. ^5-4

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-5]]

Māra, having approached through the basis of names,
Speaks thus: "These are the names of you and your parents,
And of your lineage up to your seven ancestors,
And when you become a Buddha, this will be your name." ^5-5

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-6]]

"Whatever ascetic practices, vows, and yogas you will possess,
Your past qualities and conduct were also like this," he says.
Whatever bodhisattva hears such words and becomes conceited,
Should be known as fully incited by Māra and having small intelligence. ^5-6

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-7]]

Very solitary villages, cities, and mountain valleys,
And isolated monasteries and forests, they thoroughly rely upon;
But a bodhisattva who praises themselves and deprecates others,
Should be known as fully incited by Māra and having small intelligence. ^5-7

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-8]]

Constantly abiding in villages, provinces, and towns,
Except for striving for awakening to mature sentient beings,
They do not produce other desires for arhatship or pratyekabuddhahood;
This is taught by the sons of the Sugata as being solitary. ^5-8

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-9]]

Whoever, in a mountain valley of five hundred yojanas,
Filled with snakes, abides for many ten millions of years,
That bodhisattva who does not know this solitude,
Having acquired superior pride, abides in mixture. ^5-9

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-10]]

When a bodhisattva striving for the benefit of beings attains dhyāna,
Powers, faculties, liberations, and samādhi,
If one despises them, thinking, "This is not the conduct of isolated monasteries,"
The Victorious One says they abide in the domain of Māra. ^5-10

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-11]]

Whether abiding in a village or else in an isolated monastery,
If free from the minds of the two vehicles and certain of supreme awakening,
This is the solitude of those engaged in the benefit of beings;
That conceited bodhisattva ruins themselves. ^5-11

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-12]]

Therefore, one who seeks holy awakening,
A wise one possessing strong intention, having definitely destroyed pride,
Just as a multitude of sick people rely on a doctor to be cured,
Should rely on spiritual friends without wavering. ^5-12

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-13]]

Bodhisattvas entered into the supreme awakening of a Buddha
Should rely on spiritual friends together with the perfections.
Those who teach accordingly, this is the stage of accomplishment;
By these two causes, they will swiftly realize the awakening of a Buddha. ^5-13

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-14]]

The Victorious Ones of the past, future, and whoever abide in the ten directions,
The path of all of them is this perfection, and no other.
This perfection is, for those entered into supreme awakening,
Explained as the supreme illumination, lamp, light, and teacher. ^5-14

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-15]]

Just as the perfection of wisdom is empty of characteristics,
Knowing all these phenomena to be similar to that characteristic,
If one thoroughly knows phenomena as empty and signless,
Practicing like that is the wisdom practice of the Sugata. ^5-15

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-16]]

Fully starving sentient beings desire food, and
Those possessing a mind attached to saṃsāra constantly wander.
Both self and the phenomena of self are unreal and empty;
Childish beings themselves have cast a knot in space. ^5-16

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-17]]

For example, just as poison aroused by the perception of suspicion,
Even if that poison has not gone inside, one will faint;
Likewise, childish beings, accepting self and what belongs to self,
Perceiving that self, which is not real, constantly are born and die. ^5-17

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-18]]

Just as there is apprehension, so is complete affliction taught;
Not apprehending self and what belongs to self is taught as complete purification;
In this, without anything becoming afflicted or completely pure,
The bodhisattva realizes the perfection of wisdom. ^5-18

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-19]]

However many sentient beings there are in Jambudvīpa without exception,
If all of them, having generated the mind toward supreme awakening,
Were to give gifts for many tens of millions of thousands of years,
And dedicate it all as a cause for awakening for the benefit of beings; ^5-19

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-20]]

Whoever visibly strives in the supreme perfection of wisdom,
If they conform to it for even a single day,
That aggregate of generosity cannot match a fraction of their merit;
Therefore, one should always engage in wisdom without wavering. ^5-20

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-21]]

The yogi practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Even when generating great compassion, has no perception of sentient beings;
At that time, the wise one becomes a field of generosity for all beings,
And always meaningfully fully enjoys the alms of the realm. ^5-21

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-22]]

Any bodhisattva who, for the sake of liberating
The sentient beings of gods, humans, and the three lower realms bound here for a long time,
Desires to teach the broad path to the other shore to the realm of beings,
Should strive in the perfection of wisdom day and night. ^5-22

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-23]]

A person who previously had not found a supreme jewel,
Finding it at another time, out of joy,
If they lose it right after finding it by becoming careless,
By losing it, desiring the jewel, they will constantly suffer. ^5-23

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-24]]

Likewise, one entered into supreme awakening should not abandon yoga
In the jewel-like perfection of wisdom;
Having found and taken the jewel, one should thoroughly apply exertion,
Swiftly progressing, just as one becomes pacified. ^5-24

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-25]]

The sun, freed from clouds, possessing a net of light rays,
Rises, destroying all dense, dark darkness,
And outshines all fireflies, living beings, and creatures,
Along with the hosts of stars and light of the moon. ^5-25

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-26]]

Just so, the bodhisattva practicing the supreme perfection of wisdom,
Practicing emptiness and signlessness,
The wise one, having destroyed the dense thicket of views, outshines all beings,
Arhats, pratyekabuddhas, and many bodhisattvas. ^5-26

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-27]]

For example, a prince who desires the purpose of giving away wealth,
Having become the chief of all, is worthy to be visibly approached.
Even as he is, he brings joy to many sentient beings;
What need is there to mention when he attains the means and abides in sovereignty! ^5-27

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-28]]

Likewise, the bodhisattva wise in practicing wisdom too,
Gives the nectar and pleases gods and humans.
Even as they are, they strive to benefit many sentient beings;
What need is there to mention when they come to abide as the Dharma King! ^5-28

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-29]]

At that time, Māra becomes afflicted by a thorn,
Tormented by sorrow, suffering, mental distress, and weakened.
Thinking, "How can the mind of this bodhisattva be made to shrink back?"
In order to show terrors, he burns the directions and casts meteors. ^5-29

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-30]]

When those wise ones, possessing intense resolve,
Behold the supreme meaning of the perfection of wisdom day and night,
At that time, their body, mind, and speech move like a bird in the sky;
How could the associates of the dark one find an opportunity? ^5-30

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-31]]

Whenever bodhisattvas become embroiled in quarrels and disputes,
And, disagreeing with one another, possess angry minds,
At that time, Māra is joyful and becomes supremely happy,
Thinking, "Those two will become distant from the wisdom of the Victorious Ones." ^5-31

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-32]]

"Those two will become distant, and they will become like flesh-eating demons;
Both will cause their own promises to degenerate.
How can there be awakening for those who are hostile and devoid of patience?"
At that time, the Māras along with their factions will become joyful. ^5-32

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-33]]

Whichever bodhisattva who has not obtained a prophecy
Gets an angry mind and initiates a dispute with one who has obtained a prophecy,
For however many moments of mind possessing the fault of hostility there are,
For that many eons, they must don the armor from the beginning anew. ^5-33

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-34]]

"Buddhas touch awakening through the perfection of patience."
Generating mindfulness, thinking, "This mind is not good,"
Confessing individually and restraining themselves in the future,
And not rejoicing, they train in this Dharma of the Buddhas. ^5-34

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-35]]

When training, whoever does not assert any training,
And does not observe the trainer or the dharmas of training,
And does not conceptualize these two: having trained and not having trained,
Whoever trains like that, trains in this Dharma of the Buddhas. ^5-35

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-36]]

Whichever bodhisattva knows this training like that,
Will never become one with degenerate training or faulty ethical discipline;
In order to please the Buddhas, they train in this;
They are wise in the higher training and lack apprehension. ^5-36

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-37]]

The wise light-makers train in wisdom like that;
They do not generate even a single non-virtuous mind.
Just as, when outshone by the rays of the sun moving in the sky,
Darkness does not remain in the space before it. ^5-37

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-38]]

For those who have trained in the perfection of wisdom,
All perfections come to be included herein.
Just as the sixty-two views are included within the view of the transitory collection,
Likewise, these perfections are taught to be included (herein). ^5-38

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-39]]

For example, when the life faculty ceases,
All the other faculties, however many there are, cease;
Likewise, for the supreme wise ones practicing wisdom,
All perfections are taught to be included herein. ^5-39

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-40]]

Wise bodhisattvas train in whatever are the qualities of śrāvakas,
And similarly all the qualities of pratyekabuddhas;
They do not abide in them nor generate desire for them;
Thinking, "This is to be trained in by me," therefore they train. ^5-40

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-41]]

Regarding those who have fully entered supreme, irreversible awakening,
Rejoicing from the depths of one's heart in their generation of the mind—
The Mount Meru of a trichiliocosm could be measured by weighing it on a scale,
But the virtue of that rejoicing is not like that. ^5-41

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-42]]

Seeking virtue and desiring to benefit however many sentient beings there are,
They also rejoice in the heaps of merit of them all;
Therefore, having attained the qualities of the Victorious Ones,
In order to exhaust suffering, they give the gift of Dharma to the world. ^5-42

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-43]]

Whichever bodhisattva, non-conceptualizing, fully knows all dharmas
As empty, signless, and free from elaboration,
They do not seek awakening through dualistic wisdom;
That yogin exerts themselves in the supreme perfection of wisdom. ^5-43

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-44]]

That realm of space and what contradicts that space
Do not exist, and no one has attained them;
Likewise, the wise bodhisattvas practicing wisdom
Are like space and practice total peace. ^5-44

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-45]]

In the midst of people, an illusory person does not think,
"I will please these people," yet still pleases them;
Though seen to display various miracles,
They have no body, no mind, and not even a name. ^5-45

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-46]]

Likewise, those practicing wisdom never think thus:
"Having realized awakening, I will completely liberate beings";
Though they demonstrate taking various births and having many activities,
Like an illusion, they practice without conceptualizing. ^5-46

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-47]]

For example, an emanation of a Buddha performs the deeds of a Buddha,
And in doing so, not even a little pride arises;
Likewise, the wise bodhisattvas practicing wisdom
Demonstrate all activities like an emanation or an illusion. ^5-47

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-48]]

A wise carpenter crafts figures similar to
Men and women, and they also perform all activities;
Likewise, the wise bodhisattvas practicing wisdom
Perform all activities with non-conceptualizing pristine awareness. ^5-48

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-49]]

To the wise who practice thus, many hosts of gods
Join their palms, bow down, and pay homage;
And however many Buddhas there are in the world systems of the ten directions,
They completely proclaim a garland of praise for their qualities. ^5-49

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-50]]

However many sentient beings there are in fields equal to the Ganges,
If all of them, taking on assumed forms, became Māras,
And even if they emanated that many from each and every single hair pore,
All of them would not be able to cause hindrances for the wise. ^5-50

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-51]]

By four causes, the wise bodhisattva possesses strength,
Is hard to overcome by the four Māras, and cannot be moved:
Dwelling in emptiness, not forsaking sentient beings,
Acting as they have spoken, and possessing the blessing of the Sugatas. ^5-51

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-52]]

This perfection of wisdom, the mother of the Tathāgatas,
When it is taught, whichever bodhisattva aspires to it,
And clearly exerts themselves to accomplish it with sincere intent,
That noble one should be known to have entered into omniscience. ^5-52

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-53]]

They do not dwell in the dharmadhātu or suchness,
For example, like clouds in space, they dwell without dwelling.
A mantra-holder, without a dwelling, moving in space, wishing to dwell,
By the power of mantras, is endowed with out-of-season flowers and blesses them. ^5-53

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-54]]

Practicing thus, the wise and luminous bodhisattvas
Do not apprehend awakening or the qualities of a Buddha;
They do not apprehend one who teaches, one who desires dharma, or one who sees;
This is the dwelling of those who desire peace and delight in qualities. ^5-54

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-55]]

Except for the liberations of Arhats and Tathāgatas,
This dwelling, among those of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
This dwelling in peaceful samādhi, supremely peaceful and endowed with bliss,
Is the supreme and unsurpassed of all however many there are. ^5-55

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-56]]

A bird dwells in the sky, yet does not fall;
A fish dwells in water, yet does not suffocate and die;
Likewise, the bodhisattva, by the powers of concentration,
Having reached the other shore, dwells in emptiness and does not pass into nirvāṇa. ^5-56

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-57]]

To go to the supreme qualities of all sentient beings,
And to touch the supremely marvelous, supreme Buddha-wisdom,
Whosoever desires to give the supreme, holy gift of Dharma,
Rely on this supreme dwelling of those who bring benefit! ^5-57

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-58]]

When the Guides fully proclaim this training,
It is the supreme and unsurpassed of all trainings however many there are.
Whichever wise one desires to go to the other shore of all trainings,
Train in this perfection of wisdom, the training of the Buddhas! ^5-58

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-59]]

This is the supreme Dharma-treasure and the holy Dharma-treasury;
It is the lineage of the Buddhas, the treasury of happiness and joy of beings.
The protectors of the world of the past, future, and the ten directions,
Though they are born from this, the dharmadhātu does not become exhausted. ^5-59

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-60]]

However many trees, fruits, flowers, and forests there are,
They all perfectly arise and come forth from the earth;
Yet, the earth has no exhaustion and has no increase,
Does not diminish, does not conceptualize, and has no weariness. ^5-60

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-61]]

The sons of the Buddha, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and gods,
And however many dharmas of happiness and joy of all beings there are,
They all arise from the supreme perfection of wisdom,
And yet wisdom has no exhaustion and has no increase. ^5-61

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-62]]

However many inferior, middling, and supreme sentient beings there are,
The Sugatas have taught that they all arise from ignorance;
When conditions come together, the machine of suffering perfectly arises,
Yet that machine of ignorance has no exhaustion and no increase. ^5-62

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-63]]

However many approaches to knowledge, methods, and roots there are,
They all arise from the supreme perfection of wisdom;
When conditions come together, the machine of activities perfectly arises,
Yet the perfection of wisdom has no exhaustion and no increase. ^5-63

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-64]]

Whichever bodhisattva, with regard to dependent origination,
Knows this wisdom as unborn and unexhausted,
Just as the cloudless sun dispels darkness by emitting rays of light,
Having destroyed dense ignorance, will attain the state of the Self-Arisen. ^5-64

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-65]]

Endowed with great power, they even abide in the four dhyānas;
They do not rely on them and do not abide in them;
Nevertheless, these four dhyānas along with their branches,
Become the support for attaining supreme holy awakening. ^5-65

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-66]]

Those who have attained supreme wisdom and abide in the dhyānas,
Also experience the four holy formless samādhis;
These dhyānas are beneficial for supreme holy awakening,
But the bodhisattva does not train to exhaust the defilements. ^5-66

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-67]]

This is the marvelous wonder of accumulating qualities:
Having abided in the samādhis of the dhyānas, they have no signs;
If the bodies of those who abide in them were to fall apart,
They are again born in the desire realm according to their intention. ^5-67

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-68]]

For example, if a man of Jambudvīpa who previously
Had not gone to the supreme city of the gods of the divine realm were to go later,
Having seen those completely possessed objects,
When he returns here, he does not become attached to them; ^5-68

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-69]]

Similarly, those who hold the supreme qualities of a bodhisattva,
Exerting themselves in yoga and abiding in the samādhis of the dhyānas,
Again abide in the desire realm without attachment;
Like a lotus in water, they do not abide in the dharmas of fools. ^5-69

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-70]]

The great beings, to completely ripen sentient beings,
Act only to purify the realms and complete the perfections;
Thinking, "The qualities of awakening and the perfections will diminish,"
They do not seek birth in the formless realm. ^5-70

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-71]]

For example, if a man were to find treasures of jewels,
But did not generate a mind of desire for them,
And at another time he completely took them,
Yet having taken them and gone home, he does not become attached; ^5-71

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-72]]

Similarly, the wise bodhisattva, joy and
Having attained the happiness-bestowing four peaceful samādhis of the dhyānas,
Lets go of the attainment of the dhyāna samādhis endowed with the bliss of the dhyānas,
And endowed with compassion for beings, again enters the desire realm. ^5-72

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-73]]

If a bodhisattva abides in the samādhis of the dhyānas,
But generates a mind desiring the vehicle of an arhat or pratyekabuddha,
They are not in meditative equipoise, but agitated and their mind is distracted;
The qualities of a Buddha completely diminish, like a boatman whose boat breaks. ^5-73

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-74]]

Moreover, forms and sounds, and likewise smells and tastes,
Touches, and the five qualities of desire, even if they exert themselves in these,
If they are free from the vehicles of the arhat and pratyekabuddha and delight in the mind of awakening,
The hero should be known to be constantly in meditative equipoise. ^5-74

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-75]]

For the sake of other sentient beings and persons, the perfectly pure bodhisattva
Manifestly exerts themselves and acts in the perfection of diligence;
For example, a servant woman drawing water has no power of her own, but is under the power of her master;
Similarly, the heroes act under the power of all sentient beings. ^5-75

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-76]]

Whether scolded or even constantly beaten,
The servant woman does not speak words of reply to her master,
But thinking, "Because of this, I will be killed,"
With an extremely terrified mind, she is overcome with fear. ^5-76

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-77]]

Similarly, those who have entered into supreme awakening for the sake of awakening,
Should abide like servants to all beings;
From this, awakening is attained and the qualities are completed;
Fire arises from grass and wood, and burns them up. ^5-77

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-78]]

Letting go of one's own happiness, with a mind free of hope,
Exert yourself day and night for the needs of other sentient beings;
Like a mother attending to her only child,
Abide without weariness with a resolute intention. ^5-78

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-79]]

Whatever bodhisattva wishes to cycle in saṃsāra for a long time,
A yogi exerting themselves for the benefit of sentient beings and purifying the realms,
If they do not generate even an atom of a weary mind,
They are endowed with the perfection of diligence and are free from idleness. ^5-79

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-80]]

The unwise bodhisattva counts tens of millions of eons;
If they perceive them as long, they suffer in accomplishing awakening;
When accomplishing the authentic dharma, they suffer for a long time;
Their perfection of diligence diminishes and they possess laziness. ^5-80

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-81]]

From the first generation of the mind for supreme awakening,
Up until unsurpassable awakening is attained,
If they consider it in their mind as just a single day and night,
The radiant wise one should be known as having commenced diligence. ^5-81

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-82]]

If someone says thus: "After destroying Mount Meru,
You will attain supreme awakening,"
If they then generate a weary mind and even think of its magnitude,
At that time, the bodhisattva has become lazy. ^5-82

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-83]]

But if they think, "How difficult is a measure just like this?"
And generate the mind to crush Mount Meru to dust in a mere instant,
The wise bodhisattva has commenced diligence,
And without taking long, they will attain the supreme awakening of a guide. ^5-83

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-84]]

Thinking, "Having completely ripened beings, I will act for their benefit,"
If they exert themselves with body, speech, and mind,
Because they abide in the perception of a self, they have become lazy,
And they become as far from omniscience as the sky is from the earth. ^5-84

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-85]]

Whenever there is no perception of body, mind, and sentient beings,
And having completely turned away from perception, one practices the non-dual dharma,
For one who desires the supreme, unwaning, peaceful awakening,
This was taught by the Benefactor to be the perfection of diligence. ^5-85

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-86]]

If one hears abusive and harsh words from others,
Thinking, "I am happy," the wise bodhisattva rejoices;
Who speaks, who hears, by what, what is it, and for what reason?
The wise one is endowed with the supreme perfection of patience. ^5-86

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-87]]

Whatever bodhisattva is endowed with the excellent dharma of patience,
Compared to someone who fills the three thousandfold world system with jewels and offers them to the Knowers of the World,
The Buddhas, arhats, and solitary realizers,
That mass of generosity cannot match a fraction of the merit of the former. ^5-87

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-88]]

The body of one who abides in patience becomes completely pure;
With the thirty-two marks and boundless power and strength,
They proclaim the supreme empty dharma to sentient beings;
Beings will rejoice in this wise one endowed with patience. ^5-88

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-89]]

If some sentient being takes a handful of sandalwood
And with great respect rubs it on the bodhisattva,
While another scatters burning coals on their head, towards both
They should generate an equal and even mind. ^5-89

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-90]]

Having practiced patience in that way, the wise bodhisattva,
If they completely dedicate that generation of the mind to supreme awakening,
The hero, through patience towards the entire world, outshines
All arhats, solitary realizers, and the many realms of sentient beings. ^5-90

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-91]]

The one who practices patience should also generate the mind thinking this:
"In the hells, animal realms, and the world of Yama, there is much suffering;
If one experiences unavoidable harm as a result of sensory pleasures,
Why should I not now practice patience for the sake of awakening?" ^5-91

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-92]]

"Whips, sticks, weapons, killing, binding, and beating,
Severing of the head, and cutting off ears, hands, feet, and nose—
Whatever sufferings exist in the world, I will endure them!"
Thinking this, the bodhisattva abides in the perfection of patience. ^5-92

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-93]]

Through discipline, those who desire peace become exalted;
Abiding in the domain of the ten powers, their discipline does not wane.
They engage in all the activities of vows, whatever they may be,
And dedicate them to awakening for the benefit of all sentient beings. ^5-93

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-94]]

If one desires to touch the awakening of an arhat or solitary realizer,
One's discipline is broken, one is unwise, and thus one's conduct degenerates.
But if one completely dedicates it to the supreme, peaceful awakening,
Even while engaged in sensory pleasures, one abides in the perfection of discipline. ^5-94

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-95]]

Whatever dharmas produce the qualities of definite awakening,
This is the purpose of discipline for those endowed with virtuous dharmas.
Whatever dharmas cause the awakening of benefactors to completely degenerate,
This was taught by the Guide to be broken discipline. ^5-95

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-96]]

Even if a bodhisattva enjoys the five sensory pleasures,
If they have taken refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, and the noble Sangha,
And direct their mind toward omniscience, thinking, "I will accomplish buddhahood!"
The wise one should be known as abiding in the perfection of discipline. ^5-96

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-97]]

Even if one practices the ten paths of virtuous action for ten million aeons,
If one generates the desire for the state of an arhat or solitary realizer,
At that time, one's discipline becomes flawed and one's discipline degenerates;
Generating that mind is much heavier even than a defeat. ^5-97

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-98]]

Guarding discipline and completely dedicating it to supreme awakening,
Without conceit about it and without praising oneself,
Completely abandoning the perception of "I" and the perception of sentient beings—
Such a bodhisattva is said to abide in the perfection of discipline. ^5-98

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-99]]

If a bodhisattva practicing the path of the Victorious Ones
Thinks, "These sentient beings have discipline; these have broken discipline,"
By engaging in such diverse perceptions, their discipline is completely broken;
Their discipline degenerates, and they lack completely pure discipline. ^5-99

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-100]]

Whoever has no perception of self and no perception of sentient beings,
Being free from attachment to perceptions, how could they have non-restraint?
Whoever has no conceit regarding restraint and non-restraint,
This was taught by the Guide to be the discipline of restraint. ^5-100

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-101]]

Any pure sentient being who thus possesses discipline
Has no view of anything as pleasant or unpleasant.
Giving away their head, hands, and feet, they have no shrinking mind;
Giving away all their possessions, they are always free from clinging. ^5-101

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-102]]

Knowing that all dharmas are by nature selfless and insubstantial,
If they give away even their own flesh without a shrinking mind,
Then what need is there to mention giving away external objects?
For them to act with miserliness is impossible. ^5-102

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-103]]

Perceiving a self, one is attached to things through grasping them as "mine";
How could fools have the mind of generosity?
Miserly ones will be born in the realm of hungry ghosts;
Or even if they are born as humans, they will then be impoverished. ^5-103

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-104]]

Then, the bodhisattva, knowing that sentient beings are impoverished,
Becomes intent on giving and always gives freely.
The four continents, beautifully adorned, are like a glob of spit;
Having given them away they become joyful, but attaining the continents is not like that. ^5-104

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-105]]

The wise and brilliant bodhisattva, [thinking], "Of the three states of existence,
To all those sentient beings, however many there are, may this gift
Be given!" Having thought thus, they give gifts,
And also dedicate them to supreme awakening for the sake of beings. ^5-105

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-106]]

Having given gifts, they do not abide in entities;
They never have hope for a ripening result;
Thus, being wise in giving, they let go of everything;
Having given a little, it becomes much, immeasurable. ^5-106

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-107]]

However many sentient beings there are without exception in the three states of existence,
Taking them fully into consideration, if all of them
To the Knowers of the World, the Buddhas, Arhats, and Pratyekabuddhas
Give gifts for infinite eons, and properly seek the śrāvaka path— ^5-107

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-108]]

Whatever wise bodhisattva, skilled in means,
Rejoices in their bases of meritorious actions,
And fully dedicates them to supreme awakening for the sake of beings,
By fully dedicating them, they outshine all beings. ^5-108

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-109]]

Even a great heap of imitation glass jewels
Is completely outshone by a single precious lapis lazuli;
Likewise, all the vast heaps of giving of all beings
Are outshone by the rejoicing bodhisattva. ^5-109

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^5-110]]

If the bodhisattva, having given gifts to beings,
Does not make them "mine" and is unsparing of entities,
Their roots of virtue will increase with great power,
Like the radiant, cloudless moon disk in the waxing fortnight. ^5-110

## Teaching on the Gradual Approach ^6-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-1]]

Chapter Six: Teaching the Gradual Training.
By the bodhisattva's giving, the destiny of hungry ghosts is cut off;
Poverty and likewise all afflictions are cut off;
While practicing, they will obtain infinite, abundant wealth,
And by giving, they fully ripen sentient beings who are suffering. ^6-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-2]]

By discipline, the nature of the manifold destinies of animals and
The eight unfree states are abandoned, and leisure is always found.
By patience, a fine, supreme, and vast form will be obtained,
Golden-colored, beautiful, and worthy to be looked at by beings. ^6-2

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-3]]

By diligence, pure qualities will not diminish,
And infinite pristine wisdom, the treasury of the Victors, will be found.
By concentration, the despised qualities of desire are discarded,
And knowledge, superknowledges, and samadhi are accomplished. ^6-3

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^6-4]]

By wisdom, having fully known the nature of phenomena,
One will completely transcend the three realms without exception;
The supreme leader of humans, having turned the precious wheel,
Teaches the Dharma to beings in order to exhaust suffering. ^6-4

## Teaching on the Dharmakāya ^8-0

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-1]]

Chapter Eight: Teaching the Dharmakāya.
Fully upholds perfectly pure fields and pure sentient beings;
Fully upholds the Buddha lineage and also the Dharma lineage;
Likewise fully upholds the Sangha lineage and all dharmas. ^8-1

![[bo-ratnagunasancayagatha#^8-2]]

By the supreme physician who heals the diseases of beings,
This teaching of wisdom, endowed with the path to awakening, was taught;
Its name is the Collection of Precious Qualities, the path to awakening;
This path was explained in order for all sentient beings to obtain it. ^8-2
