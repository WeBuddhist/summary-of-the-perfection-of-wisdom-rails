# Style sheet: Hindi (Devanagari) termbase, Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā

## Policies

1. Script and punctuation: Devanagari only (no Roman or IAST in Hindi output); use the danda । for sentence ends, ordinary commas and quotes, and Western digits for numbers; this keeps all agents' output uniform and machine-comparable.
2. Spelling of Sanskrit loans: use the full-nasal conjunct form (पञ्च, मञ्जुश्री, सङ्घ, अभिसम्बोधि, सञ्चय), ऋ for ṛ (ऋद्धि), visarga in the academic register (दुःख), and the geminate त्त्व (बोधिसत्त्व, सत्त्व); one spelling per word avoids false variants in the glossary.
3. Academic register uses the tatsama (Sanskrit-derived) Buddhist terms named in registers-hi.md first (प्रज्ञापारमिता, बोधिसत्त्व, सुगत, तथागत, शून्यता, निमित्त, अनुपलम्भ, स्कन्ध, श्रावक, प्रत्येकबुद्ध, अर्हत्, मार, दान, शील, क्षान्ति, वीर्य, ध्यान, क्लेश); Hindu-devotional words (ईश्वर, प्रभु, आत्मा, मोक्ष) and Urdu-Persian substitutes are not used for technical terms, because they import other doctrines.
4. The two senses of ཆོས stay apart by form and by register: for phenomena (the sense "phenomena") academic uses धर्म only in the plural, quantified or compound form (सभी धर्म, धर्मों, सर्वधर्म, धर्मता, धर्मधातु) and children's uses चीज़ें; for the Buddha's teaching, academic uses धर्म only in the singular, never quantified (जिनों का धर्म) and children's uses शिक्षा; the third sense "quality (of a Buddha, virtuous)" is गुण in both registers (बुद्धधर्म = बुद्ध-गुण); the sense label in the term packet, not Gemini's single word धर्म, decides which applies.
5. Established usage versus the Gemini zero-shot: Gemini is evidence of one possible reading, not authority; where it already uses an established Hindi Buddhist term, keep it (प्रज्ञापारमिता, तथागत, सुगत); where it paraphrases loosely or swaps terms (सत्त्व/प्राणी, ज्ञान/प्रज्ञा, उपलब्ध/अनुपलम्भ, चर्या/आचरण), the register list and the approved English sense distinction win, and the chosen word is then used every time.
6. Keep Tibetan distinctions that English keeps: ཤེས་རབ = प्रज्ञा but ཡེ་ཤེས = ज्ञान (never swapped); སེམས་ཅན = सत्त्व but འགྲོ་བ = जगत् के प्राणी / प्राणी; རྣམ་ཤེས = विज्ञान but སེམས་པ = चेतना; Tibetan variants that English merges (full, truncated, or misspelled forms such as T097, T541) share one Hindi rendering.
7. Epithets of the Buddha stay in the established Sanskrit form in the academic register (जिन, सुगत, तथागत, भगवान्, शास्ता, लोकनाथ, धर्मराज, लोकविद्), with भगवान् reserved for bhagavat only; where Hindi नायक would read as "hero", the three "Guide" terms (འདྲེན་པ, རྣམ་པར་འདྲེན་པ, ཡོངས་འདྲེན) all become मार्गदर्शक, matching the English; children's epithets give the plain meaning of the approved English (विजेता, भली-भाँति गए हुए, इसी प्रकार गए हुए, शिक्षक, मार्गदर्शक).
8. Titles of persons: आर्य for འཕགས་པ (also in "आर्य मञ्जुश्री"), स्थविर for the elder, भिक्षु for the monk, ब्राह्मण for the brahmin; children's register uses the same Devanagari titles where the word is already everyday (भिक्षु, आर्य) and a short phrase otherwise.
9. Names: Indian names in Sanskrit form in Devanagari in both registers (सुभूति, शारिपुत्र, मैत्रेय, मञ्जुश्री, शक्र, विद्याकरसिंह); place and object names in the standard Hindi form (गङ्गा, मेरु पर्वत, जम्बूद्वीप, अनवतप्त सरोवर); Tibetan-only names (the translator) are written as pronounced in Devanagari; the children's register keeps all names unchanged, never translated.
10. The three knowledges that title chapters 1 to 3 are one fixed set (T140, T148, T175): "omniscience of all aspects" = सर्वाकारज्ञता, "knowledge of all paths" = मार्गज्ञता, "knowledge of all bases" = वस्तुज्ञता; plain "omniscience" (T083, T157, T192) stays सर्वज्ञता, so Gemini's सर्वज्ञता for the third title is overridden to keep the two apart.
11. The eight topics that title the sections run in order: सर्वाकारज्ञता, मार्गज्ञता, वस्तुज्ञता, सर्वाकाराभिसमय, मूर्धाभिसमय, क्रमिक मार्ग, अभिसम्बोधि, धर्मकाय; the "complete realization" titles end in अभिसमय and the "complete awakening" title in अभिसम्बोधि, so the two Tibetan families are not blurred.
12. Children's register simplifies a technical term by a short everyday Hindi phrase that carries the same meaning as the approved English children's gloss, never a different idea (निर्वाण = दुख से परे शान्ति, शून्य = खाली, संज्ञा = धारणा); everyday one-word Sanskrit loans may stay (बुद्ध, प्रज्ञा, बोधि, करुणा, मार, ध्यान), while compounds and abstract nouns are opened up; no devotional or Hindu terms are introduced.
13. Compounds take their parts' renderings: if a term is built from a core term, its Hindi uses that term's locked rendering plus the other part (परम बोधि and its वर-/अग्र- variants, बोधि-वीर, पूर्ण प्रज्ञा, जिनपुत्र, लोकधातु, उपायकुशल), so a part never changes meaning between entries.
14. Sanskrit compound titles and set phrases are kept as one Sanskrit compound in the academic register when the Hindi tradition already has it (धर्मधातु, कामधातु, सत्त्वधातु), and the children's register spells them out in plain words (सब चीज़ों का विस्तार, इच्छाओं का क्षेत्र).
15. Verbs and doctrine words of the path keep one form each: academic परिणामना (dedicate; the object is the merit), अनुमोदना (rejoice), अनुपलम्भ (non-apprehension), चर्या (conduct), संस्कृत/असंस्कृत (conditioned/unconditioned); children's use समर्पित करना, खुश होना, न पाना, जीने का तरीका, कारणों से बना / कारणों से न बना.
16. Where the Hindi word has another everyday meaning (वीर्य, वेदना, क्लेश, भूमि, संस्कृत), the registers-hi.md list or established Buddhist usage decides for the academic register; the children's register avoids such words (लगातार मेहनत, अनुभूति, मन की परेशानी, स्तर, कारणों से बना).
