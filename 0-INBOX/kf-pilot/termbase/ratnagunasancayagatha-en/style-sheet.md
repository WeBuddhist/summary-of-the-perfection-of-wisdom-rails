## Policies

1. Awakening, not enlightenment: བྱང་ཆུབ is always "awakening" (supreme awakening, mind of awakening, qualities of awakening) in both registers; both machines split between the two words, "awakening" is the majority in each (DharmaMitra 32 to 24, Gemini 43 to 13), and one word keeps the many བྱང་ཆུབ compounds uniform.
2. Bodhisattva (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ and its truncations སེམས་དཔའ, བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔ, བྱང་ཆུང་སེམས་དཔའ) is kept as "bodhisattva" in the academic register, lower case, no italics; the registers allow it and both machines keep it (80 and 73 times).
3. Mahāsattva: སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ, སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེ and བདག་ཉིད་ཆེན་པོ are "great being" in the academic register (attested by both machines, and the Diamond Sutra termbase of this pilot uses it); where the text joins the two, write "bodhisattva great being".
4. Children's register has no Sanskrit except names, so "bodhisattva" becomes "awakening hero" and "great being" becomes "great hero", the children's renderings of the Diamond Sutra termbase in this pilot (kept the same across texts).
5. The perfection of wisdom (ཤེས་རབ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན, with the variants ཤེས་རབ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ, ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ) is "perfection of wisdom" in the academic register, lower case in running text; Gemini renders all 41 occurrences this way and DharmaMitra's capital P is inconsistent (28 lower case, 13 capitalised).
6. Capitalise "Perfection of Wisdom" only in the title of the work (and the title's English form "The Verse Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom", with "Noble" added for the ཨཱརྻ form); lower case elsewhere; the Sanskrit title (T230) is given in IAST with hyphens, "Ārya-prajñāpāramitā-sañcayagāthā", in the academic register only.
7. Children's register: the perfection of wisdom is "perfect wisdom" (a fixed exception to the compound rule, because it is the subject of the whole text and "perfection of wisdom" is too heavy for 8 to 12 year olds); the six perfections otherwise use "perfection of X".
8. Mother epithets: ཡུམ is "Mother" with a capital whenever it is an epithet of the perfection of wisdom (རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ, རྒྱལ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཡུམ "Mother of the Victors"; བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་ཀྱི་ཡུམ "Mother of the Sugatas"; དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་ཡུམ "Mother of the Tathāgatas"); use "Victors" (not DharmaMitra's variants or Gemini's "Jinas") because "Victors" is the majority rendering of རྒྱལ་བ in both.
9. ཆོས is one Tibetan word with two senses and is kept as two terms: the Buddha's teaching, academic "Dharma" (capitalised) and children's "teaching"; and phenomena, academic "phenomena" and children's "things". The translator decides the sense from context; "dharma(s)" is not used in the academic register because it is not on the kept-Sanskrit list and "phenomena" is attested in both machines.
10. ཆོས in compounds follows its sense: སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས is the lexicalised "qualities of a Buddha" (both machines attest it); ཆོས་མིན "non-phenomena"; ཆོས་རྒྱལ and ཆོས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ "Dharma King"; ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྦྱིན་པ "gift of Dharma"; ཆོས་ཉིད "true nature of phenomena" (distinct from ཆོས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན "nature of phenomena"); ཆོས་དབྱིངས "dharmadhātu" (academic) and ཆོས་སྐུ "Dharmakāya".
11. The six perfections are named: སྦྱིན་པ "generosity", ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས "ethical discipline", བཟོད་པ "patience", བརྩོན་འགྲུས "diligence", བསམ་གཏན "meditative concentration", ཤེས་རབ "wisdom"; each is attested by at least one machine and the set is fixed so a term never changes between its bare and its "perfection of X" use.
12. ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས is "ethical discipline" (not bare "discipline" as Gemini often has it) so it can be told apart from བསླབ་པ "training"; the short form ཁྲིམས is also "ethical discipline", and ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་འཆལ and ཁྲིམས་འཆལ are "lacking ethical discipline".
13. བསམ་གཏན is "meditative concentration", not "dhyāna" (Gemini's usual choice), so Sanskrit stays rare; བསམ་གཏན་བཞི is "four meditative concentrations"; ཏིང་འཛིན is "samādhi" in the academic register (Gemini; and "meditative stabilization" is a minority DharmaMitra variant), which keeps it apart from བསམ་གཏན.
14. Emptiness: སྟོང་པ་ཉིད and སྟོང་ཉིད are "emptiness"; སྟོང་པ and སྟོང are "empty" when adjectival or predicate and "emptiness" when the noun is wanted (both machines split exactly this way); in the children's register it stays "emptiness" and is never "nothing" or "void", per the register rule.
15. Signs: མཚན་མ is "sign" (5 of 5 in both machines), not "mark" or "characteristic"; མཚན་མ་མེད, མཚན་མེད and མཚན་མ་མེད་པ are "signless" (adjective) or "signlessness" (noun), one rendering for all three spellings; མཚན་ཉིད is "characteristic"; the thirty-two marks (མཚན) are "marks".
16. Wishlessness: སྨོན་པ་མེད is "wishless"/"wishlessness"; with emptiness and signlessness these form the three doors of liberation (རྣམ་ཐར་སྒོ "doors of liberation", Gemini 2).
17. Non-apprehension: མི་དམིགས is "not apprehend" (verb) and "non-apprehension" (noun), དམིགས་པ is "apprehension" or "object of apprehension"; Gemini is uniform on "apprehend" while DharmaMitra scatters over "observed", "perceive" and "apprehended"; "perceive" is reserved for འདུ་ཤེས.
18. Non-abiding: གནས་མེད is "non-abiding" and གནས་མེད་གནས is "abide without abiding"; གནས as a place is "dwelling place".
19. The aggregates: ཕུང་པོ "aggregate", ཕུང་པོ་ལྔ "five aggregates"; the members are གཟུགས "form", ཚོར་བ (and ཚོར) "feeling", འདུ་ཤེས "perception", སེམས་པ "volition", རྣམ་ཤེས/རྣམ་པར་ཤེས/རྣམ་པར་ཤེས་པ "consciousness", all attested by both machines; "formations" is reserved for འདུ་བྱེད, which this text does not use.
20. འདུ་ཤེས is "perception" everywhere including the compound "perception of a self" (བདག་ཏུ་འདུ་ཤེས, བདག་གི་འདུ་ཤེས, ང་ཡི་འདུ་ཤེས all "perception of a self"), not "notion" or "concept", which each machine uses only a few times.
21. The three knowledges that title the sections: ལམ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཤེས་པ་ཉིད "knowledge of all paths", གཞི་ཐམས་ཅད་ཤེས་པ "knowledge of all bases", རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད "omniscience of all aspects" (the third is the all-aspect omniscience); plain "omniscience" is ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད, ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་ཉིད and ཀུན་མཁྱེན; keep "knowledge" for ཤེས་པ and "omniscience" for མཁྱེན་པ so the three can be told apart.
22. The clear-realization titles: རྣམ་པ་ཀུན་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་རྟོགས་པ is "complete realization of all aspects" and རྩེ་མོ་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་རྟོགས་པ is "complete realization of the peak" (a minority DharmaMitra "peak of complete realization" and Gemini's "Clear Realization" are not used, so the "realization" stem is one word).
23. Śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, arhat: academic "śrāvaka", "pratyekabuddha", "arhat" in lower case with IAST (both machines mostly agree, and the register keeps arhat); the pratyekabuddha variants རང་རྒྱལ, རྐྱེན་རྟོགས, རང་སངས་རྒྱས, རྐྱེན་རྒྱལ and རྐྱེན་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས are one term; stages are "stage of an arhat", "stage of a pratyekabuddha".
24. Children's: ཉན་ཐོས "listener", རང་རྒྱལ "solitary Buddha", དགྲ་བཅོམ "worthy one" (as in the Diamond Sutra termbase); all three are new because the register bans Sanskrit, and "Buddha" keeps its form.
25. The fruits: this text's overview contains no entries for stream-enterer, once-returner or non-returner; if they arise, use "stream-enterer", "once-returner", "non-returner" and "arhat" (academic) and "one who has entered the stream", "one who returns once", "one who does not return", "worthy one" (children's); འབྲས་བུ is "fruit".
26. Māra: བདུད is "Māra" (academic) and "Mara" (children's), capitalised, plural "Māras"; བདུད་བཞི "four Māras"; བདུད་ཀྱི་ལས is "work of Māra" (Gemini 4, DharmaMitra 3) and children's "Mara's work"; བདུད་ཀྱི་ཕྱོགས "factions of Māra" and བདུད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ "domain of Māra" follow the same pattern.
27. Epithets of the Buddha are capitalised when they stand for the Buddha: Blessed One (བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས), Victor (རྒྱལ་བ, རྒྱལ), Guide (འདྲེན་པ, རྣམ་འདྲེན, རྣམ་པར་འདྲེན་པ, ཡོངས་འདྲེན), Teacher (སྟོན་པ), Protector (མགོན), Knower of the World (འཇིག་རྟེན་མཁྱེན་པ); in a generic plural sense ("teachers", "protectors" of others) keep lower case.
28. Sugata and Tathāgata: བདེ་གཤེགས, བདེ་བར་གཤེགས, བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་པ are "Sugata" and དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས, དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ are "Tathāgata" in the academic register (the register names both); children's use "Well-Gone One" and "Thus-Gone One" (new), keeping the two names distinct.
29. Buddha is always capitalised ("Buddha", "Buddhas", "a Buddha", "Buddha-field"), also in the generic sense, because the children's register fixes "Buddha" and the machines mostly capitalise; སངས་རྒྱས་ཞིང is "Buddha-field" (hyphenated), while ཞིང alone is "field".
30. Proper names take the standard Sanskrit form: academic with IAST (Subhūti, Śāriputra, Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, Śakra, Brahmā, Māra, Jambudvīpa, Anavatapta, Vidyākarasiṃha, Rāhu) and children's without diacritics (Subhuti, Shariputra, Maitreya, Manjushri, Shakra, Brahma, Mara, Jambudvipa, Anavatapta, Vidyakarasimha); Śāriputra and Maitreya do not occur in the overview but follow this rule if they appear.
31. Indra is not used: བརྒྱ་བྱིན is "Śakra" (3 of 4 attestations), and ལྷ་རྒྱལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན is "Śakra, king of the gods"; Mount Meru, not Sumeru (རི་རབ, རེ་རབ, 5 of 6).
32. The translator Paltsek (དཔལ་བརྩེགས) is written "Paltsek" in both registers (Gemini; DharmaMitra "Paltshek" is a Wylie-like spelling).
33. Kept Sanskrit in the academic register (IAST, no italics, lower case unless a name): bodhisattva, Sugata, Tathāgata, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa, parinirvāṇa, arhat, śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, samādhi, dharmadhātu, Dharmakāya, stūpa, yojana, nāga, mantra, yogin, śramaṇa, brahmin and the proper names above; every other term is translated.
34. Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa: འཁོར་བ and འཁོར are "saṃsāra" and མྱ་ངན་འདས, མྱ་ངན་འདའ and མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ are "nirvāṇa" (with ṇ, never "nirvana"), while ཡོངས་སུ་མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ is "parinirvāṇa"; "cyclic existence" is not used.
35. Diacritics: IAST in the academic register (ā ī ū ṛ ṃ ṅ ñ ṇ ś ṣ), never in children's; the machines drop them inconsistently (Mara 2, nirvana 7, samadhi, Sangha), and these are all restored; the one exception is "Sangha", written without ṅ in both registers since it is normal English (children's: "the community").
36. Capitalisation: all headwords are lower case singular unless they are names, titles of the work, epithets, "Buddha", "Dharma" (teaching) or "Mother"; sentence-initial capitals and plurals are the translator's job, not recorded in the termbase.
37. Compounds take their parts' locked renderings: སེམས་ཅན་འདུ་ཤེས is "perception of sentient beings", ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན "perfection of ethical discipline", བྱང་ཆུབ་ཡོན་ཏན "qualities of awakening", ཐབས་མཁས "skilled in means"; the only exceptions are ཤེས་རབ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན (children's "perfect wisdom") and lexicalised compounds listed in this sheet.
38. Spelling variants and truncations of one Tibetan word (ཤེས་རབ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན/པ, བཟོད/བཟོད་པ, དགེ་བའི་བཤེས/དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན, ཚོར/ཚོར་བ, ཡི་རང/རྗེས་སུ་ཡི་རང, གཟུགས variants, བྱང་ཆུང་ for བྱང་ཆུབ་, མཚན་མ་མེད/མཚན་མེད) share one rendering; the overview's garbled entries (གངགā, གང་གā, བཻḍūརྱ) are read as Ganges and lapis lazuli.
39. Beings: སེམས་ཅན is "sentient beings" and འགྲོ་བ, འགྲོ are "beings" (academic); children's སེམས་ཅན is "living beings" (new for this term, DharmaMitra attests it for འགྲོ་བ) and འགྲོ་བ is "beings"; the two never swap, because "sentient beings" outnumbers "beings" 6 to 1 for སེམས་ཅན.
40. བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས is "mind of awakening" (Gemini 5; DharmaMitra "mind of enlightenment") and སེམས་བསྐྱེད, ཐུགས་བསྐྱེད are "generation of the mind (of awakening)", with the verb "generate the mind"; the four entries where machines rendered བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ as "bodhisattva" (T034) were checked against every occurrence: at 1-10, 1-18 and 5-40 བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས is the metrical short form of བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ ("bodhisattva", its own sense in T034); elsewhere it is "mind of awakening".
41. ཁམས has two senses and is kept as two terms: "realm" in the cosmological compounds (འདོད་པའི་ཁམས "desire realm", ཁམས་གསུམ "three realms", སེམས་ཅན་ཁམས "realm of sentient beings", འདུས་མ་བྱས་ཁམས "unconditioned realm") and "element" for the constituent (ནམ་མཁའི་ཁམས "element of space"); འཇིག་རྟེན་ཁམས is "world system" (both machines).
42. ས is "stage" (bhūmi) in the academic register, not "level" or "ground" (DharmaMitra's "stage" 3, Gemini's "level" 4); the compounds དགྲ་བཅོམ་ས, རང་རྒྱལ་ས, རྐྱེན་རྟོགས་ས and སངས་རྒྱས་ས follow it ("stage of an arhat", "stage of a Buddha"); children's use "level".
43. Prophecy: ལུང་བསྟན is "prophecy"/"prophesy" (the machines agree); མི་ལྡོག་ལུང་བསྟན is "prophecy of irreversibility" (new, so that it follows policy 44 rather than either machine's "non-retrogression" or "irreversible and prophesied"); ལུང་མ་བསྟན "unprophesied".
44. Irreversibility: མི་ལྡོག, ཕྱིར་མི་ལྡོག་པ are "irreversible" (Gemini 5, DharmaMitra 2 plus T271 and T418), not "non-retrogression", so མི་ལྡོག་བྱང་ཆུབ is "irreversible awakening".
45. Powers: སྟོབས "power" and སྟོབས་བཅུ "ten powers", དབང་པོ "faculty", རྫུ་འཕྲུལ "miraculous power", མངོན་པར་ཤེས "superknowledge"; these four are kept distinct by their head word.
46. Qualities versus virtue: ཡོན་ཏན 21 and 23 times is "quality" and དགེ་བ is "virtue" (དགེ་བའི་རྩ་བ "roots of virtue"); དཀར་པོའི་ཡོན་ཏན, དཀར་པོའི་ཆོས and དགེ་ཆོས are "virtuous qualities" (DharmaMitra), so Gemini's "white" and "dharmas" are not used for them.
47. Dedication and rejoicing: བསྔོ, ཡོངས་བསྔོ, ཡོངས་སུ་བསྔོ, ཡོངས་སུ་བསྔོ་བ are "dedicate"/"dedication" (the "fully" and "perfectly" variants are not locked in); རྗེས་སུ་ཡི་རང and ཡི་རང are "rejoice"/"rejoicing".
48. Verbal terms (བསྔོ, ཡི་རང, དམིགས, རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག) are recorded as a base form, "dedicate", "rejoice", "apprehend", "non-conceptual", and the translator inflects them in context, as the registers require.
49. Rebirth destinies are plain nouns: ངན་སོང་གསུམ "three lower realms", ངན་སོང "lower realms", སེམས་དམྱལ "hell", ཡི་དགས "hungry ghost", དུད་འགྲོ "animal", ལྷ "god", མི "human"; མི་ཁོམ་བརྒྱད is "eight unfree states" and དལ་བ "leisure" (both machines agree).
50. Conceit versus pride: རློམ་སེམས is "conceit" and ང་རྒྱལ is "pride" (both machines agree), ལྷག་པའི་ང་རྒྱལ is "superior pride", and མཆོག་འཛིན is "clinging to the supreme"; the two stems are never swapped.
51. Wisdom words: ཤེས་རབ "wisdom" (perfection and faculty), ཡེ་ཤེས "primordial wisdom", ཤེས་པ "knowledge", མངོན་པར་ཤེས "superknowledge"; do not use "gnosis" or "pristine" (each attested once or only by Gemini).
52. American spelling and hyphenation: "skillful", "armor", "eons" (not "aeons"), "Buddha-field", "non-arising", "non-conceptual", "non-apprehension", "buddha-" prefixes only in set compounds; the machines both use American forms.
53. Children's register keeps the verse sense of every term: a plain phrase replaces a technical word, never a different idea, and where the plain phrase might mislead (suchness, emptiness, non-apprehension) the first use in a retelling may carry a short gloss in the story text, not in the termbase.
